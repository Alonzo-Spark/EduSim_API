### Pressure in Fluids

#### Quick Definition  
**Pressure** is defined as the **force applied perpendicularly per unit area** on a surface.  
**SI Unit:** Pascal (Pa) = 1 Newton per square meter (N/m²).

---

#### Formula  
The mathematical expression for pressure is:  
$$ P = \frac{F_{\perp}}{A} $$  

| Symbol | Quantity              | SI Unit          |
|--------|-----------------------|------------------|
| \( P \) | Pressure             | Pascal (Pa)      |
| \( F_{\perp} \) | Perpendicular force | Newton (N)       |
| \( A \)  | Surface area         | Square meter (m²) |

**Key Insight:**  
- Pressure increases if force rises or area decreases (e.g., knife edges, high heels).  
- Pressure decreases if force decreases or area increases (e.g., snowshoes, tank treads).  

---

#### Characteristics of Pressure in Fluids  
1. **Scalar quantity** (has magnitude, no direction).  
2. **Transmitted equally** in all directions (Pascal’s principle).  
3. **Depth-dependent** in fluids: \( P = \rho gh \) (where \( \rho \) = density, \( g \) = gravity, \( h \) = depth).  

---

#### Practical Applications  
- **Hydraulic systems**: Use fluid pressure to multiply force (e.g., car brakes, lifts).  
- **Weather forecasting**: Atmospheric pressure changes predict storms.  
- **Medical devices**: Blood pressure monitors.  

---

#### Common Misconceptions  
❌ *"Pressure and force are interchangeable."*  
✅ **Correction**: Force is a vector acting on a surface; pressure is the scalar *effect* of that force per unit area.
