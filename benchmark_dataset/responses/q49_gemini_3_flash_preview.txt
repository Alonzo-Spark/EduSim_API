:::section{title="Introduction"}
In this scenario, we are examining the relationship between electrical current, resistance, and the resulting potential difference (voltage) across a conductor. This relationship is governed by one of the most fundamental laws in physics: **Ohm's Law**.
:::

:::section{title="Key Concepts"}
*   **Current ($I$):** The rate of flow of electric charge, measured in Amperes (A).
*   **Resistance ($R$):** The opposition to the flow of electric current, measured in Ohms ($\Omega$).
*   **Voltage ($V$):** The electrical potential difference between two points, measured in Volts (V).
:::

:::section{title="Formula Card"}
### Ohm's Law Calculation

$$
V = I \times R
$$

**Variables:**
*   $V$ = Voltage (Volts)
*   $I$ = Current (Amperes)
*   $R$ = Resistance (Ohms)

[ Explain Formula: The voltage across a resistor is directly proportional to the current flowing through it. ]
[ Open Formula Lab ]
:::

:::section{title="Step-by-Step Solution"}
To find the voltage, we substitute the given values into the formula:

1.  **Identify Given Values:**
    *   Current ($I$) = $2\text{ A}$
    *   Resistance ($R$) = $10\text{ }\Omega$

2.  **Apply Formula:**
    *   $V = 2\text{ A} \times 10\text{ }\Omega$

3.  **Result:**
    *   $V = 20\text{ V}$
:::

:::section{title="Important Notes"}
*   **Direct Proportionality:** If you double the current while keeping resistance the same, the voltage will also double.
*   **Standard Units:** Always ensure current is in Amperes and resistance is in Ohms before calculating to get the result in Volts.
:::
