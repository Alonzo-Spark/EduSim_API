To derive the expression for acceleration due to gravity, we start with Newton's law of universal gravitation and some fundamental principles of physics.

┌───────────────────────────┐
│ Introduction              │
│                           │
│ The acceleration due to gravity is a crucial concept in physics,     │
│ representing the acceleration that an object experiences due to the   │
│ gravitational pull of the Earth (or another celestial body).          │
└───────────────────────────┘

┌───────────────────────────┐
│ Key Concepts              │
│                           │
│ 1. **Gravitational Force**: The force of attraction between two masses.│
│ 2. **Newton's Law of Gravitation**: \( F = \frac{G \cdot m_1 \cdot m_2}{r^2} \)  │
│ 3. **Acceleration**: Defined as the rate of change of velocity.       │
└───────────────────────────┘

┌───────────────────────────┐
│ Derivation Steps          │
│                           │
│ 1. **Identify the Forces**: For an object of mass \( m \) near the Earth's surface, the gravitational force acting on it is given by the formula:│
│                             │
│    $$ F = \frac{G \cdot M \cdot m}{r^2} $$                             │
│                             │
│    where:                                                         │
│    - \( F \) is the gravitational force,                             │
│    - \( G \) is the gravitational constant,                          │
│    - \( M \) is the mass of the Earth, and                          │
│    - \( r \) is the distance from the center of the Earth to the object.│
│                           │
│ 2. **Set the Forces Equal**: The gravitational force also equals the weight of the object:│
│                             │
│    $$ F = m \cdot g $$                                               │
│                             │
│    where \( g \) is the acceleration due to gravity.               │
│                           │
│ 3. **Combine the Equations**: Setting the two expressions for \( F \) equal gives:│
│                             │
│    $$ m \cdot g = \frac{G \cdot M \cdot m}{r^2} $$                  │
│                           │
│ 4. **Solve for \( g \)**: Dividing both sides by \( m \) (assuming \( m \neq 0 \)) results in:│
│                             │
│    $$ g = \frac{G \cdot M}{r^2} $$                                   │
└───────────────────────────┘

┌───────────────────────────┐
│ Main Formula              │
│                           │
│     Rendered Formula      │
│                           │
│    $$ g = \frac{G \cdot M}{r^2} $$                                   │
│                           │

└───────────────────────────┘

┌───────────────────────────┐
│ Important Notes           │
│                           │
│ - \( g \) varies slightly over the Earth's surface due to factors like altitude and latitude.│
│ - Standard value of \( g \) at sea level is approximately \( 9.81 \, \text{m/s}^2 \).│
└───────────────────────────┘

┌───────────────────────────┐
│ Summary                   │
│                           │
│ The acceleration due to gravity, derived from Newton's law of gravitation, is expressed as:│
│                             │
│    $$ g = \frac{G \cdot M}{r^2} $$                                   │
│                           │
│ This formula demonstrates how gravitational acceleration is dependent on the mass of the Earth and the distance from its center.│
└───────────────────────────┘
