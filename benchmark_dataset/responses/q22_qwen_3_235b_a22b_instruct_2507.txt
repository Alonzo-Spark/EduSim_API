│ **Quick Definition**      │  
│                           │  
│ Acceleration due to gravity (\( g \)) is the acceleration experienced by an object due to the gravitational force exerted by a massive body, such as Earth. It is independent of the object’s mass and depends only on the mass and radius of the celestial body. │  
└───────────────────────────┘  

---

### **Introduction**

All objects near the surface of a planet (like Earth) experience a constant acceleration when falling freely under gravity, assuming no air resistance. This acceleration is known as the **acceleration due to gravity**, denoted by \( g \). Its value on Earth is approximately \( 9.8 \, \text{m/s}^2 \), but it can be derived from fundamental physical laws.

We derive this expression using **Newton’s Law of Universal Gravitation** and **Newton’s Second Law of Motion**.

---

### **Step-by-Step Derivation**

#### **Step 1: Newton’s Law of Universal Gravitation**

The gravitational force \( F \) between two masses is given by:

$$
F = \frac{G M m}{R^2}
$$

Where:  
- \( G \) = Universal gravitational constant (\(6.674 \times 10^{-11} \, \text{N·m}^2/\text{kg}^2\))  
- \( M \) = Mass of the Earth (or planet)  
- \( m \) = Mass of the object near the surface  
- \( R \) = Radius of the Earth (distance from center to surface)

---

#### **Step 2: Newton’s Second Law of Motion**

When this gravitational force acts on mass \( m \), it produces an acceleration \( g \):

$$
F = m g
$$

---

#### **Step 3: Equating the Two Expressions for Force**

Since both expressions represent the same force (gravitational pull), equate them:

$$
m g = \frac{G M m}{R^2}
$$

---

#### **Step 4: Cancel the mass \( m \)**

The mass of the object cancels out on both sides:

$$
g = \frac{G M}{R^2}
$$

### **Explanation of the Formula**

- \( g \): Acceleration due to gravity (in \( \text{m/s}^2 \))  
- \( G \): Universal gravitational constant  
- \( M \): Mass of the Earth (\( \approx 5.972 \times 10^{24} \, \text{kg} \))  
- \( R \): Radius of the Earth (\( \approx 6.371 \times 10^6 \, \text{m} \))

This shows that \( g \) depends only on:
- The mass of the planet,
- The radius of the planet,
- The universal gravitational constant.

It **does not depend** on the mass of the falling object — which is why all objects fall at the same rate in a vacuum.

---

### **Key Concepts**

- Gravity is a universal force described by Newton’s inverse-square law.
- The derived expression applies to any celestial body — just substitute its mass and radius.
- The cancellation of the object’s mass explains the equivalence principle (a foundation for general relativity).

---

### **Example Calculation (Earth's Surface)**

$$
g = \frac{(6.674 \times 10^{-11}) (5.972 \times 10^{24})}{(6.371 \times 10^6)^2}
$$

$$
g \approx \frac{3.986 \times 10^{14}}{4.058 \times 10^{13}} \approx 9.82 \, \text{m/s}^2
$$

Matches observed value closely.

---

### **Important Notes**

- This formula assumes:
  - The object is at or near the surface.
  - The Earth is a perfect sphere with uniform density (approximation).
  - No rotational effects (centrifugal force is ignored).

- At higher altitudes: Replace \( R \) with \( R + h \), where \( h \) is height above surface.

---

### **Summary**

The acceleration due to gravity is derived from equating gravitational force and inertial force:

$$
g = \frac{G M}{R^2}
$$

This fundamental expression allows us to calculate \( g \) for any planet or moon given its mass and radius.
