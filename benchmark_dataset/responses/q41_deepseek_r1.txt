### Coulomb's Law: Fundamental Electrostatic Principle

#### ⚡ Quick Definition  
Coulomb's Law quantitatively describes the **electrostatic force** between two stationary point charges. This force is:
- Proportional to the product of the charge magnitudes  
- Inversely proportional to the square of the distance between them  
- Directed along the line connecting the charges  

---

#### 🔬 Main Formula
```math
\boxed{F = \frac{1}{4\pi\varepsilon_0} \frac{|q_1 q_2|}{r^2}}
```

#### 📖 Detailed Explanation
**Force Direction:**  
- **Like charges** (+,+ or −,−) → **Repulsive force**  
- **Opposite charges** (+,−) → **Attractive force**  

**Key Variables:**  
| Symbol | Quantity | SI Unit | Physical Meaning |
|--------|----------|---------|------------------|
| \( F \) | Force | Newton (N) | Electrostatic force magnitude |
| \( q_1, q_2 \) | Charges | Coulomb (C) | Magnitude of point charges |
| \( r \) | Distance | Meter (m) | Separation between charges |
| \( \varepsilon_0 \) | Vacuum permittivity | 8.85×10⁻¹² C²/N·m² | Electric constant |
| \( k_e \) | Coulomb's constant | 8.99×10⁹ N·m²/C² | \( \frac{1}{4\pi\varepsilon_0} \) |

**Mathematical Properties:**  
- **Inverse-square law**: Force decreases with \( \frac{1}{r^2} \)  
- **Superposition principle**: For multiple charges, net force is vector sum of individual forces  

---

#### 🌍 Real-World Applications
1. **Atomic physics**: Electron-nucleus attraction in atoms  
2. **Capacitor design**: Charge storage in electronic components  
3. **Electrostatic precipitators**: Pollution control in smokestacks  
4. **Inkjet printing**: Precise droplet control using charged plates  

---

#### ⚖️ Limitations
- Applies **only to point charges** (finite-size objects require integration)  
- Valid for **stationary charges** (moving charges require magnetism corrections)  
- Assumes **uniform dielectric medium** (vacuum or air approximation)

#### 📝 Key Takeaways  
1. Electrostatic force follows inverse-square law  
2. Coulomb's constant \( k_e = 8.99 \times 10^9 \) N·m²/C²  
3. Direction determined by charge signs  
4. Forms foundation for classical electromagnetism  

> "Coulomb's law is the electric counterpart to Newton's law of universal gravitation."  
> *— Richard Feynman, The Feynman Lectures on Physics*
