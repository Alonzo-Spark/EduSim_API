**Markdown Input (Example):**

```markdown
# Inertia and Types of Motion

## Introduction

Inertia is a fundamental concept in physics describing an object's resistance to changes in its state of motion.  Objects at rest tend to stay at rest, and objects in motion tend to stay in motion with the same speed and in the same direction unless acted upon by an external force.

## Definition

In simpler terms, inertia is the tendency of an object to resist changes in its velocity.  The greater the mass of an object, the greater its inertia.

## Key Concepts

* **Mass:** A measure of an object's resistance to acceleration.
* **Newton's First Law of Motion:** An object at rest stays at rest and an object in motion stays in motion with the same speed and in the same direction unless acted upon by a force.

## Formula

$$F = ma$$

## Types of Inertia

There are different types of inertia depending on the context.  These include:

* **Linear Inertia:** Resistance to changes in linear motion (e.g., acceleration).
* **Rotational Inertia:** Resistance to changes in rotational motion (e.g., angular acceleration).

## Table

| Property | Description |
|---|---|
| Mass | A measure of inertia. |
| Velocity | Rate of change of position. |

## Applications

Understanding inertia is crucial in many fields, including engineering, transportation, and sports.  For example, seatbelts utilize inertia to prevent injuries during sudden stops.

## Advantages

* Provides a foundational understanding of motion.
* Allows for prediction of object behavior under force.

## Disadvantages

* Can be complex to apply in situations with multiple forces.
* Requires careful consideration of friction.

## Important Notes

Inertia is a vector quantity, meaning it has both magnitude and direction.

## Summary

Inertia is a key concept in understanding how objects behave under the influence of forces.  It is directly related to mass and is essential for understanding motion.

## Suggested Questions

1. What is the relationship between mass and inertia?
2. How does inertia relate to Newton's First Law?
```
