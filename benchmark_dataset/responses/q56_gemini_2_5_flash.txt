## Understanding Collision Bounce and Restitution

Collision bounce, in the context of rigid bodies, refers to how much kinetic energy is conserved or dissipated during an impact. This behavior is primarily governed by a material property called the **coefficient of restitution (COR)**.

### Quick Definition

The **coefficient of restitution (COR)** is a dimensionless quantity that quantifies the "bounciness" of a collision. It is a measure of the ratio of the relative speed of separation after impact to the relative speed of approach before impact.

### Main Formula

The coefficient of restitution ($e$) for a one-dimensional collision between two bodies is given by:

$$
e = \frac{v'_2 - v'_1}{v_1 - v_2}
$$

Where:
*   $v_1$ and $v_2$ are the velocities of body 1 and body 2 **before** the collision, respectively.
*   $v'_1$ and $v'_2$ are the velocities of body 1 and body 2 **after** the collision, respectively.

### Key Concepts

*   **Relative Velocity of Approach:** This is the speed at which the two bodies are closing in on each other before the collision ($v_1 - v_2$).
*   **Relative Velocity of Separation:** This is the speed at which the two bodies are moving apart after the collision ($v'_2 - v'_1$).
*   **Kinetic Energy:** The energy an object possesses due to its motion.
*   **Momentum:** The product of an object's mass and velocity. In a closed system, total momentum is always conserved during a collision.
