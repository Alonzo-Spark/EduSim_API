## ┌─ Quick Definition ──────────────────────────────┐

**Inertia** is the natural tendency of an object to **resist any change in its state of rest or uniform motion** unless acted upon by an external force.

> It is not a force — it is a **property of matter**.

## └─────────────────────────────────────────────────┘

---

## ┌─ Foundation Principle ──────────────────────────┐

### Newton's First Law of Motion

$$\sum \vec{F} = 0 \implies \vec{v} = \text{constant}$$

| Symbol | Meaning |
|--------|---------|
| $\sum \vec{F}$ | Net external force on the object |
| $\vec{v}$ | Velocity of the object |

> If net force is **zero**, velocity stays **unchanged** — this is inertia in action.

## └─────────────────────────────────────────────────┘

---

## ┌─ Inertia & Mass ────────────────────────────────┐

$$\text{Inertia} \propto m$$

- A **heavier object** has **more inertia** → harder to move or stop
- A **lighter object** has **less inertia** → easier to accelerate
- Mass is the **quantitative measure** of inertia

## ┌─ Types of Inertia ──────────────────────────────┐

Inertia has **three distinct types**, each describing resistance to a different kind of change:

---

### 🔵 Type 1 — Inertia of Rest

> **Resistance of an object to start moving from rest**

An object **at rest** tends to **stay at rest** unless an external force acts on it.

**Examples:**
- A passenger **jerks backward** when a bus suddenly starts — the body resists the forward motion
- A coin on a card falls into a glass when the card is flicked — the coin resists horizontal motion
- Dust falls off a carpet when beaten — dust particles resist movement

---

### 🟠 Type 2 — Inertia of Motion

> **Resistance of an object to stop or change its speed while moving**

An object **in motion** tends to **continue moving** at the same speed in the same direction.

**Examples:**
- A passenger **lurches forward** when a moving bus brakes suddenly
- An athlete **runs past the finish line** — cannot stop instantly
- Clothes in a spin dryer — water flies off because clothes resist stopping

---

### 🟢 Type 3 — Inertia of Direction

> **Resistance of an object to change its direction of motion**

A moving object tends to **continue in a straight line** and resists any change in direction.

**Examples:**
- Mud flies **tangentially off** a spinning wheel
- A passenger **slides sideways** when a car takes a sharp turn
- Water flies off an umbrella when spun — it resists the circular path

## ┌─ Comparison Table ──────────────────────────────┐

| Type | What is Resisted | State of Object | Classic Example |
|------|-----------------|-----------------|-----------------|
| **Inertia of Rest** | Starting motion | At rest | Coin-on-card trick |
| **Inertia of Motion** | Stopping / changing speed | Moving | Passenger lurching forward |
| **Inertia of Direction** | Changing direction | Moving | Mud flying off a wheel |

## ┌─ Key Characteristics ───────────────────────────┐

- ✅ Every object with **mass** possesses inertia
- ✅ Inertia has **no unit** of its own — measured via mass (kg)
- ✅ Greater mass → Greater inertia
- ✅ Inertia is an **intrinsic property** — it cannot be removed
- ❌ Inertia is **not a force**
- ❌ Inertia does **not depend** on velocity or position
