The problem asks us to find the acceleration of a body given its mass and the force acting on it. This is a classic application of Newton's Second Law of Motion.

### Quick Definition

Newton's Second Law of Motion states that the acceleration of an object is directly proportional to the net force acting on it and inversely proportional to its mass. The direction of the acceleration is in the direction of the net force.

### Main Formula

The relationship between force, mass, and acceleration is given by:

$$ F = ma $$

Where:
*   $F$ is the net force acting on the object (measured in Newtons, N)
*   $m$ is the mass of the object (measured in kilograms, kg)
*   $a$ is the acceleration of the object (measured in meters per second squared, m/s²)

### Given Values

Let's identify the information provided in the problem:
*   Force ($F$) = 50 N
*   Mass ($m$) = 10 kg

### Step-by-Step Derivations

1.  **Recall Newton's Second Law:**
    $$ F = ma $$

2.  **Rearrange the formula to solve for acceleration ($a$):**
    To find acceleration, we need to isolate $a$. We can do this by dividing both sides of the equation by mass ($m$):
    $$ a = \frac{F}{m} $$

3.  **Substitute the given values into the rearranged formula:**
    $$ a = \frac{50 \text{ N}}{10 \text{ kg}} $$

4.  **Calculate the acceleration:**
    $$ a = 5 \text{ m/s}^2 $$
    (Note: 1 Newton is defined as 1 kg·m/s², so N/kg simplifies to m/s²)

### Solution

The acceleration of the body is $5 \text{ m/s}^2$.

### Key Concepts Illustrated

*   **Newton's Second Law:** This problem directly applies $F=ma$.
*   **Direct Proportionality:** If the force increases (with constant mass), the acceleration increases.
*   **Inverse Proportionality:** If the mass increases (with constant force), the acceleration decreases.
*   **Units:** Understanding how units cancel out (N/kg = m/s²) is crucial for verifying the correctness of the answer.

### Summary

We used Newton's Second Law of Motion, $F=ma$, to calculate the acceleration of a body. By rearranging the formula to $a = F/m$ and substituting the given force of 50 N and mass of 10 kg, we found the acceleration to be $5 \text{ m/s}^2$. This demonstrates a fundamental principle in classical mechanics.
