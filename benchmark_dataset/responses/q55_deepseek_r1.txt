### Behavior of a Sliding Block as Friction Coefficient Approaches Zero  

#### Quick Definition  
As the coefficient of kinetic friction (\(\mu_k\)) decreases toward zero, a sliding block transitions from decelerating due to friction to moving at a **constant velocity** (Newton’s first law). In the ideal case (\(\mu_k = 0\)), the block experiences no opposing force and maintains its initial velocity indefinitely.  

---

#### Key Physics Concepts  
1. **Friction Force**:  
   \( F_{\text{friction}} = \mu_k \cdot N \)  
   Where \(N = mg\) (normal force for a horizontal surface).  
2. **Deceleration**:  
   \( a = -\mu_k \cdot g \)  
   (Negative acceleration due to friction).  
3. **Velocity**:  
   \( v(t) = v_0 + a \cdot t \)  
   (Without friction, \(a = 0\), so \(v(t) = v_0\)).  

---

#### Effect of Reducing Friction  
| Friction Coefficient (\(\mu_k\)) | Block Behavior                     |  
|----------------------------------|-----------------------------------|  
| \(\mu_k > 0\)                    | Slows down linearly until stop.   |  
| \(\mu_k \to 0\)                  | Velocity decreases imperceptibly. |  
| \(\mu_k = 0\)                    | **Moves at constant velocity \(v_0\) forever**. |  

---

#### Formula Breakdown  
**Friction Force & Motion**  
$$  
\boxed{  
\begin{array}{c}  
F_{\text{net}} = m a = -F_{\text{friction}} \\  
\downarrow \\  
m a = -\mu_k m g \\  
\downarrow \\  
a = -\mu_k g \\  
\end{array}  
}  
$$  
**As \(\mu_k \to 0\)**:  
- Acceleration \(a \to 0\).  
- Velocity change \(\Delta v \to 0\).  

---

#### Real-World Insight  
- **Ideal Case (\(\mu_k = 0\))**: Only possible in vacuum/space (no external forces).  
- **Practical Case**: Near-zero friction (e.g., ice or air bearings) mimics constant velocity but requires energy to *maintain* motion due to tiny residual forces.  

---

#### Critical Implications  
1. **Newton’s First Law**: Dominates when \(\mu_k = 0\) → constant velocity unless acted upon.  
2. **Energy Conservation**: Kinetic energy \(\left(\frac{1}{2}mv^2\right)\) remains constant without friction.  
3. **Indefinite Motion**: No work is done against friction → no energy loss.  

---

#### Summary  
- **\(\mu_k \to 0\)**: Block’s deceleration \(\to 0\).  
- **\(\mu_k = 0\)**: Block moves at **unchanging velocity** indefinitely.  
- Friction is necessary for stopping; its absence leads to idealized inertial motion.  

> **Suggested Experiment**: Simulate \(\mu_k = 0\) using an air hockey table. The puck glides ≈ constant velocity until collisions or air resistance intervene.
