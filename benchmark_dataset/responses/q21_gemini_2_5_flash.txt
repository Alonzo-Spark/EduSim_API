## Gravitation

This section explores Newton's Law of Gravitation, a fundamental principle governing the attraction between objects with mass.

***

### Quick Definition

Newton's Law of Gravitation describes the **attractive force** that exists between any two objects possessing mass. It's a universal law, meaning it applies throughout the cosmos.

***

### Main Formula

$$ F = G \frac{m_1 m_2}{r^2} $$

***

### Key Concepts

*   **Gravitational Force ($F$):** The attractive force between two objects. It's always a pull, never a push.
*   **Gravitational Constant ($G$):** A universal constant that quantifies the strength of the gravitational force. Its approximate value is $6.674 \times 10^{-11} \text{ N} \cdot \text{m}^2/\text{kg}^2$.
*   **Masses ($m_1, m_2$):** The quantities of matter in the two interacting objects. The force is directly proportional to the product of their masses.
*   **Distance ($r$):** The distance between the centers of the two objects. The force is inversely proportional to the square of this distance.

***

### Characteristics of Gravitational Force

| Characteristic            | Description                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                     |
| :------------------------ | :---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Always Attractive**     | Gravitational force only pulls objects together; it never pushes them apart.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                    |
| **Long-Range Force**      | The force diminishes with distance but theoretically extends infinitely. This is why galaxies can influence each other even across vast cosmic distances.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                          |
| **Weakest Fundamental Force** | Compared to the strong nuclear force, electromagnetic force, and weak nuclear force, gravity is by far the weakest. Its effects are only noticeable when at least one of the interacting objects has a very large mass (like a planet or star).                                                                                                                                                    |
| **Acts Between Centers**  | For spherical objects of uniform density (or objects that are far apart), the distance 'r' is measured from the center of mass of one object to the center of mass of the other.                                                                                                                                                                                                                 |
| **Independent of Medium** | The gravitational force between two objects is not affected by the presence of other objects or the medium between them. For example, the gravitational force between the Earth and the Moon is the same whether there's a vacuum or air between them.                                                                                                                                               |
| **Action-Reaction Pair**  | As per Newton's Third Law, if object 1 exerts a gravitational force on object 2, then object 2 exerts an equal and opposite gravitational force on object 1. For example, the Earth pulls on you with the same force that you pull on the Earth.                                                                                                                                                       |

***

### Important Notes

*   **Inverse Square Law:** The "inverse square" relationship with distance ($1/r^2$) is crucial. It means that if you double the distance between two objects, the gravitational force between them decreases by a factor of four ($2^2$).
*   **Applicability:** While often demonstrated with celestial bodies, Newton's Law of Gravitation applies to *all* objects with mass, no matter how small. However, the force is only significant for objects with substantial mass.
*   **Limitations:** Newton's law is an excellent approximation for most everyday and astronomical phenomena. However, it breaks down under extreme conditions, such as very strong gravitational fields (near black holes) or at very high speeds, where Albert Einstein's theory of General Relativity provides a more accurate description.

***

### Summary

Newton's Law of Gravitation provides a powerful framework for understanding the fundamental attractive force between any two masses. It's characterized by its inverse-square dependence on distance and direct dependence on the product of the masses, mediated by the universal gravitational constant.
