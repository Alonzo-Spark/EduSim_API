To derive the expression for the maximum height (\(h_{\text{max}}\)) of a projectile, we analyze its vertical motion under constant gravitational acceleration. Here's the step-by-step derivation:

### Key Assumptions
- Projectile launched with **initial velocity** \(v_0\) at angle \(\theta\) to the horizontal.
- **Acceleration due to gravity** (\(g\)) acts downward.
- Air resistance is negligible.

---

### Step-by-Step Derivation

1. **Initial Velocity Components**:
   - Vertical component: \(v_{0y} = v_0 \sin \theta\)
   - Horizontal component: \(v_{0x} = v_0 \cos \theta\)

2. **Condition for Maximum Height**:
   - At maximum height, the **vertical velocity** (\(v_y\)) becomes **zero**.
   - This occurs when the projectile stops rising and begins to fall.

3. **Kinematic Equation for Vertical Motion**:
   - Use the equation: \(v_y^2 = v_{0y}^2 + 2a_y \Delta y\)
   - Here:
     - \(v_y = 0\) (at max height),
     - \(v_{0y} = v_0 \sin \theta\),
     - \(a_y = -g\) (acceleration opposes upward motion),
     - \(\Delta y = h_{\text{max}}\) (vertical displacement).

4. **Substitute Values**:
   \[
   0 = (v_0 \sin \theta)^2 + 2(-g)h_{\text{max}}
   \]
   \[
   0 = v_0^2 \sin^2 \theta - 2g h_{\text{max}}
   \]

5. **Solve for \(h_{\text{max}}\)**:
   \[
   2g h_{\text{max}} = v_0^2 \sin^2 \theta
   \]
   \[
   \boxed{h_{\text{max}} = \frac{v_0^2 \sin^2 \theta}{2g}}
   \]

---

### Key Insight
- **Dependence on \(\theta\)**: Maximum height increases with \(\sin \theta\).  
  - **Maximum value** when \(\theta = 90^\circ\) (vertical projection):  
  \[
  h_{\text{max}} = \frac{v_0^2}{2g}
  \]
  - **Zero height** when \(\theta = 0^\circ\) (horizontal projection).

---

### Summary
| Parameter | Formula |
|-----------|---------|
| **Maximum Height** | \(h_{\text{max}} = \dfrac{v_0^2 \sin^2 \theta}{2g}\) |

### Practical Applications
1. Calculating launch angles for reaching specific heights (e.g., sports, artillery).
2. Designing ramps or trajectories in engineering projects.
3. Modeling atmospheric particle motion.
