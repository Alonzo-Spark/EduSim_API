### Simple Harmonic Motion (SHM): Quick Definition  
Simple harmonic motion (SHM) is a **periodic oscillatory motion** where an object moves back and forth around an equilibrium position under a **restoring force directly proportional to its displacement** and directed toward the equilibrium point.  

---

### Key Characteristics of SHM  
1. **Restoring Force**:  
   \( F = -kx \)  
   - \( F \): Restoring force (N)  
   - \( k \): Spring constant (N/m)  
   - \( x \): Displacement from equilibrium (m)  

2. **Motion Properties**:  
   - **Sinusoidal**: Position, velocity, and acceleration follow sine/cosine functions.  
   - **Amplitude (A)**: Maximum displacement from equilibrium.  
   - **Period (T)**: Time for one full cycle (s). Independent of amplitude.  
   - **Frequency (f)**: Number of cycles per second (Hz); \( f = \frac{1}{T} \).  

3. **Phase Constant**:  
   Determines initial position/velocity at \( t = 0 \).  

---

### Formula Card: Displacement in SHM  
$$ x(t) = A \cos(\omega t + \phi) $$  
**Where**:  
- \( x(t) \): Displacement at time \( t \)  
- \( A \): Amplitude (max displacement)  
- \( \omega \): Angular frequency (\( \omega = 2\pi f = \sqrt{\frac{k}{m}} \))  
- \( \phi \): Phase constant (radians)  

**Explanation**:  
- **Acceleration**: \( a = -\omega^2 x \)  
- **Velocity**: \( v = -\omega A \sin(\omega t + \phi) \)  
- **Energy**: Kinetic and potential energy interchange; total energy \( E = \frac{1}{2} k A^2 \).  

---

### Real-World Examples  
- **Mass-Spring System**: Object attached to a spring.  
- **Pendulum**: Small-angle approximation (\( \theta < 10^\circ \)).  
- **Atoms in Crystals**: Vibrations in lattice structures.  
- **AC Circuits**: Charge/current oscillations.  

---

### Why SHM Matters  
- **Foundation for Waves**: SHM is the building block for understanding wave propagation.  
- **Universal Model**: Applies to mechanics, electromagnetism, and quantum systems.  
- **Idealization**: Frictionless/damped extensions refine real-world predictions.  

> **Note**: SHM requires a **linear restoring force** (\( F \propto -x \)). Nonlinear forces produce *anharmonic* oscillations.
