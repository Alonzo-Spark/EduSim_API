### Introduction  
In physics, **work** is a fundamental concept that describes the transfer of energy when a force acts on an object over a distance. It plays a central role in understanding motion, energy, and power in mechanical systems.

---

┌───────────────────────────┐  
│ **Key Concept**           │  
│                           │  
│ Work is only done when:   │  
│ 1. A force is applied     │  
│ 2. The object moves       │  
│ 3. The displacement is in the direction of the force (or has a component in that direction) │  
└───────────────────────────┘  

---

┌───────────────────────────┐  
│ **Main Formula**          │  
│                           │  
│ $$                         │  
│ W = F \cdot d \cdot \cos\theta  
$$                         │  
│                           │  
│ **W** = Work done         │  
│ **F** = Magnitude of force applied │  
│ **d** = Magnitude of displacement │  
│ **θ** = Angle between force and displacement vectors │  
│                           │

└───────────────────────────┘

### Important Notes  
- If the object does **not move**, no work is done — regardless of how much force is applied.  
- When force and displacement are in the **same direction** (θ = 0°), work is **maximum and positive**.  
- When force is **perpendicular** to displacement (θ = 90°), work done is **zero**.  
- When force opposes motion (θ = 180°), work is **negative** (e.g., friction doing negative work).  

---

### SI Unit of Work  
The SI unit of work is the **joule (J)**.  
$$
1~\text{joule} = 1~\text{newton} \times 1~\text{meter} \quad \text{or} \quad 1~\text{J} = 1~\text{N·m}
$$

This means that **1 joule** of work is done when a force of **1 newton** moves an object by **1 meter** in the direction of the force.

---

### Real-Life Examples  
| Situation                     | Work Done? | Explanation |  
|------------------------------|------------|-----------|  
| Pushing a wall that doesn't move | No       | No displacement → $ W = 0 $ |  
| Lifting a book upward        | Yes        | Force and displacement are aligned |  
| Carrying a box horizontally  | Zero (by lifting force) | Force is vertical, displacement horizontal → $ \theta = 90^\circ $, $ \cos 90^\circ = 0 $ |  
| Slowing down a sliding box using friction | Negative work | Friction acts opposite to displacement |  

---

### Connection to Energy  
Work is closely related to energy:  
> **"Work is the process of transferring energy."**

- When work is done **on** an object, energy is **transferred to** it.  
- When work is done **by** an object, it **loses energy**.  
- Hence, **work and energy have the same unit: the joule (J)**.

---

### Summary  
- **Work** is defined as the product of force, displacement, and the cosine of the angle between them.  
- Work is a **scalar quantity** — it has magnitude but no direction.  
- The **SI unit** is the **joule (J)**.  
- Work can be **positive, negative, or zero**, depending on the direction of force relative to motion.
