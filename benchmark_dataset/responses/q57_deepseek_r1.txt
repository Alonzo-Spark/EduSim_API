To understand the visual effect of increasing the driven frequency in a **forced harmonic oscillator**, let’s break it down:

---

### Quick Definition
A **forced harmonic oscillator** is a system (e.g., a mass-spring or pendulum) subjected to an external periodic force. The system oscillates at the **driven frequency** (\( \omega_d \)) of the external force, not just its natural frequency (\( \omega_0 \)).

---

### Key Observations When Increasing Driven Frequency
1. **Amplitude Response**:
   - **Below Resonance** (\( \omega_d < \omega_0 \)):  
     The oscillator follows the driver closely. Amplitude increases as \( \omega_d \) approaches \( \omega_0 \).
   - **At Resonance** (\( \omega_d = \omega_0 \)):  
     Amplitude peaks dramatically (resonance). Energy transfer is maximized.
   - **Above Resonance** (\( \omega_d > \omega_0 \)):  
     Amplitude decreases sharply. The system struggles to keep up with the driver.

   ```math
   \text{Amplitude } A \propto \frac{1}{\sqrt{(\omega_0^2 - \omega_d^2)^2 + (2\beta\omega_d)^2}}
   ```
   *(\( \beta \): damping coefficient)*

2. **Phase Shift**:
   - **Low \( \omega_d \)** → Oscillator moves **in phase** with the driver.
   - **At \( \omega_d = \omega_0 \)** → Phase lag is **90°**.
   - **High \( \omega_d \)** → Oscillator lags by **180°** (out of phase).

---

### Visual Effects
- **Amplitude Changes**:  
  - Starting from low \( \omega_d \), oscillations grow larger until resonance, then shrink.  
  - Example: Pushing a swing—gentle pushes at its natural frequency make it swing high; pushing too fast reduces the effect.  

- **Phase Behavior**:  
  - Below resonance, the system moves in sync with the driver.  
  - Above resonance, it moves oppositely (e.g., driver pushes left while the mass moves right).

---

### Formula Card: Resonance Amplitude
\boxed{A = \frac{F_0/m}{\sqrt{(\omega_0^2 - \omega_d^2)^2 + (2\beta\omega_d)^2}}}
- \( F_0 \): Amplitude of driving force  
- \( m \): Mass of the oscillator  

| Parameter       | Effect on Amplitude          |
|-----------------|------------------------------|
| \( \omega_d ≈ \omega_0 \) | **Max amplitude** (resonance) |
| High damping (\( \beta \)) | Reduces peak amplitude       |

---

### Summary
Increasing \( \omega_d \) causes:
1. **Amplitude** to rise, peak at resonance, then fall.
2. **Phase shift** from 0° to 180°, with a 90° lag at resonance.
