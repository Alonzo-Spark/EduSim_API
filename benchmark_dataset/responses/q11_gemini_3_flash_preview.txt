### 📘 THE PHYSICS CORE: NEWTON’S LAWS OF MOTION

---

| 🧩 **Quick Definition** |
| :--- |
| **Mechanics** is the branch of physics dealing with the motion of objects and the forces that cause that motion. Sir Isaac Newton formulated three fundamental laws that describe the relationship between a body and the forces acting upon it. |

---

### 🟢 SECTION 1: THE LAW OF INERTIA (FIRST LAW)

**The Concept:** An object will not change its motion unless acted upon by an external force. If it is at rest, it stays at rest. If it is moving, it keeps moving at the same speed and direction.

┌──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│ **Key Characteristics**                                  │
│ • **Inertia:** The inherent resistance of an object to   │
│   change its state of motion.                            │
│ • **Mass Connection:** Greater mass = Greater inertia.    │
└──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘

> **Real-World Example:** When a bus suddenly stops, passengers jerk forward. Their bodies were in motion and "want" to stay in motion due to inertia.

---

### 🔵 SECTION 2: THE LAW OF ACCELERATION (SECOND LAW)

**The Concept:** The acceleration of an object depends on the net force acting upon the object and the mass of the object.

┌──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│ 📐 **Main Formula**                                      │
│                                                          │
│                  $$F = m \cdot a$$                       │
│                                                          │
│ **Variables:**                                           │
│ • $F$ = Net Force (Newtons, $N$)                         │
│ • $m$ = Mass (Kilograms, $kg$)                           │
│ • $a$ = Acceleration ($m/s^2$)                           │
│                                                          │
│ [ 🔬 Open Formula Lab ] [ 📘 Detailed Derivation ]       │
└──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘

> **Real-World Example:** It is much easier to push an empty shopping cart (low mass) than a full one (high mass). To get the full cart to move at the same speed, you must apply much more force.

---

### 🔴 SECTION 3: THE LAW OF ACTION & REACTION (THIRD LAW)

**The Concept:** For every action, there is an equal and opposite reaction. Forces always exist in pairs.

| Action Force | Reaction Force |
| :--- | :--- |
| You push against a wall | The wall pushes back on you |
| A rocket expels gas downward | The gas pushes the rocket upward |
| A bird's wing pushes air down | The air pushes the bird up |

> **Important Note:** Action and reaction forces act on **different** objects, which is why they do not cancel each other out.

### 📊 SUMMARY COMPARISON

│ **Law Summary Table**                                                   │
│                                                                          │
| Law | Focus | Core Message |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **1st Law** | Inertia | Objects are "lazy"; they resist change. |
| **2nd Law** | Force/Mass | Force equals mass times acceleration. |
| **3rd Law** | Interactions | You cannot touch without being touched back. |
