### Equivalent Resistance in Series Combination

#### Key Concept: Series Circuit
In a series circuit, resistors are connected end-to-end, forming a single path for current flow. The **same current** passes through each resistor, but the voltage across each resistor depends on its resistance.

---

#### Derivation of Equivalent Resistance

Consider three resistors \(R_1\), \(R_2\), and \(R_3\) connected in series to a voltage source \(V\):

```mermaid
circuit LR
    B(( )) -->|I| R1[R₁] -->|I| R2[R₂] -->|I| R3[R₃] --> E(( ))
    B --> V[Voltage V] --> E
    style B fill:none,stroke:none;
    style E fill:none,stroke:none;
```

##### Step 1: Current Flow
- The current \(I\) is identical through all resistors:  
  \[ I_{\text{total}} = I_{R_1} = I_{R_2} = I_{R_3} \]

##### Step 2: Voltage Distribution (Kirchhoff's Voltage Law)
- The total voltage \(V\) equals the sum of voltages across each resistor:  
  \[ V = V_1 + V_2 + V_3 \]

##### Step 3: Apply Ohm's Law (\(V = IR\))
- Substitute Ohm's Law for each resistor:  
  \[ V_1 = I R_1, \quad V_2 = I R_2, \quad V_3 = I R_3 \]
- Combine with Step 2:  
  \[ V = I R_1 + I R_2 + I R_3 \]  
  \[ V = I (R_1 + R_2 + R_3) \]

##### Step 4: Define Equivalent Resistance (\(R_{\text{eq}}\))
- For a single equivalent resistor \(R_{\text{eq}}\) connected to voltage \(V\):  
  \[ V = I R_{\text{eq}} \]
- Equate to Step 3:  
  \[ I R_{\text{eq}} = I (R_1 + R_2 + R_3) \]
- Solve for \(R_{\text{eq}}\) (canceling \(I \neq 0\)):  
  \[ \boxed{R_{\text{eq}} = R_1 + R_2 + R_3} \]

---

#### General Formula
For \(n\) resistors in series:  
\[
\boxed{R_{\text{eq}} = R_1 + R_2 + R_3 + \cdots + R_n}
\]

---

#### Key Properties
1. **Current Uniformity**:  
   Current remains constant throughout the circuit.
2. **Voltage Division**:  
   Voltage across each resistor is proportional to its resistance:  
   \[ V_k = I R_k = \left( \frac{V}{R_{\text{eq}}} \right) R_k \]
3. **Additive Resistance**:  
   Total resistance increases with each added resistor.

---

#### Practical Example
If \(R_1 = 2\ \Omega\), \(R_2 = 3\ \Omega\), and \(R_3 = 5\ \Omega\) are in series:  
\[ R_{\text{eq}} = 2 + 3 + 5 = 10\ \Omega \]

---

#### Why Does Resistance Add?
- Resistors in series increase the total path length for electrons.
- Each resistor contributes to opposing the current flow, leading to cumulative resistance.

---

### Summary
- **Equivalent resistance** in series is the **arithmetic sum** of individual resistances.
- **Current** is identical through all components.
- **Total voltage** is the sum of voltages across each resistor.
