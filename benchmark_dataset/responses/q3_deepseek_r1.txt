### Understanding Distance vs. Displacement

#### 🔷 Quick Definition  
- **Distance**: A *scalar* quantity measuring the total path length traveled.  
- **Displacement**: A *vector* quantity measuring the straight-line change in position from start to end (including direction).  

---

#### 📊 Key Differences  
| **Aspect**        | **Distance**                          | **Displacement**                     |
|-------------------|---------------------------------------|--------------------------------------|
| **Nature**        | Scalar (magnitude only)               | Vector (magnitude + direction)       |
| **Path Dependence**| Depends on the actual path taken     | Independent of path; only considers start/end points |
| **Zero Value**    | Never zero if motion occurs           | Can be zero if object returns to start |
| **Symbol/Unit**   | \(d\) (meters, km, miles)            | \(\vec{d}\) or \(s\) (meters + direction) |

---

#### 📝 Examples  
1. **Walking a Square Path**  
   - You walk 10m north → 10m east → 10m south → 10m west.  
   - **Distance**: \(10 + 10 + 10 + 10 = 40 \text{m}\) (total path).  
   - **Displacement**: \(0 \text{m}\) (back to start; no net direction).  

2. **Hiking a Mountain Trail**  
   - You climb 3 km up a mountain, then descend 2 km.  
   - **Distance**: \(3 + 2 = 5 \text{km}\) (total traveled).  
   - **Displacement**: \(1 \text{km}\) **upward** (net movement from start).  

3. **Driving to a Friend's House**  
   - Drive 5 km east → 3 km north.  
   - **Distance**: \(5 + 3 = 8 \text{km}\).  
   - **Displacement**: \(\sqrt{5^2 + 3^2} \approx 5.8 \text{km}\) **northeast** (as the crow flies).  

---

#### ⚖️ Formula Card  
**Displacement Calculation**  
For motion in 2D:  
\[
\vec{d} = \sqrt{(x_f - x_i)^2 + (y_f - y_i)^2}
\]  
**Where**:  
- \(x_i, y_i\) = Initial coordinates  
- \(x_f, y_f\) = Final coordinates  
- Direction: \(\theta = \tan^{-1}\left(\frac{y_f - y_i}{x_f - x_i}\right)\) from horizontal  

> **Example**: Moving from \((0, 0)\) to \((3, 4)\):  
> \[
> \vec{d} = \sqrt{(3-0)^2 + (4-0)^2} = 5 \text{ units} \quad (\theta = \tan^{-1}(4/3) \approx 53^\circ \text{ NE})
> \]

---

#### 💎 Key Insights  
1. **Distance ≥ Displacement**:  
   Distance is always equal to or greater than displacement’s magnitude.  
   - *Equality* only occurs for straight-line motion without direction changes.  

2. **Displacement Direction Matters**:  
   Saying "displacement is 50 km" is incomplete; it must include direction (e.g., "50 km east").  

3. **Real-World Analogy**:  
   - **Distance** = Odometer reading in a car.  
   - **Displacement** = Straight-line GPS navigation from home to destination.

#### ❓ Self-Check  
1. A bee flies in a circle of radius 2 m and returns to its hive. What are its distance and displacement?  
   - **Distance**: \(4\pi \approx 12.56 \text{m}\) (circumference).  
   - **Displacement**: \(0 \text{m}\) (back to start).  

2. If displacement is zero, can distance be nonzero?  
   - **Yes!** (e.g., running laps and finishing where you started).
