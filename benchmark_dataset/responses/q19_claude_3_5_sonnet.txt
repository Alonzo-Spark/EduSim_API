# Kinetic Energy

---

## Quick Definition

Kinetic energy is the energy possessed by a body **by virtue of its motion**. Any object that is moving — whether a bullet, a planet, or a raindrop — carries kinetic energy.

> It is a **scalar quantity** and is always **non-negative**.

---

## At a Glance

| Property | Value |
|----------|-------|
| Symbol | $KE$ or $K$ |
| SI Unit | Joule $(\text{J})$ |
| Type | Scalar |
| Depends on | Mass $m$ and Velocity $v$ |
| Value at rest | Zero |

---

## Derivation — Step by Step

**Physical Setup**

A body of mass $m$ is initially **at rest** on a smooth, frictionless surface. A constant net force $F$ acts on it over a displacement $s$, accelerating it to a final velocity $v$.

---

**Step 1 — Newton's Second Law**

$$F = ma$$

---

**Step 2 — Kinematic Equation**

Since the body starts from rest, initial velocity $u = 0$:

$$v^2 = u^2 + 2as \implies v^2 = 2as$$

Solving for displacement:

$$s = \frac{v^2}{2a}$$

---

**Step 3 — Work Done by the Force**

$$W = F \times s = ma \times \frac{v^2}{2a}$$

$$\boxed{W = \frac{1}{2}mv^2}$$

---

**Step 4 — Work-Energy Theorem**

All the work done on the body is stored entirely as kinetic energy (no friction losses):

$$KE = W = \frac{1}{2}mv^2$$

---

## Main Formula

$$KE = \frac{1}{2}mv^2$$

| Symbol | Quantity | Unit |
|--------|----------|------|
| $KE$ | Kinetic Energy | Joule $(\text{J})$ |
| $m$ | Mass of the object | Kilogram $(\text{kg})$ |
| $v$ | Speed of the object | $\text{m/s}$ |

---

## Key Characteristics

**Velocity dominates** — KE grows with the *square* of speed.

$$v \times 2 \implies KE \times 4$$

**Mass is linear** — doubling mass doubles KE.

$$m \times 2 \implies KE \times 2$$

**Always positive** — since both $m > 0$ and $v^2 \geq 0$.

---

## Bonus Relation — KE and Momentum

Momentum is defined as $p = mv$. Substituting $v = p/m$:

$$KE = \frac{p^2}{2m}$$

This connects kinetic energy directly to linear momentum — useful in collision problems.

---

## Applications

| Context | How KE Appears |
|---------|----------------|
| Moving vehicles | Energy stored in motion; released during braking |
| Hydroelectric power | Flowing water's KE drives turbines |
| Wind energy | Moving air does work on turbine blades |
| Ballistics | A fired bullet carries enormous KE due to high $v$ |
| Pendulum | KE is maximum at the lowest point of swing |

---

## Important Note

The **Work-Energy Theorem** — the foundation of this derivation — states:

$$W_{\text{net}} = \Delta KE = \frac{1}{2}mv_f^2 - \frac{1}{2}mv_i^2$$

This holds for **any** net force, not just constant forces. The derivation above is a special case where $u = 0$ and $F$ is constant.
