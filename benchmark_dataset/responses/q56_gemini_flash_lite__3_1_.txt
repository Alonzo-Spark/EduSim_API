        The coefficient of restitution (COR), often denoted by *e*, is a number between 0 and 1 that describes the

        collision.
    </p>
    <ul>

        <li>*e* = 0:  Perfectly inelastic collision (the objects stick together after the collision).</li>
    </ul>

    </p>
    <ul>
        <li><b>Perfectly Elastic Collision (*e* = 1):</b> In this ideal scenario, kinetic energy is conserved.  The objects
        separate with the same relative speed as they approached each other.  Think of idealized collisions like those
        between billiard balls.</li>
        <li><b>Inelastic Collision (0 < *e* < 1):</b>  Some kinetic energy is lost during the collision, typically
        converted into heat, sound, or deformation of the objects.  The objects separate with a lower relative speed than
        they approached each other.  Most real-world collisions fall into this category.</li>
        <li><b>Perfectly Inelastic Collision (*e* = 0):</b> The objects stick together after the collision and move as a
        single unit.  All kinetic energy not used to move the combined mass is lost.  Imagine a collision where two pieces
        of clay stick together.</li>
