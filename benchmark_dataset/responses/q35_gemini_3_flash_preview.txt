### 📘 Physics Module: Phase Transitions

┌──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│ **Quick Definition: Latent Heat**                                │
│                                                                  │
│ Latent heat is the energy absorbed or released by a substance    │
│ during a change in its physical state (phase) that occurs        │
│ **without** changing its temperature.                            │
└──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘

### 🌡️ Key Concepts: Change of State
Matter exists in three primary phases: **Solid, Liquid, and Gas**. A change of state occurs when thermal energy breaks or forms molecular bonds rather than increasing kinetic energy (temperature).

| Process | Transition | Energy Change |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Fusion (Melting)** | Solid → Liquid | Absorbed |
| **Vaporization** | Liquid → Gas | Absorbed |
| **Condensation** | Gas → Liquid | Released |
| **Freezing** | Liquid → Solid | Released |

---

### 🧪 Main Formula: Specific Latent Heat

┌──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│ **Formula Card: Latent Heat Calculation**                        │
│                                                                  │
│ $$Q = m \cdot L$$                                                │
│                                                                  │
│ **Where:**                                                       │
│ • $Q$ = Total Heat Energy (Joules, $J$)                          │
│ • $m$ = Mass of the substance (kg)                               │
│ • $L$ = Specific Latent Heat ($J/kg$)                            │
│                                                                  │

└──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘

### 🔍 Characteristics of Phase Change
*   **Isothermal Process:** During the actual transition (e.g., boiling water at $100^\circ C$), the temperature remains constant until the entire mass has changed state.
*   **Internal Energy:** While temperature (average kinetic energy) stays the same, the **potential energy** of the molecules increases or decreases as bonds are manipulated.
*   **Hidden Energy:** The word "Latent" comes from the Latin *latere* (to lie hidden), because the heat added does not show up on a thermometer.

---

### 💡 Types of Latent Heat

┌──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│ **Latent Heat of Fusion ($L_f$)**                                │
│                                                                  │
│ The heat required to change $1kg$ of a substance from **solid**  │
│ to **liquid** at its melting point.                              │
│ *Example: Ice to Water*                                          │
└──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘

┌──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│ **Latent Heat of Vaporization ($L_v$)**                          │
│                                                                  │
│ The heat required to change $1kg$ of a substance from **liquid** │
│ to **gas** at its boiling point.                                 │
│ *Example: Water to Steam*                                        │
└──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘

---

### ⚠️ Important Notes
> **Why is $L_v$ usually much larger than $L_f$?**
> To turn a liquid into a gas, molecules must be pulled completely apart, overcoming almost all attractive forces. To turn a solid into a liquid, the bonds only need to be loosened/weakened. Therefore, vaporization requires significantly more energy.
