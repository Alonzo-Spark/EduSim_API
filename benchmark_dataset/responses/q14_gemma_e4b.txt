## Introduction

Understanding momentum allows us to analyze collisions, explosions, and other interactions between objects. DEFINITION

## Definition

Momentum, denoted by the symbol *p*, is defined as the product of an object's mass (*m*) and its velocity (*v*).  It is a vector quantity, meaning it has both magnitude and direction.

$$
p = mv
$$

The momentum is measured in kilogram-meters per second (kg⋅m/s). A heavier object moving at the same velocity as a lighter object will have greater momentum. Similarly, an object moving faster will have more momentum.



=========================================================
KEY CONCEPTS
=========================================================

## Key Concepts

*   **Vector Quantity:** Momentum has both magnitude and direction, aligned with the velocity.
*   **Inertia:** Momentum is related to an object's inertia, representing its resistance to changes in motion.  Higher momentum signifies higher inertia.
*   **Impulse:**  A change in momentum is caused by a force acting over a period of time.  The impulse-momentum theorem quantifies this relationship.
*   **Conservation of Momentum:**  In a closed system (no external forces), the total momentum remains constant.

## Formula

The fundamental formula for momentum is:

$$
p = mv
$$

This equation states that the momentum (*p*) of an object is equal to its mass (*m*) multiplied by its velocity (*v*).

## Law of Conservation of Momentum

The law of conservation of momentum states that in a closed system (one where no external forces act), the total momentum remains constant. This means that the total momentum *before* an event (like a collision) is equal to the total momentum *after* the event.

Mathematically, this can be expressed as:

$$
\sum_{i} p_i = p_{total} = constant
$$

where the sum is over all objects in the system, and *p<sub>i</sub>* is the momentum of the *i*-th object.

Let's consider two objects, A and B, with masses *m<sub>A</sub>* and *m<sub>B</sub>*, and velocities *v<sub>A</sub>* and *v<sub>B</sub>* respectively, before a collision.  After the collision, their velocities are *v'<sub>A</sub>* and *v'<sub>B</sub>*.

The law of conservation of momentum can then be written as:

$$
m_A v_A + m_B v_B = m_A v'_A + m_B v'_B
$$
