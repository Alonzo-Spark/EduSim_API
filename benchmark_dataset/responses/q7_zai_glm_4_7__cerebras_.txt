                <p>
                    When an object is thrown into the air, it follows a curved path known as a parabola. The highest vertical point reached by the projectile during its flight is called the <strong>maximum height</strong> (denoted as $H$).
                </p>
                <p>
                    At this specific point, the vertical component of the projectile's velocity becomes zero momentarily before gravity pulls it back down. To derive the expression, we focus solely on the vertical motion of the object.
                </p>

        <!-- Card: Derivation -->
        <section class="card">
            <div class="card-header">
                <div class="card-icon">🧮</div>
                <h2>Step-by-Step Derivation</h2>
            </div>
            <div class="card-body">
                <ol class="step-list">
                    <li>
                        <strong>Identify Vertical Motion Parameters:</strong>
                        Consider a projectile launched with initial velocity $u$ at an angle $\theta$ to the horizontal.
                        The initial vertical velocity is:
                        $$u_y = u \sin \theta$$
                        The acceleration acting downwards is gravity: $a = -g$.
                    </li>
                    <li>
                        <strong>Condition at Maximum Height:</strong>
                        At the highest point ($H$), the vertical velocity becomes zero.
                        $$v_y = 0$$
                    </li>
                    <li>
                        <strong>Apply Equation of Motion:</strong>
                        We use the kinematic equation that relates velocity, acceleration, and displacement (not time):
                        $$v^2 = u^2 + 2as$$
                    </li>
                    <li>
                        <strong>Substitute Values:</strong>
                        Substitute vertical components into the equation:
                        $$0^2 = (u \sin \theta)^2 + 2(-g)H$$
                        Simplify:
                        $$0 = u^2 \sin^2 \theta - 2gH$$
                    </li>
                    <li>
                        <strong>Solve for Height ($H$):</strong>
                        Rearrange the equation to isolate $H$:
                        $$2gH = u^2 \sin^2 \theta$$
                        $$H = \frac{u^2 \sin^2 \theta}{2g}$$
                    </li>
                </ol>
            </div>
        </section>
