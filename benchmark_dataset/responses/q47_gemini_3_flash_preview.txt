### 📘 Physics Module: Electrical Circuits

---

#### 🟢 Introduction
In a series combination, electrical components are arranged in a single path, such that the same current flows through all of them sequentially. This derivation determines the single total resistance ($R_s$) that could replace all individual resistors while keeping the circuit behavior identical.

---

#### 📍 Definition
A **Series Circuit** is a configuration where resistors are connected end-to-end. There is only one path for the flow of electrons, meaning the current ($I$) remains constant across every resistor in the chain.

---

#### ⚡ Key Concepts
*   **Current ($I$):** Remains constant throughout the circuit. $I = I_1 = I_2 = I_3$.
*   **Voltage ($V$):** The total potential difference is the sum of individual voltage drops. $V = V_1 + V_2 + V_3$.
*   **Ohm's Law:** The fundamental relationship $V = IR$ applies to each component and the whole circuit.

#### 📝 The Derivation
Consider three resistors $R_1, R_2,$ and $R_3$ connected in series to a battery of voltage $V$.

1.  **Apply Ohm's Law** to each individual resistor:
    *   $V_1 = I \cdot R_1$
    *   $V_2 = I \cdot R_2$
    *   $V_3 = I \cdot R_3$

2.  **Substitute** these into the total voltage equation:
    *   $V = (I \cdot R_1) + (I \cdot R_2) + (I \cdot R_3)$

3.  **Factorize** the common current ($I$):
    *   $V = I(R_1 + R_2 + R_3)$

4.  **Define Equivalent Resistance ($R_s$):**
    If $R_s$ is the total resistance, then by Ohm's Law: $V = I \cdot R_s$.

5.  **Equate the two expressions:**
    *   $I \cdot R_s = I(R_1 + R_2 + R_3)$
    *   Cancel $I$ from both sides.

#### 📊 Characteristics Summary
| Feature | Behavior in Series |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Current** | Same at every point in the circuit. |
| **Voltage** | Divided among resistors proportional to their resistance. |
| **Total Resistance** | Always greater than the largest individual resistor. |
| **Fault Impact** | If one component breaks, the entire circuit fails. |

---

#### 💡 Important Notes
> **Maximized Resistance:** Use a series connection when you need to increase the total resistance in a circuit or limit the overall current flow.
>
> **Identical Resistors:** If $n$ resistors of equal value $R$ are connected in series, the total resistance is simply $R_s = nR$.
