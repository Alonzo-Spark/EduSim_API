## Gravitational Force Calculation

Let's calculate the gravitational force between two objects using Newton's Law of Universal Gravitation.

---

### Quick Definition

The **gravitational force** is the attractive force that exists between any two objects with mass. Its strength depends on the masses of the objects and the distance between their centers.

---

### Main Formula

$$ F = G \frac{m_1 m_2}{r^2} $$

<details>
<summary>Explain Formula</summary>
The formula for gravitational force ($F$) is derived from Newton's Law of Universal Gravitation.

*   $F$: The gravitational force between the two objects (measured in Newtons, N).
*   $G$: The universal gravitational constant, approximately $6.674 \times 10^{-11} \text{ N} \cdot \text{m}^2/\text{kg}^2$. This constant accounts for the proportionality between mass, distance, and force.
*   $m_1$: The mass of the first object (measured in kilograms, kg).
*   $m_2$: The mass of the second object (measured in kilograms, kg).
*   $r$: The distance between the centers of the two objects (measured in meters, m).

The formula shows that the gravitational force is directly proportional to the product of the masses and inversely proportional to the square of the distance between them. This means that if the masses increase, the force increases, and if the distance increases, the force decreases rapidly.

### Given Values

Let's identify the values provided in the problem:

*   Mass of the first object ($m_1$): $5 \text{ kg}$
*   Mass of the second object ($m_2$): $5 \text{ kg}$
*   Distance between the objects ($r$): $2 \text{ m}$
*   Universal Gravitational Constant ($G$): $6.674 \times 10^{-11} \text{ N} \cdot \text{m}^2/\text{kg}^2$

---

### Step-by-Step Calculation

1.  **Write down the formula:**
    $$ F = G \frac{m_1 m_2}{r^2} $$

2.  **Substitute the given values into the formula:**
    $$ F = (6.674 \times 10^{-11} \text{ N} \cdot \text{m}^2/\text{kg}^2) \frac{(5 \text{ kg})(5 \text{ kg})}{(2 \text{ m})^2} $$

3.  **Calculate the product of the masses:**
    $$ m_1 m_2 = 5 \text{ kg} \times 5 \text{ kg} = 25 \text{ kg}^2 $$

4.  **Calculate the square of the distance:**
    $$ r^2 = (2 \text{ m})^2 = 4 \text{ m}^2 $$

5.  **Substitute these results back into the formula:**
    $$ F = (6.674 \times 10^{-11} \text{ N} \cdot \text{m}^2/\text{kg}^2) \frac{25 \text{ kg}^2}{4 \text{ m}^2} $$

6.  **Perform the division:**
    $$ \frac{25 \text{ kg}^2}{4 \text{ m}^2} = 6.25 \text{ kg}^2/\text{m}^2 $$

7.  **Multiply by the gravitational constant:**
    $$ F = 6.674 \times 10^{-11} \times 6.25 \text{ N} $$
    $$ F = 41.7125 \times 10^{-11} \text{ N} $$

8.  **Express the answer in scientific notation:**
    $$ F \approx 4.17 \times 10^{-10} \text{ N} $$

---

### Result

The gravitational force between the two masses is approximately $4.17 \times 10^{-10} \text{ N}$.

---

### Important Notes

*   The gravitational force is a very weak force, especially between objects of ordinary mass. This is why we don't observe everyday objects attracting each other noticeably.
*   The distance 'r' is measured from the center of mass of each object. For spherical objects, this is the distance between their centers.
*   The unit of force is Newtons (N).
