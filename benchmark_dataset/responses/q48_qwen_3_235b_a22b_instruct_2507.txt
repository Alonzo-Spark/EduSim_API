┌───────────────────────────┐  
│ **Key Concept**           │  
│ Parallel Resistance       │  
└───────────────────────────┘  

When resistors are connected in **parallel**, they share the same voltage across each resistor, but the current divides among the branches.  

To derive the **equivalent resistance** \( R_{eq} \) of resistors in parallel, we apply **Ohm’s Law** and **Kirchhoff’s Current Law (KCL)**.

---

**Step 1: Apply Kirchhoff’s Current Law (KCL)**  
At the junction, the total current \( I \) entering is equal to the sum of the currents leaving through each resistor:

\[
I = I_1 + I_2 + I_3 + \cdots + I_n
\]

---

**Step 2: Use Ohm’s Law**  
Each current through a resistor is given by:

\[
I_1 = \frac{V}{R_1},\quad I_2 = \frac{V}{R_2},\quad I_3 = \frac{V}{R_3},\quad \dots,\quad I_n = \frac{V}{R_n}
\]

Substitute into KCL equation:

\[
I = \frac{V}{R_1} + \frac{V}{R_2} + \frac{V}{R_3} + \cdots + \frac{V}{R_n}
\]

Factor out voltage \( V \) (same across all resistors in parallel):

\[
I = V \left( \frac{1}{R_1} + \frac{1}{R_2} + \frac{1}{R_3} + \cdots + \frac{1}{R_n} \right)
\]

---

**Step 3: Express in terms of equivalent resistance**  
By definition, the total current \( I = \frac{V}{R_{eq}} \). Substitute:

\[
\frac{V}{R_{eq}} = V \left( \frac{1}{R_1} + \frac{1}{R_2} + \frac{1}{R_3} + \cdots + \frac{1}{R_n} \right)
\]

Divide both sides by \( V \) (assuming \( V \neq 0 \)):

\[
\frac{1}{R_{eq}} = \frac{1}{R_1} + \frac{1}{R_2} + \frac{1}{R_3} + \cdots + \frac{1}{R_n}
\]

---

│ **Main Formula**          │  
│                           │  
│   \[                     │  
│   \frac{1}{R_{eq}} =     │  
│   \sum_{i=1}^{n}          │  
│   \frac{1}{R_i}           │  
│   \]                     │  
│                           │

This is the general expression for **equivalent resistance in parallel combination**.

---

**Special Case: Two Resistors in Parallel**

\[
R_{eq} = \frac{R_1 R_2}{R_1 + R_2}
\]

---

┌───────────────────────────┐  
│ **Important Notes**       │  
│                           │  
• The equivalent resistance \( R_{eq} \) is **always less than** the smallest individual resistor.  
• Adding more resistors in parallel **decreases** total resistance.  
• Voltage is the **same** across all parallel branches.  
• Current **splits** inversely proportional to resistance.  
└───────────────────────────┘
