# Kinetic Energy

## Quick Definition

Kinetic energy is the energy possessed by an object due to its motion. Any object in motion has kinetic energy, which depends on both its mass and speed.

---

## Key Concept

When work is done on an object to make it move from rest, energy is transferred to the object. This energy, stored in the form of motion, is called **kinetic energy**.

---

## Derivation of Kinetic Energy Expression

Let’s derive the expression for kinetic energy using the **work-energy principle**.

Suppose:
- A body of mass $ m $ is initially at rest (initial velocity $ u = 0 $).
- A constant force $ F $ is applied, causing it to accelerate to a final velocity $ v $ over a distance $ s $.

We know from Newton’s second law:
$$
F = ma
$$
where $ a $ is the constant acceleration.

From the third equation of motion:
$$
v^2 = u^2 + 2as
$$
Since $ u = 0 $, this becomes:
$$
v^2 = 2as \quad \Rightarrow \quad s = \frac{v^2}{2a}
$$

Work done by the force $ F $ over distance $ s $ is:
$$
W = F \cdot s = (ma) \cdot \left( \frac{v^2}{2a} \right) = \frac{1}{2} m v^2
$$

This work done is equal to the **kinetic energy** gained by the object.

---

## Main Formula

$$
K = \frac{1}{2}mv^2
$$

## Important Notes

- Kinetic energy is a **scalar quantity** — it has magnitude but no direction.
- It depends on the **square of the velocity**, so doubling the speed quadruples the kinetic energy.
- Kinetic energy is always **positive or zero**, since mass is positive and $ v^2 \geq 0 $.

---

## Applications

- Calculating stopping distances in vehicles.
- Understanding energy transfer in collisions.
- Used in engineering, space missions, and particle physics.

---

## Summary

- Kinetic energy is energy due to motion.
- Derived from work done to accelerate a body from rest.
- Formula: $ K = \frac{1}{2}mv^2 $.
- Proportional to mass and the square of velocity.
