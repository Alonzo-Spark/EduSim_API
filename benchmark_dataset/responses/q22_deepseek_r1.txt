To derive the expression for acceleration due to gravity, we'll start with Newton's Law of Universal Gravitation and analyze gravity at Earth's surface and at a height \(h\) above it. Here's the step-by-step derivation:

---

### **1. Newton's Law of Universal Gravitation**
The gravitational force \(F\) between two masses \(M\) (Earth) and \(m\) (object) is:
\[
F = G \frac{M \cdot m}{r^2}
\]
where:
- \(G\) = Gravitational constant (\(6.674 \times 10^{-11}  \text{N·m}^2\text{/kg}^2\)),
- \(r\) = Distance between centers of the masses.

---

### **2. Acceleration Due to Gravity at Earth's Surface**
- For an object on Earth's surface, \(r = R\) (Earth's radius).
- By Newton's second law, \(F = m \cdot g\), where \(g\) is acceleration due to gravity.
- Equate the two expressions:
  \[
  m \cdot g = G \frac{M \cdot m}{R^2}
  \]
- Cancel \(m\) (mass of the object):
  \[
  g = \frac{G M}{R^2}
  \]

---

### **3. Acceleration Due to Gravity at Height \(h\)**
- For an object at height \(h\) above Earth, \(r = R + h\).
- Gravitational force:
  \[
  F = G \frac{M \cdot m}{(R + h)^2}
  \]
- Using \(F = m \cdot g'\) (where \(g'\) is gravity at height \(h\)):
  \[
  m \cdot g' = G \frac{M \cdot m}{(R + h)^2}
  \]
- Cancel \(m\):
  \[
  g' = \frac{G M}{(R + h)^2}
  \]
- Substitute \(g = \frac{G M}{R^2}\):
  \[
  g' = g \cdot \frac{R^2}{(R + h)^2} = g \left( \frac{R}{R + h} \right)^2
  \]

---

### **Key Results**
| Scenario | Formula |
|----------|---------|
| **At Earth's surface** | \( g = \dfrac{GM}{R^2} \) |
| **At height \(h\)** | \( g' = g \left( \dfrac{R}{R + h} \right)^2 \) |

---

### **Important Notes**
1. **Why mass \(m\) cancels**:  
   Acceleration due to gravity is independent of the object's mass, consistent with Galileo's observation that all objects fall at the same rate in a vacuum.

2. **Assumptions**:  
   - Earth is a uniform sphere (not strictly true but valid for surface calculations).
   - \(h \ll R\) simplifies to \(g' \approx g(1 - \frac{2h}{R})\).

3. **Decrease with height**:  
   \(g' < g\) since \(\left( \frac{R}{R + h} \right)^2 < 1\). For example:
   - At \(h = R\), \(g' = \frac{g}{4}\).

---

### **Practical Applications**
- **Satellite orbits**: Determines orbital velocity (\(v = \sqrt{g' (R + h)}\)).
- **Topography studies**: Measures tiny variations in \(g\) to map underground structures.
- **Space missions**: Adjusts thrust for gravity changes during ascent.

### **Summary**
- Gravity arises from mass and distance: \(g \propto \frac{M}{r^2}\).
- At Earth's surface: \(g = \frac{GM}{R^2}\).
- Above surface: \(g\) decreases as \(\left( \frac{R}{R + h} \right)^2\).
