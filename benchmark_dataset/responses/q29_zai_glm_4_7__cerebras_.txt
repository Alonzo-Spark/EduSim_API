┌───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│ Quick Definition                                               │
│                                                               │
│ Surface tension is the tendency of liquid surfaces to shrink   │
│ into the minimum surface area possible. It is defined as the   │
│ force acting per unit length perpendicular to an imaginary     │
│ line drawn on the surface of the liquid.                       │
│                                                               │
│ This phenomenon occurs because the molecules at the surface    │
│ experience a net inward force due to higher cohesion with      │
│ neighboring molecules below them compared to the air above.   │
└───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘

┌───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│ Main Formula                                                   │
│                                                               │
│                    $$T = \frac{F}{l}$$                        │
│                                                               │
│ Where:                                                         │
│ • $T$ = Surface Tension (N/m)                                 │
│ • $F$ = Force acting perpendicular to the surface (N)         │
│ • $l$ = Length of the imaginary line on the surface (m)       │

┌───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│ Factors Affecting Surface Tension                              │
│                                                               │
│ 1. Temperature                                                │
│ Surface tension is inversely proportional to temperature. As  │
│ temperature increases, the kinetic energy of molecules        │
│ increases, weakening the intermolecular cohesive forces. It   │
│ becomes zero at the critical temperature.                     │
│                                                               │
│ 2. Nature of the Liquid                                        │
│ Liquids with stronger intermolecular forces (like water due   │
│ to hydrogen bonding) have higher surface tension than those   │
│ with weaker forces (like benzene or ether).                   │
│                                                               │
│ 3. Impurities (Solutes)                                        │
│ • **Highly soluble substances** (e.g., salt, sugar): Usually  │
│   increase surface tension.                                   │
│ • **Surfactants** (e.g., soap, detergent): Decrease surface   │
│   tension significantly by accumulating at the surface.       │
│                                                               │
│ 4. Contamination                                               │
│ Dust, oil, or grease on the surface generally reduces surface  │
│ tension by disrupting the cohesive forces at the interface.   │
│                                                               │
│ 5. Medium Above the Surface                                    │
│ The surface tension depends on the medium in contact with the  │
│ liquid (e.g., water-air interface vs. water-glass interface). │
└───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘

┌───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│ Important Notes                                                │
│                                                               │
│ • Surface tension creates a "skin" effect on the liquid,      │
│   allowing small insects to walk on water.                    │
│ • It is responsible for the spherical shape of liquid drops   │
│   (minimum surface area for a given volume).                  │
│ • The SI unit of surface tension is N/m (Newton per meter)    │
│   or J/m² (Joule per square meter).                           │
└───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘
