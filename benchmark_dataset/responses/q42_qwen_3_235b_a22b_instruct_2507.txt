# Electric Field and Electric Potential

---

## Quick Definition

**Electric Field**:  
A region around a charged particle where a force would be exerted on other charged particles. It is a vector quantity.

**Electric Potential**:  
The amount of work needed to move a unit positive charge from infinity to a specific point in an electric field without acceleration. It is a scalar quantity.

---

## Key Concepts

### Electric Field

- Denoted by **E**
- Measured in **volts per meter (V/m)** or **newtons per coulomb (N/C)**
- Acts in the direction a positive test charge would move
- Created by electric charges or changing magnetic fields

### Electric Potential

- Denoted by **V**
- Measured in **volts (V)** or **joules per coulomb (J/C)**
- Describes the potential energy per unit charge at a point
- Independent of direction

---

## Formula Card

$$
\vec{E} = \frac{\vec{F}}{q}
$$

**Electric Field Definition Formula**

$$
V = \frac{U}{q}
$$

**Electric Potential Definition Formula**

$$
V = \frac{kQ}{r}
$$

**Electric Potential due to a Point Charge**

$$
E = \frac{kQ}{r^2}
$$

**Electric Field due to a Point Charge**

> **Note:** $ k = \frac{1}{4\pi\varepsilon_0} $, $ Q $ = source charge, $ r $ = distance, $ q $ = test charge

---

## Characteristics Comparison

| Feature | Electric Field | Electric Potential |
|--------|----------------|--------------------|
| **Nature** | Vector | Scalar |
| **Units** | N/C or V/m | Volts (V) |
| **Direction** | Yes (force on + charge) | No (only magnitude) |
| **Zero Reference** | No absolute zero | Zero at infinity |
| **Superposition** | Vector sum | Algebraic sum |

---

## Relationship Between E and V

The electric field is the negative gradient of electric potential:

$$
\vec{E} = -\nabla V
$$

In one dimension:
$$
E = -\frac{dV}{dr}
$$

This means:
- The electric field points in the direction of **decreasing potential**
- A strong electric field exists where the potential changes rapidly with distance

---

## Important Notes

- The electric field tells you **how strong** the force is and **which way** it acts.
- The electric potential tells you **how much energy** a charge has at a point.
- You can have a non-zero electric potential even when the electric field is zero (e.g., inside a charged spherical shell).
- Conversely, electric field can be high while potential is low — depends on the reference point.

---

## Applications

- **Capacitors**: Use electric fields to store energy
- **Cathode Ray Tubes (CRTs)**: Use electric fields to steer electrons
- **Electrostatic Precipitators**: Remove particles from air using electric fields
- **Voltage Measurement**: Based on differences in electric potential

---

## Summary

> The **electric field** describes the **force** experienced by a charge in space, while **electric potential** describes the **energy** associated with placing a charge at a point. They are fundamentally related — the electric field is the rate of change of potential with distance.
