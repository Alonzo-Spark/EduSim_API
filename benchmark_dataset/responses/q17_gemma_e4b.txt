# Work-Energy Theorem

## Introduction

The work-energy theorem is a fundamental principle in physics that relates the work done on an object to its kinetic energy. It states that the net work done on an object is equal to the change in its kinetic energy. This is a cornerstone concept for understanding motion and energy transformations.

## Definition

The work-energy theorem can be expressed mathematically as:

$$W_{net} = \Delta KE = KE_f - KE_i$$

Where:

*   $W_{net}$ is the net work done on the object.
*   $\Delta KE$ is the change in kinetic energy.
*   $KE_f$ is the final kinetic energy.
*   $KE_i$ is the initial kinetic energy.



## Key Concepts

*   **Work:**  Work is done when a force causes a displacement of an object.  It is measured as the product of the force and the displacement in the direction of the force.
*   **Kinetic Energy:** Kinetic energy is the energy an object possesses due to its motion. It is given by the formula $KE = \frac{1}{2}mv^2$, where $m$ is mass and $v$ is velocity.
*   **Net Work:**  The net work is the sum of all the work done on an object.  It can be positive (work done *by* the system), negative (work done *on* the system), or zero.



## Formula

$$W_{net} = \Delta KE = KE_f - KE_i$$

The work-energy theorem assumes that all forces are either conservative (like gravity) or non-conservative (like friction).  For conservative forces, work done by a closed path is zero.
