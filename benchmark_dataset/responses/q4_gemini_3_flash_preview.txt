:::section{title="Introduction"}
In kinematics, the second equation of motion relates the **displacement ($s$)** of an object to its **initial velocity ($u$)**, **time ($t$)**, and constant **acceleration ($a$)**. This derivation assumes motion in a straight line with uniform acceleration.
:::

:::section{title="Key Concepts"}
To derive this relation, we utilize two fundamental definitions of velocity:
1.  **Average Velocity:** The arithmetic mean of initial and final velocity.
2.  **Displacement:** The product of average velocity and time.
3.  **First Equation of Motion:** $v = u + at$.
:::

:::formula-card{title="The Displacement-Time Relation"}
$$
s = ut + \frac{1}{2}at^2
$$
**Variables:**
*   $s$: Displacement (meters)
*   $u$: Initial velocity (m/s)
*   $a$: Uniform acceleration (m/s²)
*   $t$: Time interval (seconds)

:::

:::section{title="Step-by-Step Derivation"}

**Step 1: Define Average Velocity**
For an object moving with uniform acceleration, the average velocity ($v_{avg}$) is given by:
$$v_{avg} = \frac{u + v}{2}$$

**Step 2: Relate Displacement to Average Velocity**
Displacement is defined as the product of average velocity and total time:
$$s = v_{avg} \times t$$
Substituting the expression from Step 1:
$$s = \left( \frac{u + v}{2} \right) \times t \quad \text{--- (Equation A)}$$

**Step 3: Substitute the First Equation of Motion**
We know from the first equation of motion that:
$$v = u + at$$
Substitute this value of $v$ into **Equation A**:
$$s = \left( \frac{u + (u + at)}{2} \right) \times t$$

**Step 4: Simplify the Expression**
Combine the like terms inside the parentheses:
$$s = \left( \frac{2u + at}{2} \right) \times t$$
Divide each term by 2:
$$s = \left( u + \frac{1}{2}at \right) \times t$$
Distribute the $t$:
$$s = ut + \frac{1}{2}at^2$$
:::

:::section{title="Graphical Interpretation"}
| Component | Representation on $v-t$ Graph |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Area of Rectangle** | Represents the term $ut$ (initial velocity $\times$ time) |
| **Area of Triangle** | Represents the term $\frac{1}{2}at^2$ (distance due to acceleration) |
| **Total Area** | The total area under the velocity-time curve equals displacement $s$ |
:::

:::section{title="Important Notes"}
*   **Uniform Acceleration:** This formula is **only** valid if acceleration is constant. If acceleration changes, calculus (integration) must be used.
*   **Vector Nature:** Displacement, velocity, and acceleration are vectors. Ensure consistent signs (e.g., if $u$ is positive, deceleration $a$ must be negative).
*   **Starting from Rest:** If the object starts from rest, $u = 0$, and the formula simplifies to $s = \frac{1}{2}at^2$.
:::
