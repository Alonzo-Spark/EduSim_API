### Key Concepts

Pressure quantifies how much force is concentrated on a given area. It plays a fundamental role in fluid mechanics, determining how fluids exert forces on containers and submerged objects.

In fluids (liquids and gases), pressure acts equally in all directions at any point and increases with depth due to the weight of the fluid above.

### Characteristics of Pressure in Fluids

- **Scalar Quantity**: Though force is a vector, pressure has no direction — it is a scalar.
- **Transmitted Equally**: In enclosed fluids, pressure changes are transmitted undiminished (Pascal’s Principle).
- **Depth-Dependent**: In static fluids, pressure increases linearly with depth:  
  $$
  P = P_0 + \rho g h
  $$
- **Acts Perpendicularly**: Fluid pressure always acts normal (perpendicular) to the surface in contact.

---

### Applications

- Hydraulic lifts and brakes (based on Pascal’s Law)
- Blood pressure measurement
- Atmospheric pressure monitoring (barometers)
- Underwater engineering (submarines, dams)

---

### Important Notes

- The **pascal (Pa)** is a relatively small unit; hence, kilopascals (kPa) or atmospheres (atm) are often used in practice.
- 1 atm = 101,325 Pa ≈ 1.013 × 10⁵ Pa
- Gauge pressure vs. absolute pressure:  
  - **Gauge Pressure**: Pressure relative to atmospheric pressure  
  - **Absolute Pressure**: Total pressure including atmospheric pressure

---

### Summary

Pressure is a core concept in the mechanical properties of fluids, describing how force is distributed over an area. It governs behavior in both static and moving fluids and underpins technologies from hydraulics to medical devices.
