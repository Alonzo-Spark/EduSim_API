### **First Law of Thermodynamics**

┌───────────────────────────┐  
│ **Quick Definition**      │  
│                           │  
│ Energy cannot be created or destroyed—only converted from one form to another. │  
└───────────────────────────┘  

$$
\Delta U = Q - W
$$

┌───────────────────────────┐  
│ **Main Formula**          │  
│                           │  
│ $$  
\Delta U = Q - W  
$$  │  
│                           │  
│ - $\Delta U$: Change in internal energy of the system  
│ - $Q$: Heat added to the system  
│ - $W$: Work done *by* the system  
│                           │  
│ [ Explain Formula ]       │  
│ [ Open Formula Lab ]      │  
└───────────────────────────┘  

> 🔹 Also known as the **law of energy conservation**.  
> 🔹 If heat is added to a system, it either increases internal energy or does work (or both).  
> 🔹 Sign convention:  
> - $Q > 0$: Heat flows *into* system  
> - $W > 0$: System does work *on surroundings*

---

### **Second Law of Thermodynamics**

┌───────────────────────────┐  
│ **Key Concepts**          │  
│                           │  
│ The total entropy of an isolated system can never decrease over time. Natural processes are irreversible and tend toward increasing disorder. │  
└───────────────────────────┘  

There are several equivalent statements:

#### 🔹 **Clausius Statement**  
> "Heat cannot spontaneously flow from a colder body to a hotter body."

#### 🔹 **Kelvin-Planck Statement**  
> "It is impossible to convert heat completely into work in a cyclic process without other effects."

#### 🔹 **Entropy Statement**  
> For any spontaneous process in an isolated system:  
>  
> $$
> \Delta S_{\text{universe}} = \Delta S_{\text{system}} + \Delta S_{\text{surroundings}} \geq 0
> $$

┌───────────────────────────┐  
│ **Main Formula**          │  
│                           │  
│ $$  
\Delta S \geq \int \frac{\delta Q}{T}  
$$  │  
│                           │  
│ - $\Delta S$: Change in entropy  
│ - $\delta Q$: Infinitesimal heat transfer  
│ - $T$: Absolute temperature (in Kelvin)  
│                           │  
│ [ Explain Formula ]       │  
│ [ Open Formula Lab ]      │  
└───────────────────────────┘  

> 🔹 Equality holds only for reversible processes.  
> 🔹 In real (irreversible) processes, $\Delta S > \int \frac{\delta Q}{T}$.

---

### **Third Law of Thermodynamics**

┌───────────────────────────┐  
│ **Definition**            │  
│                           │  
│ As the temperature of a perfect crystalline substance approaches absolute zero, its entropy approaches zero. │  
└───────────────────────────┘  

$$
\lim_{T \to 0} S = 0
$$

┌───────────────────────────┐  
│ **Main Formula**          │  
│                           │  
│ $$  
\lim_{T \to 0} S = 0  
$$  │  
│                           │  
│ - $S$: Entropy of a perfect crystal  
│ - $T \to 0$: Approaching absolute zero (0 K)  
│                           │  
│ [ Explain Formula ]       │  
│ [ Open Formula Lab ]      │  
└───────────────────────────┘  

> 🔹 At 0 K, particles are in their lowest energy state with no disorder.  
> 🔹 Only applies to perfectly ordered crystals.  
> 🔹 Absolute zero is unattainable in finite steps (**Nernst’s theorem**).

---

### **Zeroth Law of Thermodynamics**

┌───────────────────────────┐  
│ **Fundamental Principle** │  
│                           │  
│ If two systems are each in thermal equilibrium with a third system, they are in thermal equilibrium with each other. │  
└───────────────────────────┘  

> 🔹 Defines the basis for temperature measurement.  
> 🔹 Establishes **temperature** as a valid and transitive property.  
> 🔹 Allows use of thermometers: if A = B and B = C, then A = C (in terms of temperature).

### **Summary Table**

│ **Laws of Thermodynamics**│

| Law       | Statement                                                                 | Key Concept             |

---

### **Important Notes**

- The **First Law** says energy is conserved, but doesn’t restrict direction.  
- The **Second Law** introduces the *arrow of time*—why processes go one way.  
- The **Third Law** implies that absolute zero is a limit we can approach but never reach.  
- The **Zeroth Law** seems obvious but is logically necessary for thermometry.
