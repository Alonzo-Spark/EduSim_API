### Key Concepts

The recoil of a gun is a direct consequence of Newton's Third Law of Motion:  "For every action, there is an equal and opposite reaction."

### Explanation

When a gun fires a bullet, a chemical reaction rapidly expands gases within the barrel.  This rapid expansion exerts a force *forward* on the bullet, propelling it out of the gun.

This forward force (the *action*) is perfectly balanced by an equal and opposite force that acts *backward* on the gun itself (the *reaction*). This backward force is what causes the gun to recoil.

The mass of the bullet is significantly smaller than the mass of the gun. Therefore, the acceleration of the bullet is much greater than the acceleration of the gun. This is why the gun moves backward slightly, while the bullet travels at high speed.

The magnitude of the recoil force depends on the mass of the bullet and the velocity of the bullet when it exits the gun.

### Formula

$$
F_{action} = F_{reaction}
$$

Where:

*   $$F_{action}$$ represents the force exerted on the bullet.
*   $$F_{reaction}$$ represents the force exerted on the gun.
