Let's explore the fascinating world of fluid mechanics, starting with a fundamental concept: **pressure**.

***

### Quick Definition

Pressure is a scalar physical quantity that describes the force exerted perpendicularly on a surface per unit area. It's a measure of how concentrated a force is over a given area.

***

### Main Formula

$$P = \frac{F}{A}$$

Where:
*   $P$ represents **pressure**
*   $F$ represents the magnitude of the **normal force**
*   $A$ represents the **area** over which the force is distributed

### SI Unit

The SI (International System of Units) unit for pressure is the **pascal (Pa)**.

From the formula $P = \frac{F}{A}$, we can derive the SI unit:
*   The SI unit for force ($F$) is the **newton (N)**.
*   The SI unit for area ($A$) is the **square meter (m²)**.

Therefore, 1 Pascal is equivalent to 1 Newton per square meter:
$$1 \text{ Pa} = 1 \frac{\text{N}}{\text{m}^2}$$

***

### Important Notes

*   **Scalar Quantity:** Pressure is a scalar quantity, meaning it has magnitude but no specific direction. At any point within a fluid, pressure acts equally in all directions.
*   **Direction of Force:** While pressure itself is scalar, the force exerted due to pressure is always perpendicular (normal) to the surface it acts upon.
*   **Fluids at Rest:** In a fluid at rest, pressure increases with depth due to the weight of the fluid above. This is a crucial concept in hydrostatics.
