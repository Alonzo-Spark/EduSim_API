### Derivation

Consider three resistors $ R_1 $, $ R_2 $, and $ R_3 $ connected in series with a battery of voltage $ V $.

Let the current flowing through the circuit be $ I $.

- Since the resistors are in series, the **same current $ I $** flows through each resistor.
- Let the voltage drops across $ R_1 $, $ R_2 $, and $ R_3 $ be $ V_1 $, $ V_2 $, and $ V_3 $ respectively.

Using **Ohm’s Law**:

$$
V_1 = I R_1,\quad V_2 = I R_2,\quad V_3 = I R_3
$$

The total voltage supplied by the battery is equal to the sum of the individual voltage drops:

$$
V = V_1 + V_2 + V_3
$$

Substitute using Ohm’s Law:

$$
V = I R_1 + I R_2 + I R_3
$$

Factor out $ I $:

$$
V = I (R_1 + R_2 + R_3)
$$

Now, if we replace the series combination with a **single equivalent resistor** $ R_{eq} $, then by Ohm’s Law:

$$
V = I R_{eq}
$$

Comparing both equations:

$$
I R_{eq} = I (R_1 + R_2 + R_3)
$$

Cancel $ I $ from both sides:

$$
R_{eq} = R_1 + R_2 + R_3
$$

---

### General Result

For $ n $ resistors in series:

$$
R_{eq} = R_1 + R_2 + R_3 + \cdots + R_n
$$

This shows that **resistances in series add up linearly**.

---

✅ **Summary**

The equivalent resistance of resistors in series is the sum of all individual resistances:

$$
R_{eq} = \sum_{i=1}^{n} R_i
$$

This principle is fundamental in circuit analysis and design.
