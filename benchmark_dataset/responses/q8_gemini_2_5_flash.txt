Here's a breakdown of how to find the range of a projectile, presented in an interactive textbook format.

***

### Quick Definition

The **range** of a projectile is the horizontal distance it travels from its launch point until it returns to the same horizontal level.

***

### Key Concepts

For projectile motion, we typically analyze the horizontal and vertical components of motion independently.

*   **Initial Velocity Components:**
    *   Horizontal component ($V_x$): $V_0 \cos(\theta)$
    *   Vertical component ($V_y$): $V_0 \sin(\theta)$
*   **Horizontal Motion:**
    *   Constant velocity (assuming no air resistance).
    *   Distance = Velocity $\times$ Time ($R = V_x \times T$)
*   **Vertical Motion:**
    *   Affected by gravity (constant downward acceleration, $g \approx 9.8 \text{ m/s}^2$).
    *   Symmetrical motion (time to reach maximum height equals time to fall back down).

***

### Main Formula

The range ($R$) of a projectile launched from a flat surface can be calculated using the following formula:

$$
R = \frac{V_0^2 \sin(2\theta)}{g}
$$

<details>
<summary>Explain Formula</summary>
This formula combines the horizontal velocity, the total time of flight, and the effects of gravity into a single expression for the range.
<ul>
    <li>$R$: The horizontal range of the projectile (in meters).</li>
    <li>$V_0$: The initial velocity of the projectile (in meters per second).</li>
    <li>$\theta$: The launch angle with respect to the horizontal (in degrees or radians, ensure your calculator is in the correct mode).</li>
    <li>$g$: The acceleration due to gravity (approximately $9.8 \text{ m/s}^2$).</li>
</ul>

### Problem Solution

Let's apply the formula to the given problem.

**Given:**
*   Initial velocity ($V_0$) = $20 \text{ m/s}$
*   Launch angle ($\theta$) = $45^\circ$
*   Acceleration due to gravity ($g$) = $9.8 \text{ m/s}^2$

**Calculation Steps:**

1.  **Substitute the values into the range formula:**
    $$
    R = \frac{(20 \text{ m/s})^2 \sin(2 \times 45^\circ)}{9.8 \text{ m/s}^2}
    $$

2.  **Calculate $2\theta$:**
    $$
    2 \times 45^\circ = 90^\circ
    $$

3.  **Find $\sin(90^\circ)$:**
    $$
    \sin(90^\circ) = 1
    $$

4.  **Calculate $V_0^2$:**
    $$
    (20 \text{ m/s})^2 = 400 \text{ m}^2/\text{s}^2
    $$

5.  **Substitute these values back into the formula:**
    $$
    R = \frac{400 \text{ m}^2/\text{s}^2 \times 1}{9.8 \text{ m/s}^2}
    $$

6.  **Calculate the final range:**
    $$
    R \approx 40.82 \text{ meters}
    $$

Therefore, the range of the ball is approximately **40.82 meters**.

***

### Important Notes

*   This formula for range assumes the projectile lands at the same height from which it was launched.
*   Air resistance is neglected in this calculation, which is a common simplification in introductory physics problems. In reality, air resistance would reduce the actual range.
*   The maximum range for a given initial velocity occurs at a launch angle of $45^\circ$.
