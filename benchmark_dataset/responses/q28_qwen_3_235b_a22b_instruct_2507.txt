### **Key Concept**

When an object is partially or fully submerged in a fluid (liquid or gas), it experiences an upward force called **buoyant force**. This force determines whether the object will float, sink, or remain suspended.

**Where:**  
- $ F_b $ = buoyant force (in newtons, N)  
- $ \rho_{\text{fluid}} $ = density of the fluid (kg/m³)  
- $ g $ = acceleration due to gravity (≈ 9.8 m/s²)  
- $ V_{\text{displaced}} $ = volume of fluid displaced (m³)  

---

### **How It Works**

1. When you place an object in water, it pushes water out of the way (displaces it).  
2. The water pushes back with an upward force equal to the weight of the displaced water.  
3. If the buoyant force ≥ object’s weight → it floats.  
4. If the buoyant force < object’s weight → it sinks.

---

### **Real-World Example: A Ship Floating on Water**

🌊 Consider a large steel ship:

- Steel is denser than water, so a solid block of steel would sink.  
- However, the ship is designed with a hollow shape, so it displaces a **large volume** of water.  
- The **weight of the displaced water** equals the **total weight of the ship**.  
- Therefore, the buoyant force balances the ship’s weight → it floats!

> ✅ The ship floats because it displaces enough water to match its own weight — even though it's made of heavy material.

---

### **Simple Experiment: The Crown and the Bathtub**

Legend says Archimedes discovered this principle while bathing. He noticed his body displaced water when he got into the tub. To test if a king’s crown was pure gold, he:

1. Submerged the crown in water and measured displaced volume.  
2. Compared it to an equal **weight** of pure gold.  
3. The crown displaced more water → less dense → not pure gold!

Thus, buoyancy can be used to measure density without damaging an object.

---

### **Applications of Archimedes’ Principle**

| Application         | How It Works |
|---------------------|--------------|
| **Submarines**       | Adjust buoyancy by filling or emptying ballast tanks with water |
| **Hot Air Balloons** | Rise because heated air is less dense than surrounding air |
| **Hydrometers**      | Measure liquid density based on how deeply they sink |
| **Life Jackets**     | Increase volume without much weight, displacing more water to stay afloat |

---

### **Important Notes**

⚠️ **Floating vs. Sinking is not about weight — it's about density and displacement.**  
A massive ship floats because its **average density** (including air inside) is less than water.

⚠️ **Buoyancy works in gases too!**  
Helium balloons rise in air because helium is less dense than air.

---

### **Summary**

- Buoyant force = weight of displaced fluid  
- Determines whether objects float or sink  
- Depends on volume displaced and fluid density  
- Applies to liquids and gases  
- Practical in engineering, navigation, and material testing
