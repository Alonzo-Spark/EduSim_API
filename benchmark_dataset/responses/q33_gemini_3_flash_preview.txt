:::section{title="Introduction"}
Heat transfer is the movement of thermal energy from a warmer object to a cooler one. This process occurs through three distinct physical mechanisms: **Conduction**, **Convection**, and **Radiation**. While they all aim to reach thermal equilibrium, they operate through different mediums and physical principles.
:::

:::section{title="Quick Definition"}
| Mechanism | Primary Medium | Physical Action |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Conduction** | Solids | Direct contact and molecular vibration. |
| **Convection** | Liquids & Gases | Bulk movement of fluid currents. |
| **Radiation** | Vacuum & Transparent Media | Electromagnetic waves (no medium required). |
:::

:::section{title="Key Concepts"}
### 1. Conduction (The Touch)
Energy is transferred via direct physical contact. Faster-moving (hotter) particles collide with slower-moving (cooler) neighbors, passing kinetic energy along the chain.
*   **Example:** A metal spoon getting hot in a cup of coffee.

### 2. Convection (The Flow)
Energy is transferred by the actual movement of heated particles. As a fluid heats up, it becomes less dense and rises, while cooler, denser fluid sinks, creating a "convection current."
*   **Example:** Boiling water in a pot or warm air rising in a room.

### 3. Radiation (The Wave)
Energy is transferred through electromagnetic waves (specifically infrared). This is the only form of heat transfer that can travel through the vacuum of space.
*   **Example:** Feeling the warmth of the sun or a campfire from a distance.
:::

:::formula-card{title="Fourier's Law of Heat Conduction"}
$$
q = -k \nabla T
$$

**Variables:**
*   $q$: Heat flux (amount of heat flowing per unit area)
*   $k$: Thermal conductivity of the material
*   $\nabla T$: Temperature gradient

:::section{title="Characteristics Comparison"}
| Feature | Conduction | Convection | Radiation |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Medium** | Necessary (best in solids) | Necessary (fluids only) | Not necessary (vacuum) |
| **Mechanism** | Molecular collision | Mass motion of molecules | Electromagnetic waves |
| **Speed** | Slowest | Medium | Speed of Light |
| **Direction** | From hot to cold | Primarily upwards | In all directions |
:::

:::formula-card{title="Stefan-Boltzmann Law (Radiation)"}
$$
P = e \sigma A T^4
$$

**Variables:**
*   $P$: Radiant power
*   $e$: Emissivity of the object
*   $\sigma$: Stefan-Boltzmann constant ($5.67 \times 10^{-8} W/m^2K^4$)
*   $A$: Surface area
*   $T$: Absolute temperature (Kelvin)

:::section{title="Important Notes"}
*   **Vacuum Insulation:** A thermos (vacuum flask) prevents heat loss by removing the medium required for conduction and convection, and using reflective linings to bounce radiation back.
*   **Thermal Conductivity:** Metals are excellent conductors because they have "free electrons" that help transport energy quickly.
*   **Fluid Dynamics:** Convection cannot happen in space (microgravity) effectively because there is no "up" or "down" for density differences to drive the flow.
:::
