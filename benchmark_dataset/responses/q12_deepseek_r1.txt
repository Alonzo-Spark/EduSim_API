#### 🧠 Core Definition
**Inertia** is the inherent property of all physical objects to resist changes in their state of motion. This fundamental concept originates from Newton's First Law of Motion:
> "An object remains at rest or in uniform motion unless acted upon by an external force."

**Key Insight**: Inertia is *not* a force itself, but rather a measure of an object's resistance to acceleration. It depends solely on **mass** – greater mass = greater inertia.

---

### 🔍 Three Types of Inertia

#### 1. **Inertia of Rest**
- **Definition**: Resistance to initiating motion from a stationary state.
- **Formula**: 
  $$
  \sum \vec{F} = 0 \implies \vec{v} = 0
  $$
- **Examples**:
  - Passengers jerk backward when a bus suddenly starts
  - Dust particles falling off a carpet when beaten
  - Difficulty pushing a heavy stationary crate

#### 2. **Inertia of Motion**
- **Definition**: Resistance to slowing down or stopping while in motion.
- **Formula**:
  $$
  \sum \vec{F} = 0 \implies \vec{v} = \text{constant}
  $$
- **Examples**:
  - Passengers lurch forward when brakes are applied
  - A running player can't instantly stop at a boundary line
  - Ice skaters coasting frictionlessly after a push

#### 3. **Inertia of Direction**
- **Definition**: Resistance to changing direction while moving.
- **Formula**:
  $$
  \Delta \theta = 0 \quad \text{(without external force)}
  $$
- **Examples**:
  - Mud flying tangentially off a spinning bicycle wheel
  - Passengers swaying sideways when a car turns sharply
  - Satellites maintaining orbital paths without thrusters

### ⚖️ Inertia vs. Mass Comparison
| Property        | Mass                            | Inertia                          |
|-----------------|---------------------------------|----------------------------------|
| **Definition**  | Quantity of matter              | Resistance to motion change      |
| **SI Unit**     | Kilogram (kg)                   | Kilogram (kg)                    |
| **Scalar/Vector**| Scalar                          | Scalar                           |
| **Dependence**  | Constant everywhere             | Proportional to mass             |
| **Measurement** | Using balances                  | Observed through force required  |

---

### 💡 Important Principles
1. **Quantifying Inertia**: Measured by mass (kg). Double the mass = double the inertia.
2. **Relativity Connection**: Einstein showed inertia remains consistent across inertial frames.
3. **Zero-G Myth**: Inertia persists in space – stopping a moving asteroid requires force despite weightlessness.
4. **Energy Aspect**: Overcoming inertia requires work (W = F × d), converting to kinetic energy.

> **Real-World Application**: Seatbelts counteract inertia during collisions by applying restraining forces over time (impulse).
