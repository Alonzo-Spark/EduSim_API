## Thermal Properties of Matter

### Quick Definition

The **thermal properties of matter** describe how materials respond to changes in temperature and how they store or transfer thermal energy. Key concepts include heat, temperature, and various measures of a substance's ability to absorb or release heat.

---

### Key Concepts

This section will define and differentiate two fundamental thermal properties: **heat capacity** and **specific heat capacity**. Understanding these concepts is crucial for analyzing energy transfer in various physical and chemical processes.

---

### Heat Capacity

#### Definition

**Heat capacity** ($C$) is a measure of the amount of heat energy required to change the temperature of an entire object or a specific quantity of a substance by a certain amount (usually 1 degree Celsius or 1 Kelvin). It is an **extensive property**, meaning it depends on the mass of the object.

#### Main Formula

$$
C = \frac{Q}{\Delta T}
$$

*   **$C$**: Heat Capacity (Units: Joules per Kelvin (J/K) or Joules per degree Celsius (J/°C))
*   **$Q$**: Amount of heat energy absorbed or released (Units: Joules (J))
*   **$\Delta T$**: Change in temperature (Units: Kelvin (K) or degrees Celsius (°C))

---

### Specific Heat Capacity

#### Definition

**Specific heat capacity** ($c$) is the amount of heat energy required to raise the temperature of a *unit mass* of a substance by a certain amount (usually 1 degree Celsius or 1 Kelvin). It is an **intensive property**, meaning it does not depend on the mass of the substance. It is a characteristic property of the material itself.

#### Main Formula

$$
c = \frac{Q}{m \Delta T}
$$

*   **$c$**: Specific Heat Capacity (Units: Joules per kilogram per Kelvin (J/kg·K) or Joules per kilogram per degree Celsius (J/kg·°C))
*   **$Q$**: Amount of heat energy absorbed or released (Units: Joules (J))
*   **$m$**: Mass of the substance (Units: kilograms (kg))
*   **$\Delta T$**: Change in temperature (Units: Kelvin (K) or degrees Celsius (°C))

---

### Relationship Between Heat Capacity and Specific Heat Capacity

Heat capacity and specific heat capacity are directly related. The heat capacity of an object can be calculated by multiplying its mass by the specific heat capacity of the material it is made from.

#### Main Formula

$$
C = m \cdot c
$$

*   **$C$**: Heat Capacity
*   **$m$**: Mass
*   **$c$**: Specific Heat Capacity

---

### Comparative Analysis

The table below highlights the key differences and similarities between heat capacity and specific heat capacity.

| Feature             | Heat Capacity ($C$)                                | Specific Heat Capacity ($c$)                             |
| :------------------ | :------------------------------------------------- | :------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Definition**      | Heat required to change temperature of an object.  | Heat required to change temperature of unit mass.        |
| **Property Type**   | Extensive (depends on mass)                        | Intensive (independent of mass)                          |
| **Units**           | J/K or J/°C                                        | J/kg·K or J/kg·°C                                        |
| **Dependence**      | Depends on mass and type of substance              | Depends only on the type of substance                    |
| **Examples**        | The heat capacity of *this 1 kg iron block* is... | The specific heat capacity of *iron* is...               |

---

### Important Notes

*   **Units:** While Kelvin and Celsius are used interchangeably for temperature *differences* ($\Delta T$), specific heat capacity values are usually given with Kelvin in the denominator in SI units.
*   **Phase Changes:** These definitions apply when a substance undergoes a temperature change without a phase change (e.g., solid to liquid, liquid to gas). During a phase change, heat is absorbed or released without a change in temperature, governed by latent heat.
*   **Constant Pressure vs. Constant Volume:** For gases, specific heat capacity can vary depending on whether the process occurs at constant pressure ($c_p$) or constant volume ($c_v$). For solids and liquids, this distinction is often negligible.

---

### Summary

*   **Heat capacity** is an extensive property describing how much heat an *entire object* absorbs for a given temperature change.
*   **Specific heat capacity** is an intensive property describing how much heat a *unit mass of a substance* absorbs for a given temperature change.
*   They are related by the mass of the substance ($C = m \cdot c$).
