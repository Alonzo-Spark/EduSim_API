<section data-type="introduction">
## Thermal Properties of Matter
Understanding how substances respond to heat energy is fundamental to thermodynamics. When energy is added to a system, the resulting temperature change depends entirely on the material's internal characteristics.
</section>

<section data-type="definition">
### 📘 Quick Definition: Heat Capacity ($C$)
**Heat Capacity** is the total amount of heat energy required to raise the temperature of a given object or system by one degree (either $1^\circ\text{C}$ or $1\text{ K}$). It is an **extrinsic property**, meaning it depends on the size and mass of the object.
</section>

<section data-type="formula">
### 🧮 Main Formula: Heat Capacity
$$
C = \frac{Q}{\Delta T}
$$
**Variables:**
*   $C$: Heat Capacity ($\text{J/K}$ or $\text{J/}^\circ\text{C}$)
*   $Q$: Heat energy added ($\text{J}$)
*   $\Delta T$: Change in temperature ($\text{K}$ or $^\circ\text{C}$)

[ **Formula Lab:** Used to calculate the energy needed for a specific object, like a copper pot or a car engine block, regardless of its material composition alone. ]
</section>

<section data-type="definition">
### 📘 Quick Definition: Specific Heat Capacity ($c$)
**Specific Heat Capacity** is the heat energy required to raise the temperature of **one unit mass** (usually $1\text{ kg}$) of a substance by one degree. It is an **intrinsic property**, meaning it is a constant for the material (e.g., water, gold, iron) regardless of how much you have.
</section>

<section data-type="formula">
### 🧮 Main Formula: Specific Heat Capacity
$$
Q = mc\Delta T
$$
**Variables:**
*   $Q$: Thermal energy ($\text{J}$)
*   $m$: Mass of the substance ($\text{kg}$)
*   $c$: Specific heat capacity ($\text{J/kg}\cdot\text{K}$)
*   $\Delta T$: Change in temperature ($\text{K}$)

[ **Formula Lab:** This is the primary equation for calorimetry. It allows us to predict temperature changes based on the material type ($c$) and the amount of material ($m$). ]
</section>

<section data-type="characteristics">
### 📊 Comparison Table
| Feature | Heat Capacity ($C$) | Specific Heat Capacity ($c$) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Mass Dependency** | Depends on mass (Extrinsic) | Independent of mass (Intrinsic) |
| **SI Unit** | $\text{J/K}$ | $\text{J/kg}\cdot\text{K}$ |
| **Focus** | The entire object | The material itself |
| **Relationship** | $C = mc$ | $c = C/m$ |
</section>

<section data-type="important-notes">
### ⚠️ Important Notes
*   **Water's High $c$:** Water has a very high specific heat capacity ($\approx 4184\text{ J/kg}\cdot\text{K}$), which is why it is used as a coolant and why coastal areas have moderate climates.
*   **Phase Changes:** These formulas only apply when the substance is **not** changing state. During melting or boiling, temperature remains constant despite heat addition (Latent Heat).
*   **Temperature Units:** Since $\Delta T$ is a difference, you can use Celsius or Kelvin interchangeably ($1^\circ\text{C}$ change $= 1\text{ K}$ change).
</section>
