### 📖 Interactive Lesson: Recoil of a Gun

┌──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│ **Introduction**                                                                 │
│                                                                  |
│ When a bullet is fired from a gun, the gun moves backward. This backward motion  │
│ is known as **recoil**. This phenomenon is a direct application of Newton's      │
│ Third Law of Motion and the Law of Conservation of Momentum.                     │
└──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘

┌──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│ **Key Concept: Newton's Third Law**                                              │
│                                                                                  │
│ "For every action, there is an equal and opposite reaction."                     │
│                                                                                  │
│ 1. **Action:** The gun exerts a forward force on the bullet to accelerate it.    │
│ 2. **Reaction:** The bullet exerts an equal and opposite force on the gun.       │
└──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘

┌──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│ **Main Formula: The Recoil Velocity**                                            │
│                                                                                  │
│ $$V_g = -\frac{m_b \cdot v_b}{M_g}$$                                             │
│                                                                                  │
│ **[ Explain Formula ]**                                                          │
│ This formula derives from the conservation of momentum. Since the total initial  │
│ momentum is zero, the final momentum of the bullet and gun must also sum to zero.│
│                                                                                  │
│ **[ Open Formula Lab ]**                                                         │
└──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘

┌──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│ **Variable Breakdown**                                                           │
│                                                                                  │
│ | Symbol | Meaning | Impact on Recoil |                                          │
│ | :--- | :--- | :--- |                                                           │
│ | $m_b$ | Mass of the bullet | Heavier bullets increase recoil. |                 │
│ | $v_b$ | Velocity of the bullet | Faster bullets increase recoil. |               │
│ | $M_g$ | Mass of the gun | Heavier guns decrease recoil velocity. |              │
│ | $-$ | Negative Sign | Indicates direction is opposite to the bullet. |          │
└──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘

┌──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│ **Characteristics of Recoil**                                                    │
│                                                                                  │
│ • **Force Equality:** The force on the bullet is exactly equal to the force on   │
│   the gun.                                                                       │
│ • **Acceleration Difference:** Because the gun has much more mass than the       │
│   bullet, its acceleration (recoil) is significantly smaller than the bullet's.  │
│ • **Momentum Balance:** The total momentum of the system remains constant (zero).│
└──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘

┌──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│ **Important Notes for Marks**                                                    │
│                                                                                  │
│ ⚠️ **Why doesn't the gun fly back as fast as the bullet?**                       │
│ According to $F = ma$, for the same force, acceleration is inversely             │
│ proportional to mass ($a = F/m$). The gun's large mass results in a much         │
│ lower acceleration compared to the tiny bullet.                                  │
└──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘
