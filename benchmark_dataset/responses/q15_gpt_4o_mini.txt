│ Introduction              │  
│                           │  
│ When a gun is fired, it recoils due to the action of the bullet being propelled forward. This phenomenon can be explained using Newton’s third law of motion.  
└───────────────────────────┘  

┌───────────────────────────┐  
│ Key Concepts              │  
│                           │  
│ - **Action and Reaction**: For every action, there is an equal and opposite reaction.  
│ - **Recoil**: The backward movement of the gun when a bullet is fired.  
└───────────────────────────┘  

┌───────────────────────────┐  
│ Explanation               │  
│                           │  
│ When the trigger of the gun is pulled, the gunpowder ignites, causing a rapid expansion of gases that propel the bullet forward. According to Newton's third law, the force exerted on the bullet in the forward direction results in an equal and opposite force acting on the gun, causing it to recoil backward.  
└───────────────────────────┘  

┌───────────────────────────┐  
│ Important Notes           │  
│                           │  
│ - The mass of the bullet and the gun plays a significant role in the extent of the recoil. A heavier gun will recoil less than a lighter one when firing the same bullet.  
│ - The speed at which the bullet is fired also impacts the recoil. A faster bullet will result in a stronger recoil.  
└───────────────────────────┘  

┌───────────────────────────┐  
│ Summary                  │  
│                           │  
│ The recoil of a gun after firing a bullet can be understood through Newton’s third law of motion, which states that every action has an equal and opposite reaction. As the bullet is propelled forward, the gun experiences an equal and opposite force that causes it to move backward.  
└───────────────────────────┘
