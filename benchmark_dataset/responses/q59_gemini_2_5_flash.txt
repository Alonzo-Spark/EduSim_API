## Collision of Two Equal Masses

This overlay provides an analysis of a collision event involving two objects of equal mass.

---

### Quick Definition

A **collision** is an isolated event in which two or more bodies exert forces on each other for a relatively short time. The forces involved are typically much greater than any external forces acting on the system, allowing us to consider momentum to be conserved.

---

### Key Concepts

*   **Momentum ($\vec{p}$):** A vector quantity defined as the product of an object's mass ($m$) and its velocity ($\vec{v}$).
    *   $\vec{p} = m\vec{v}$
*   **Conservation of Momentum:** In an isolated system (where no external forces act), the total momentum before a collision is equal to the total momentum after the collision.
    *   $\sum \vec{p}_{\text{before}} = \sum \vec{p}_{\text{after}}$
*   **Kinetic Energy (KE):** The energy an object possesses due to its motion.
    *   $KE = \frac{1}{2}mv^2$
*   **Types of Collisions:**
    *   **Elastic Collision:** Both momentum and kinetic energy are conserved. The objects bounce off each other without any loss of energy to heat, sound, or deformation.
    *   **Inelastic Collision:** Momentum is conserved, but kinetic energy is *not* conserved. Some kinetic energy is converted into other forms of energy (e.g., heat, sound, deformation).
    *   **Perfectly Inelastic Collision:** A type of inelastic collision where the objects stick together after the collision and move as a single unit.

---

### Main Formula

$$
m_1\vec{v}_{1,i} + m_2\vec{v}_{2,i} = m_1\vec{v}_{1,f} + m_2\vec{v}_{2,f}
$$

*   **Explain Formula:** This is the **Law of Conservation of Momentum**. It states that the total momentum of the system before the collision (left side) is equal to the total momentum of the system after the collision (right side).
    *   $m_1$: Mass of object 1
    *   $m_2$: Mass of object 2
    *   $\vec{v}_{1,i}$: Initial velocity of object 1
    *   $\vec{v}_{2,i}$: Initial velocity of object 2
    *   $\vec{v}_{1,f}$: Final velocity of object 1
    *   $\vec{v}_{2,f}$: Final velocity of object 2

### Characteristics of Collision with Equal Masses

When two objects of equal mass ($m_1 = m_2 = m$) collide, the conservation of momentum equation simplifies:

$$
m\vec{v}_{1,i} + m\vec{v}_{2,i} = m\vec{v}_{1,f} + m\vec{v}_{2,f}
$$

Dividing by $m$:

$$
\vec{v}_{1,i} + \vec{v}_{2,i} = \vec{v}_{1,f} + \vec{v}_{2,f}
$$

This simplified equation highlights a key characteristic: the *sum of the initial velocities equals the sum of the final velocities*.

### Important Notes

*   This analysis assumes a one-dimensional collision if vector quantities are not explicitly specified. For 2D or 3D collisions, vector components must be conserved independently.
*   The terms "elastic" and "inelastic" refer to the conservation of *kinetic energy*, not total energy. Total energy is always conserved in an isolated system.
*   In real-world scenarios, perfectly elastic collisions are rare; some energy is always lost to sound, heat, or deformation.

---

### Summary

For a collision between two equal masses:

1.  **Momentum is always conserved.**
    *   $m\vec{v}_{1,i} + m\vec{v}_{2,i} = m\vec{v}_{1,f} + m\vec{v}_{2,f}$
    *   $\vec{v}_{1,i} + \vec{v}_{2,i} = \vec{v}_{1,f} + \vec{v}_{2,f}$
2.  **Kinetic energy conservation depends on the type of collision:**
    *   **Elastic:** Kinetic energy is conserved.
    *   **Inelastic:** Kinetic energy is NOT conserved.
3.  **Special Case - Elastic Collision with one mass initially at rest:** If $\vec{v}_{2,i} = 0$, then $\vec{v}_{1,f} = 0$ and $\vec{v}_{2,f} = \vec{v}_{1,i}$. The first mass stops, and the second mass takes on the initial velocity of the first. This is commonly observed in billiards.
