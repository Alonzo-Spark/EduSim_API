**Introduction**

Welcome to the exploration of acceleration due to gravity! This fundamental concept explains why objects fall towards the Earth (or any celestial body). We'll derive the formula that governs this phenomenon.

**Key Concepts**

Before we begin, let's review the key concepts:

*   **Newton's Law of Universal Gravitation:**  States that every particle of matter in the universe attracts every other particle with a force directly proportional to the product of their masses and inversely proportional to the square of the distance between their centers.
*   **Mass (m):** A measure of the amount of matter in an object.
*   **Gravitational Constant (G):** A fundamental physical constant that determines the strength of the gravitational force.  G ≈ 6.674 × 10⁻¹¹ N⋅m²/kg².
*   **Distance (r):** The distance between the centers of the two masses.
*   **Force (F):** A push or pull that can cause a change in an object's motion.
*   **Acceleration (a):** The rate of change of velocity over time.
*   **Mass of Earth (M):** The mass of the Earth.
*   **Radius of Earth (R):** The radius of the Earth.

**Derivation**

Let's derive the formula for acceleration due to gravity (g).

1.  **Newton's Law of Universal Gravitation:**  We start with the fundamental law:

    $$F = G\frac{Mm}{r^2}$$

    Where:

    *   *F* is the gravitational force between two objects.
    *   *G* is the gravitational constant.
    *   *M* is the mass of the Earth.
    *   *m* is the mass of the object.
    *   *r* is the distance between the center of the Earth and the center of the object.  For an object at or near the Earth's surface, this is approximately the Earth's radius (R).

2.  **Newton's Second Law of Motion:**  Force is also equal to mass times acceleration:

    $$F = ma$$

    Where:

    *   *F* is the force acting on the object.
    *   *m* is the mass of the object.
    *   *a* is the acceleration of the object. In this case, it's the acceleration due to gravity (g).

3.  **Equating the Forces:** Now, we can equate the two expressions for force:

    $$ma = G\frac{Mm}{r^2}$$

4.  **Isolating Acceleration (g):**  Notice that the mass *m* of the object appears on both sides of the equation. We can divide both sides by *m*:

    $$a = G\frac{M}{r^2}$$

    Since we're specifically interested in the acceleration due to gravity (g), we can replace *a* with *g*:

    $$g = G\frac{M}{r^2}$$

    If the object is on the surface of the Earth, *r* is the Earth's radius, *R*:

    $$g = G\frac{M}{R^2}$$

**Explanation of the Formula**

*   **g = GM/r²**:  The acceleration due to gravity (*g*) is directly proportional to the gravitational constant (*G*) and the mass of the Earth (*M*), and inversely proportional to the square of the distance (*r*) from the center of the Earth.

*   **G:** The gravitational constant, a fundamental constant.
*   **M:** The mass of the Earth. A larger mass means a stronger gravitational pull.
*   **r:** The distance from the center of the Earth. As you move further away from the Earth, the acceleration due to gravity decreases because the gravitational force weakens with distance.  At the Earth's surface, *r* is the Earth's radius.

**Important Notes**

*   The formula assumes that the mass of the object is negligible compared to the mass of the Earth.  This is a reasonable assumption for most objects near the Earth's surface.
*   Air resistance is not considered in this derivation. In reality, air resistance can affect the acceleration of falling objects.
*   The value of *g* is approximately 9.8 m/s² at the Earth's surface. This value varies slightly depending on location due to factors like altitude and the Earth's non-spherical shape.

**Summary**

We have successfully derived the expression for acceleration due to gravity, a crucial concept in understanding how objects behave under the influence of the Earth's gravitational field. The formula highlights the relationships between gravitational force, mass, and distance.
