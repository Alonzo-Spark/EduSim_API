To derive the expression for the electric force between two charges, we use **Coulomb's Law**, which describes the electrostatic interaction between point charges. Here's the structured breakdown:

---

### **Coulomb's Law Formula**
$$
\boxed{F = k \frac{q_1 q_2}{r^2}}
$$
**Where**:
- \( F \): Magnitude of the electric force (in newtons, N)
- \( q_1, q_2 \): Charges of the two point charges (in coulombs, C \))
- \( r \): Distance between the charges (in meters, m)
- \( k \): Coulomb's constant (\( 8.988 \times 10^9 \, \text{N·m}^2/\text{C}^2 \))

---

### **Key Steps in Derivation**

1. **Proportionality to Charges**  
   The force is directly proportional to the product of the charges (\( q_1 q_2 \)):  
   $$ F \propto q_1 q_2 $$

2. **Inverse Square Law**  
   The force decreases with the square of the distance (\( r \)) between the charges:  
   $$ F \propto \frac{1}{r^2} $$

3. **Coulomb's Constant (\( k \))**  
   Combines the above relationships and introduces a proportionality constant:  
   $$ F = k \frac{q_1 q_2}{r^2} $$  
   Here, \( k = \frac{1}{4\pi \varepsilon_0} \), where \( \varepsilon_0 \) is the permittivity of free space (\( 8.854 \times 10^{-12} \, \text{C}^2/\text{N·m}^2 \)).

---

### **Vector Form & Direction**
The force acts along the line joining the two charges. For charges \( q_1 \) and \( q_2 \):  
- **Like charges** (\( q_1 q_2 > 0 \)): Repulsive force.  
- **Opposite charges** (\( q_1 q_2 < 0 \)): Attractive force.  

**Vector equation**:  
$$ \vec{F}_{12} = k \frac{q_1 q_2}{r^2} \hat{r}_{12} $$  
where \( \hat{r}_{12} \) is the unit vector from \( q_1 \) to \( q_2 \).

---

### **Assumptions**
1. **Point charges**: Sizes of charges are negligible compared to \( r \).
2. **Static charges**: Charges are not moving.
3. **Vacuum**: Formula assumes no external medium (use \( \varepsilon_0 \) for vacuum).

---

### **Example**
If \( q_1 = 2 \, \text{C} \), \( q_2 = -3 \, \text{C} \), and \( r = 1 \, \text{m} \):  
$$ F = (8.99 \times 10^9) \frac{(2)(-3)}{1^2} = -5.39 \times 10^{10} \, \text{N} $$  
The negative sign indicates **attraction**.

---

### **Applications**
- Calculating forces in atomic nuclei.
- Designing capacitors and circuits.
- Understanding molecular bonding.
