```html
<div class="section-card">
  <div class="section-title">Derivation</div>
  <div class="section-content">
    <p>Let's derive the formula for the maximum height.</p>

    <p>1.  <b>Vertical Motion:</b> Consider the vertical motion of the projectile. The initial vertical velocity is given by:</p>
    $$
    v_{0y} = v_0 \sin(\theta)
    $$

    <p>2.  <b>At Maximum Height:</b> At the maximum height, the vertical velocity of the projectile is momentarily zero (v<sub>y</sub> = 0).</p>

    <p>3.  <b>Using Kinematic Equation:</b> We can use the following kinematic equation to relate initial velocity, final velocity, acceleration, and displacement:</p>
    $$
    v_y^2 = v_{0y}^2 + 2 a \Delta y
    $$

    <p>where:</p>
    <ul>
      <li>v<sub>y</sub> = final vertical velocity (0 m/s at max height)</li>
      <li>v<sub>0y</sub> = initial vertical velocity (v₀sin(θ))</li>
      <li>a = acceleration due to gravity (-g)</li>
      <li>Δy = change in vertical position (h<sub>max</sub>)</li>
    </ul>

    <p>4.  <b>Substituting Values:</b> Substitute the known values into the kinematic equation:</p>
    $$
    0^2 = (v_0 \sin(\theta))^2 + 2(-g) h_{max}
    $$

    <p>5.  <b>Solving for h<sub>max</sub>:</b> Rearrange the equation to solve for h<sub>max</sub>:</p>
    $$
    0 = v_0^2 \sin^2(\theta) - 2g h_{max}
    $$
    $$
    2g h_{max} = v_0^2 \sin^2(\theta)
    $$
    $$
    h_{max} = \frac{v_0^2 \sin^2(\theta)}{2g}
    $$
  </div>
</div>
```
