To derive the expression for the time period of a simple pendulum, we analyze the motion of a pendulum bob under the influence of gravity using **simple harmonic motion (SHM)** principles. Here's the step-by-step derivation:

---

### **Step 1: Define the System**
- A simple pendulum consists of a **point mass (bob)** suspended by a **massless, inextensible string** of length \(L\).
- The bob is displaced by a small angle \(\theta\) from its equilibrium position.
- Forces acting on the bob:
  - **Gravity**: \(mg\) (downward),
  - **Tension**: \(T\) (along the string).

---

### **Step 2: Identify the Restoring Force**
- The **restoring force** \(F\) is the tangential component of gravity:
  \[
  F = -mg \sin\theta
  \]
  The negative sign indicates that \(F\) opposes the displacement.

---

### **Step 3: Apply Small-Angle Approximation**
- For small \(\theta\) (typically \(\theta < 10^\circ\)):
  \[
  \sin\theta \approx \theta
  \]
  Thus:
  \[
  F = -mg\theta
  \]

---

### **Step 4: Relate \(\theta\) to Displacement**
- The arc length displacement \(s\) is:
  \[
  s = L\theta \implies \theta = \frac{s}{L}
  \]
- Substitute into the restoring force:
  \[
  F = -mg \left(\frac{s}{L}\right) = -\left(\frac{mg}{L}\right)s
  \]

---

### **Step 5: Apply Newton's Second Law**
\[
F = ma \implies m \frac{d^2s}{dt^2} = -\left(\frac{mg}{L}\right)s
\]
- Simplify by canceling \(m\):
  \[
  \frac{d^2s}{dt^2} = -\left(\frac{g}{L}\right)s
  \]

---

### **Step 6: Recognize the SHM Equation**
- The equation \(\frac{d^2s}{dt^2} = -\omega^2 s\) defines SHM, where \(\omega\) is the **angular frequency**.
- Compare to Step 5:
  \[
  \omega^2 = \frac{g}{L} \implies \omega = \sqrt{\frac{g}{L}}
  \]

---

### **Step 7: Derive the Time Period**
- The time period \(T\) is the time for one complete oscillation:
  \[
  T = \frac{2\pi}{\omega} = 2\pi \sqrt{\frac{L}{g}}
  \]

---

### **Final Formula**
\[
\boxed{T = 2\pi \sqrt{\frac{L}{g}}}
\]

---

### **Key Notes**
1. **Small-Angle Assumption**:  
   The derivation assumes \(\theta\) is small (\(\sin\theta \approx \theta\)). For larger \(\theta\), the period increases slightly.
2. **Independence of Mass**:  
   \(T\) depends only on \(L\) and \(g\), **not** on the bob's mass or amplitude (for small \(\theta\)).
3. **Practical Applications**:  
   - Measuring \(g\) using \(T\) and \(L\).  
   - Designing pendulum clocks.

---

### **Formula Lab**
Explore the relationship between \(T\), \(L\), and \(g\):
- If \(L\) **doubles**, \(T\) increases by \(\sqrt{2} \approx 1.414\).
- On the Moon (\(g_{\text{moon}} \approx \frac{g}{6}\)), \(T\) increases by \(\sqrt{6} \approx 2.45\).

---

**Summary**: The time period of a simple pendulum is governed by gravity and string length, following \(T = 2\pi \sqrt{L/g}\). This result highlights the elegance of SHM in idealized mechanical systems.
