Let's solve this step by step using Newton's Second Law of Motion.

---

### 📘 Quick Definition  
**Newton's Second Law of Motion**:  
The acceleration of an object is directly proportional to the net force acting on it and inversely proportional to its mass.  
$$
\vec{F} = m \vec{a}
$$

---

### 🔢 Formula Card

**Main Formula**

$$
a = \frac{F}{m}
$$

- $ a $ = acceleration (in m/s²)  
- $ F $ = force applied (in newtons, N)  
- $ m $ = mass of the object (in kilograms, kg)

---

### ✏️ Given:
- Force, $ F = 50 \, \text{N} $
- Mass, $ m = 10 \, \text{kg} $

We need to find acceleration $ a $.

Using the formula:

$$
a = \frac{F}{m} = \frac{50}{10} = 5 \, \text{m/s}^2
$$

---

### ✅ Final Answer:  
$$
\boxed{5} \text{m/s}^2
$$

The body accelerates at $ 5 \, \text{m/s}^2 $ when a 50 N force acts on it.

---

### 💡 Key Concepts
- Larger force → greater acceleration  
- Larger mass → smaller acceleration for the same force  
- Units must be in SI (N, kg, m/s²) for correct results
