:::section{title="Quick Definition"}
**Orbital Motion** occurs when an object's forward velocity is perfectly balanced by the gravitational pull of a central mass, resulting in a continuous, stable falling path known as a **stable loop**.
:::

:::section{title="Main Formula"}
$$
v_c = \sqrt{\frac{GM}{r}}
$$
**Title:** Circular Orbital Velocity  
**Variables:**  
*   $v_c$: Velocity required to maintain orbit ($m/s$)
*   $G$: Gravitational Constant ($6.674 \times 10^{-11} \, m^3kg^{-1}s^{-2}$)
*   $M$: Mass of the central body ($kg$)
*   $r$: Radius from the center of mass ($m$)

[ **Explain Formula**: This formula determines the exact speed required for an object to stay in a circular orbit at a specific distance. If velocity decreases, the orbit decays; if it increases, the orbit becomes elliptical. ]

:::

:::section{title="Key Concepts"}
*   **Centripetal Force:** The "center-seeking" force provided by gravity that keeps the satellite from flying off in a straight line.
*   **Freefall State:** An orbiting body is technically in a state of perpetual freefall toward the center, but its tangential velocity causes it to miss the surface constantly.
*   **Orbital Period:** The time taken for one full revolution, determined by the altitude and mass of the primary body.
:::

:::section{title="The Period-Radius Relationship"}
$$
T = 2\pi \sqrt{\frac{r^3}{GM}}
$$
**Title:** Kepler's Third Law (Period)  
**Variables:**  
*   $T$: Orbital Period (seconds)
*   $r$: Semi-major axis/Radius
*   $GM$: Standard gravitational parameter

[ **Explain Formula**: This shows that the time it takes to complete an orbit is proportional to the cube of the distance from the center. Higher orbits move slower. ]

:::section{title="Important Notes"}
> [!WARNING]
> **Atmospheric Drag:** In Low Earth Orbit (LEO), trace gases create friction. Without periodic "re-boosts," the `orbit_started` state will eventually transition to an `orbital_decay` event.
