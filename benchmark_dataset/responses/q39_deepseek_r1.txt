### Wave Properties: Wavelength, Frequency, and Amplitude

Here's a structured breakdown of these fundamental wave characteristics:

---

#### 📏 **Wavelength (λ)**  
**Definition:**  
The distance between two consecutive **identical points** on a wave (e.g., crest-to-crest or trough-to-trough).  
**Key Details:**  
- Measured in **meters (m)**.  
- Represents the **spatial period** of the wave.  
- Determines wave color in light (e.g., red = long λ, blue = short λ).  
**Visual:**  
```plaintext
    λ          λ
~~~⭱~~~⭱~~~⭱~~~  (Crests marked with ⭱)
```

---

#### 🔁 **Frequency (f)**  
**Definition:**  
The number of **complete wave cycles** passing a fixed point per second.  
**Key Details:**  
- Measured in **Hertz (Hz)** (1 Hz = 1 cycle/second).  
- Represents the **temporal period** of the wave.  
- Determines pitch in sound (e.g., high f = high pitch).  
**Formula:**  
$$ f = \frac{1}{T} $$  
where \(T\) = time period (seconds per cycle).

---

#### 📈 **Amplitude (A)**  
**Definition:**  
The **maximum displacement** of a wave particle from its equilibrium (rest) position.  
**Key Details:**  
- Measured in **meters (m)** for mechanical waves.  
- Directly relates to wave **energy** (\(E \propto A^2\)).  
- Determines loudness in sound or brightness in light.  
**Visual:**  
```plaintext
Equilibrium ────────────────
           ▲     ▲
           |A    |A
           ▼     ▼
```

---

### Relationship Between Wavelength and Frequency  
**Core Formula:**  
$$ v = f \lambda $$  
where:  
- \(v\) = wave speed (m/s),  
- \(f\) = frequency (Hz),  
- \(\lambda\) = wavelength (m).  

**Key Implications:**  
1. **Inverse Relationship:** For a fixed speed \(v\):  
   - Higher \(f\) → Shorter \(\lambda\)  
   - Lower \(f\) → Longer \(\lambda\)  
   *(Example: Radio waves have long λ/low f; gamma rays have short λ/high f)*  

2. **Medium Dependence:**  
   - \(v\) is constant for a given medium (e.g., sound in air ≈ 343 m/s).  
   - Changing medium alters \(v\), affecting λ and \(f\) proportionally.

#### 🧪 Formula Lab: Wave Equation  
Experiment with the wave equation:  
$$ v = f \lambda $$  

| Variable | Role | Example Values |
|----------|------|----------------|
| \(v\)    | Wave speed | Light: \(3 \times 10^8\) m/s<br>Sound (air): 343 m/s |
| \(f\)    | Frequency | Human hearing: 20 Hz – 20 kHz |
| \(\lambda\) | Wavelength | Visible light: 400–700 nm |

**Try it:**  
If \(v = 340  \text{m/s}\) (sound) and \(f = 1000  \text{Hz}\), then:  
$$ \lambda = \frac{v}{f} = \frac{340}{1000} = 0.34  \text{m} $$

---

### 💎 Summary  
| Property | Symbol | Unit | Physical Meaning |
|----------|--------|------|------------------|
| **Wavelength** | \(\lambda\) | meters (m) | Distance between identical points |
| **Frequency** | \(f\) | Hertz (Hz) | Cycles per second |
| **Amplitude** | \(A\) | meters (m) | Maximum displacement from rest |

**Energy Connection:**  
- Amplitude \(A\) → Controls **intensity** (\(I \propto A^2\)).  
- Frequency \(f\) → Controls **energy per photon** (for light: \(E = hf\)).
