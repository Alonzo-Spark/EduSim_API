    <div class="card-title">Introduction</div>
    <p>Waves are disturbances that transfer energy through a medium or space. They are broadly classified into two main types based on the direction of oscillation relative to the direction of energy transfer: transverse and longitudinal waves.</p>
  </div>

  <div class="card">
    <div class="card-title">Definition</div>
    <p><b>Transverse Waves:</b> These are waves in which the displacement of the medium is perpendicular to the direction of wave propagation.  Think of it like a "side-to-side" movement.</p>
    <p><b>Longitudinal Waves:</b> In these waves, the displacement of the medium is parallel to the direction of wave propagation.  Think of it like a "back-and-forth" movement.</p>
  </div>

  <div class="card">
    <div class="card-title">Key Concepts</div>
    <p>Understanding the difference hinges on how the particles of the medium move as the wave passes through.  Key concepts include:</p>
    <ul>
      <li><b>Crest:</b> The highest point of a transverse wave.</li>
      <li><b>Trough:</b> The lowest point of a transverse wave.</li>
      <li><b>Compression:</b> A region in a longitudinal wave where the particles are closest together.</li>
      <li><b>Rarefaction:</b> A region in a longitudinal wave where the particles are furthest apart.</li>
    </ul>

    <p><b>Longitudinal Waves:</b> Crucial for understanding sound and how we hear. Used in medical imaging (ultrasound). Seismic P-waves, which are longitudinal, also provide information about the Earth's interior.</p>
