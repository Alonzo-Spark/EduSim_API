A car starting from rest and accelerating uniformly at 2 m/s² will have its velocity calculated as follows:

### Solution
We use the kinematic equation for velocity under uniform acceleration:  
**\( v = u + at \)**  
where:  
- \( v \) = final velocity (unknown)  
- \( u \) = initial velocity = 0 m/s (starts from rest)  
- \( a \) = acceleration = 2 m/s²  
- \( t \) = time = 10 s  

Substitute the values:  
\[ v = 0 + (2  \text{m/s}^2) \times (10  \text{s}) \]  
\[ v = 20  \text{m/s} \]  

### Final Answer  
The velocity after 10 seconds is **20 m/s**.  

---

### Key Concept  
**Uniform Acceleration**  
When an object accelerates at a constant rate, its velocity increases linearly with time. The formula \( v = u + at \) directly relates initial velocity, acceleration, time, and final velocity.  

### Explanation  
- **Initial velocity (\( u \))**: The starting speed (0 m/s here).  
- **Acceleration (\( a \))**: The rate of change of velocity (2 m/s² means velocity increases by 2 m/s every second).  
- **Time (\( t \))**: Duration of acceleration (10 s).  
- **Result**: After 10 s, the velocity increases by \( 2  \text{m/s} \times 10 = 20  \text{m/s} \).
