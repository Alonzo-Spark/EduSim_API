### 🧭 Introduction

In this sandbox, we explore how **gravity preset scaling**—a simulation parameter that adjusts the effective strength of gravitational acceleration—affects the **range** (horizontal distance traveled) of a block sliding down an inclined plane and launching off its end.

This scenario combines **inclined plane motion**, **projectile launch**, and **scaled gravity** to study kinematic sensitivity.

---

### 📐 Key Concepts

┌───────────────────────────┐
│ Quick Definition          │
│                           │
│ **Gravity Preset Scaling**│
│ adjusts the effective     │
│ value of gravitational    │
│ acceleration (g) in a     │
│ simulation or model.      │
│ e.g., Scale = 0.5 → g =   │
│ 4.9 m/s² instead of 9.8.  │
└───────────────────────────┘

┌───────────────────────────┐
│ Key Variables             │
│                           │
│ - **θ**: Incline angle    │
│ - **L**: Length of ramp   │
│ - **h**: Height at base   │
│ - **g' = s·g**: Scaled g  │
│    (s = scale factor)     │
│ - **Range**: Horizontal   │
│   distance after launch   │
└───────────────────────────┘

---

### 🧮 Formula

$$
g' = s \cdot g
$$

$$
v = \sqrt{2 g' L \sin\theta}
$$

$$
\text{Range} = v \cos\theta \cdot t_{\text{flight}}, \quad t_{\text{flight}} = \frac{v \sin\theta + \sqrt{(v \sin\theta)^2 + 2g'h}}{g'}
$$

┌───────────────────────────┐
│ Main Formula              │
│                           │
│ $$                        │
│ \text{Range} \propto     │
│ \sqrt{s}                  │
$$                        │

> 💡 The final range scales approximately with **√s**, since velocity depends on √(g') and flight time also scales with 1/√(g') — but combined, the dominant effect is **√s**.

### 📊 Behavior Summary (Table)

| Gravity Scale (s) | Effective g (m/s²) | Exit Speed | Flight Time | Range (approx) |
|-------------------|--------------------|----------|-------------|----------------|
| 0.25              | 2.45               | Low      | Long        | Medium         |
| 0.5               | 4.9                | Medium   | Medium      | ↑ Increasing   |
| 1.0 (normal)      | 9.8                | High     | Shorter     | Max?           |
| 2.0               | 19.6               | Very High| Very Short  | ↓ Decreasing   |

> 📌 **Surprise Insight**: Maximum range may occur at **intermediate gravity scaling**, depending on ramp height and angle.

---

### 🔍 Characteristics

- 🔹 Lower **g-scale** → slower acceleration → lower launch speed
- 🔹 But lower **g** → longer air time → more horizontal travel
- 🔹 Trade-off creates **non-linear range curve**
- 🔹 Optimal **s** exists when horizontal velocity and flight time are balanced

---

### 🧪 Applications

- Video game physics tuning
- Robotics ramp design in low-gravity environments
- Educational simulations (Moon vs Earth ramps)
- Space mission lander testing (e.g., Mars gravity = ~0.38g)

---

### ⚠️ Important Notes

- Assumes **no friction** on ramp
- Assumes **instant transition** from ramp to free flight
- Air resistance neglected
- Launch point height **h > 0** required for asymmetric flight

---

### 📘 Summary

- Gravity scaling **nonlinearly affects** range
- Range ∝ √s roughly, but **not monotonic**
- There exists an **optimal gravity level** for maximum range
- Simulations must balance **exit velocity** and **flight duration**
