### Introduction

Surface tension is a fundamental concept in fluid physics and chemistry. It arises due to the imbalance of intermolecular forces at the surface of a liquid compared to those in the bulk. Molecules within the liquid experience equal attraction from all sides, but surface molecules are pulled only inward and sideways, creating a net inward force. This causes the liquid surface to behave like a stretched elastic membrane.

This phenomenon explains everyday observations such as water droplets forming spherical shapes, insects walking on water, and the rise of liquid in narrow capillary tubes.

### Formula

$$
\gamma = \frac{F}{L}
$$

┌───────────────────────────┐  
│ Main Formula              │  
│                           │  
│     $$ \gamma = \frac{F}{L} $$      │  
│                           │  
│ [ Explain Formula ]       │  
│ [ Open Formula Lab ]      │  
└───────────────────────────┘  

- **$\gamma$**: surface tension (N/m)  
- **$F$**: force acting along the surface (N)  
- **$L$**: length over which the force acts (m)  

This formula defines surface tension as the force per unit length acting perpendicular to an imaginary line drawn on the surface.

---

### Characteristics of Surface Tension

- Acts **parallel to the surface** and perpendicular to any line drawn on it.  
- Always tries to **minimize surface area** (e.g., spherical droplets).  
- Measured in **newtons per meter (N/m)** or **joules per square meter (J/m²)**.  
- Stronger in liquids with **strong intermolecular forces**, especially hydrogen bonding.

---

### Factors Affecting Surface Tension

| Factor | Effect on Surface Tension | Explanation |
|-------|----------------------------|-----------|
| **Temperature** | Decreases with increasing temperature | Higher thermal energy reduces intermolecular cohesion. |
| **Impurities** (e.g., soap, detergent) | Decrease surface tension | Disrupt hydrogen bonding; surfactants align at surface and reduce net inward pull. |
| **Dissolved Substances** | Varies: <br>• **Inorganic salts** → Increase <br>• **Organic compounds** → Decrease | Ions increase cohesion; organic molecules act as surfactants. |
| **Nature of Liquid** | Water > Alcohol > Ether | Depends on strength of intermolecular forces (H-bonding > dipole-dipole > van der Waals). |
| **Adjacent Phase** | Slightly affected by vapor or solid contact | Presence of different media alters net molecular attraction. |

### Summary

Surface tension is a result of **molecular cohesion** and **surface energy minimization**. It is influenced by:

- Temperature  
- Impurities and solutes  
- Molecular structure of the liquid  

Understanding surface tension helps explain natural phenomena and is vital in fields like material science, medicine, and engineering.
