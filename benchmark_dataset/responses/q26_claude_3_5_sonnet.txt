# Pressure — Interactive Concept Card

---

## Quick Definition

**Pressure** is defined as the **force acting perpendicularly on a unit area** of a surface.

It measures how concentrated a force is over a given area.

---

## Formula Card

$$P = \frac{F}{A}$$

| Symbol | Quantity | Unit |
|--------|----------|------|
| $P$ | Pressure | Pascal (Pa) |
| $F$ | Normal Force | Newton (N) |
| $A$ | Area | m² |

---

## SI Unit

$$1 \ \text{Pascal} = 1 \ \frac{\text{N}}{\text{m}^2}$$

> **Named after:** Blaise Pascal (French mathematician & physicist)
>
> **Symbol:** Pa

---

## Key Characteristics

**① Scalar Quantity**
Pressure has magnitude only — no direction.

**② Force must be perpendicular**
Only the component of force *normal* to the surface contributes to pressure.

**③ Area inversely affects pressure**
Smaller area → Greater pressure (for same force)

---

## Intuitive Example

| Situation | Force | Area | Pressure |
|-----------|-------|------|----------|
| Nail tip | Same | Very small | **Very high** |
| Flat head | Same | Large | **Low** |

> This is why a sharp nail pierces wood easily — same force, tiny area = enormous pressure.

---

## Other Common Units

| Unit | Relation to Pa |
|------|---------------|
| **Bar** | $1 \ \text{bar} = 10^5 \ \text{Pa}$ |
| **Atmosphere (atm)** | $1 \ \text{atm} = 1.013 \times 10^5 \ \text{Pa}$ |
| **mm of Hg (Torr)** | $1 \ \text{atm} = 760 \ \text{mm Hg}$ |

---

## 💡 Important Note

> Pressure is a **scalar**, but the *force causing it* is a vector.
> In fluids, pressure acts **equally in all directions** at a point — this is **Pascal's Law**.
