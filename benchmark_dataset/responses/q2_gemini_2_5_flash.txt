## Quick Definition

**Uniform acceleration** means the velocity of an object changes by the same amount in every equal time interval.

## Key Concepts

To solve problems involving uniform acceleration, we use a set of equations known as the **equations of motion** or **kinematic equations**. These equations relate initial velocity ($u$), final velocity ($v$), acceleration ($a$), time ($t$), and displacement ($s$).

## Given Information

Let's identify the information provided in the problem:

*   **Initial velocity ($u$):** The car starts from rest, so $u = 0 \text{ m/s}$.
*   **Acceleration ($a$):** The car accelerates uniformly at $a = 2 \text{ m/s}^2$.
*   **Time ($t$):** We want to find the velocity after $t = 10 \text{ s}$.

## Goal

We need to find the **final velocity ($v$)** of the car.

## Main Formula

To find the final velocity when initial velocity, acceleration, and time are known, we use the first equation of motion:

$$ v = u + at $$

*   $v$: Final velocity (m/s)
*   $u$: Initial velocity (m/s)
*   $a$: Acceleration (m/s²)
*   $t$: Time (s)

## Step-by-Step Solution

1.  **Identify the knowns and unknown:**
    *   $u = 0 \text{ m/s}$
    *   $a = 2 \text{ m/s}^2$
    *   $t = 10 \text{ s}$
    *   $v = ?$

2.  **Substitute the values into the formula:**
    $$ v = 0 \text{ m/s} + (2 \text{ m/s}^2)(10 \text{ s}) $$

3.  **Calculate the result:**
    $$ v = 0 \text{ m/s} + 20 \text{ m/s} $$
    $$ v = 20 \text{ m/s} $$

## Answer

The velocity of the car after 10 seconds is $20 \text{ m/s}$.
