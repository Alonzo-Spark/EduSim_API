        <Title>Friction Coefficient ($\mu$)</Title>
        <Content>
            The **coefficient of friction ($\mu$)** is a dimensionless scalar quantity that describes the ratio of the force of friction between two bodies and the force pressing them together. A higher coefficient of friction indicates greater resistance to motion.

    <Section type="Key Concepts">
        <Title>Understanding Friction</Title>
        <Content>
            When a block slides on a surface, two primary types of friction might be involved:
            <ul>
                <li>
                    <strong>Static Friction ($\text{f}_\text{s}$):</strong> This force opposes the *initiation* of motion. It acts when an object is at rest relative to the surface it's on.
                </li>
                <li>
                    <strong>Kinetic Friction ($\text{f}_\text{k}$):</strong> This force opposes the *ongoing* motion of an object already sliding. It acts when there is relative motion between the surfaces.
                </li>
            </ul>
            In the context of a "sliding block," we are primarily concerned with kinetic friction.
        </Content>
