Welcome to the **Collision Restitution Sandbox**, where you can explore how the **coefficient of restitution (e)** affects how rigid bodies bounce during a collision.

---

### **Key Concept: What Is Restitution?**  

┌───────────────────────────┐  
│ **Quick Definition**      │  
│                           │  
│ The **coefficient of    │  
│ restitution (e)** measures│  
│ how "bouncy" a collision │  
│ is. It determines the    │  
│ ratio of relative speed   │  
│ after and before impact. │  
└───────────────────────────┘  

- **e = 0**: No bounce (perfectly inelastic)  
- **e = 1**: Perfect bounce (perfectly elastic)  
- **0 < e < 1**: Partial bounce (realistic materials)  

---

### **Main Formula**  

┌───────────────────────────┐  
│ **Collision Restitution** │  
│                           │  
│ $$                        │  
│ e = \frac{v_{2f} - v_{1f}}│  
│ {v_{1i} - v_{2i}}         │  
$$                        │  
│                           │  
│ [ Explain Formula ]       │  
│ [ Open Formula Lab ]      │  
└───────────────────────────┘  

**Variables**:  
- \( e \): Coefficient of restitution  
- \( v_{1i}, v_{2i} \): Initial velocities of objects 1 and 2  
- \( v_{1f}, v_{2f} \): Final velocities after collision  

> 🔍 *The higher the `e`, the more kinetic energy is preserved during the bounce.*

### **Behavior by Restitution Level**  

┌─────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐  
│ **Restitution Effects on Bounce**                   │  
├───────────────┬─────────────────────────────────────┤  
│ **e = 0.0**   │ Objects **stick together** after    │  
│ (Clay-like)   │ impact. No separation. Final speed: │  
│               │ both move at 0 m/s (center of mass).│  
├───────────────┼─────────────────────────────────────┤  
│ **e = 0.5**   │ **Partial rebound**. Objects bounce │  
│ (Rubber ball) │ weakly. Final speeds reduced by half │  
│               │ relative to initial separation.     │  
├───────────────┼─────────────────────────────────────┤  
│ **e = 1.0**   │ **Perfect rebound**. Objects reverse│  
│ (Superball)   │ velocities completely. Total kinetic│  
│               │ energy is conserved.                │  
└─────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘  

> 📈 **Trend**: As `e` increases →  
> - Greater separation speed after collision  
> - More kinetic energy retained  
> - "Bouncier" interaction  

---

### **Applications in Real-World Design**  

🔧 **Engineering & Game Physics**:  
- **Low `e`**: Car crumple zones (absorb energy)  
- **High `e`**: Tennis balls, basketballs (maximize bounce)  
- **Simulations**: Video games use tuned `e` values for realism  

---

### **Important Notes**  
⚠️ Restitution **only affects the normal (impact) direction**.  
⚠️ Values >1.0 are unphysical (would create energy).  
⚠️ Real materials vary:  
   - Steel on steel: ~0.9  
   - Wood on wood: ~0.5  
   - Putty on wall: ~0.1
