            <p>
                In classical mechanics, equations of motion describe the behavior of a physical system in terms of its motion as a function of time. 
                Specifically, we are looking to derive the relationship between <strong>displacement</strong> ($s$), <strong>initial velocity</strong> ($u$), <strong>acceleration</strong> ($a$), and <strong>time</strong> ($t$).
            </p>
            <p>
                This equation is crucial when we do not know the final velocity of an object but need to calculate how far it has traveled under constant acceleration.
            </p>

            <ul>
                <li><strong>s</strong>: Displacement (the distance traveled in a straight line).</li>
                <li><strong>u</strong>: Initial velocity (speed at time $t = 0$).</li>
                <li><strong>v</strong>: Final velocity (speed at time $t$).</li>
                <li><strong>a</strong>: Constant acceleration.</li>
                <li><strong>t</strong>: Time interval.</li>

            <p>
                The graph shows velocity increasing linearly with time (constant acceleration). The shape under the line is a <strong>trapezium</strong>. 
                The area of this trapezium is equal to the total displacement ($s$).
            </p>
            
            <p>
                The area of a trapezium is given by:
                $$ \text{Area} = \frac{\text{Sum of parallel sides}}{2} \times \text{Height} $$
            </p>

            <p>Applying this to our graph:</p>
            <ul>
                <li>Parallel sides are initial velocity ($u$) and final velocity ($v$).</li>
                <li>Height is the time interval ($t$).</li>
            </ul>
            
            <p>Therefore:</p>
            <div class="formula-display" style="font-size: 1.2rem; margin: 1rem 0;">
                $$ s = \left( \frac{u + v}{2} \right) t $$
            </div>
            
            <p>
                However, our target formula does not contain final velocity ($v$). We must eliminate $v$. 
                We use the <strong>First Equation of Motion</strong>:
            </p>
             <div class="formula-display" style="font-size: 1.2rem; margin: 1rem 0;">
                $$ v = u + at $$
            </div>
            
            <p>Substitute $(u + at)$ for $v$ in the area equation:</p>
            <div class="formula-display" style="font-size: 1.2rem; margin: 1rem 0;">
                $$ s = \left( \frac{u + (u + at)}{2} \right) t $$
            </div>
            
            <p>Simplify the numerator:</p>
            <div class="formula-display" style="font-size: 1.2rem; margin: 1rem 0;">
                $$ s = \left( \frac{2u + at}{2} \right) t $$
            </div>
            
            <p>Distribute $t$ across the terms:</p>
            <div class="formula-display" style="font-size: 1.2rem; margin: 1rem 0;">
                $$ s = \frac{2ut}{2} + \frac{at^2}{2} $$
            </div>
            
            <p>Final simplification:</p>
        </article>
