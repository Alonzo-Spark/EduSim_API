## Gravitational Force Between Two Masses

<div class="card definition-card">
  <div class="card-label">📖 Quick Definition</div>
  <div class="card-content">
    Every object with mass attracts every other object with mass. This attraction is called <strong>gravitational force</strong>. The strength of this force depends on how massive the objects are and how far apart they are.
  </div>
</div>

<div class="card formula-card">
  <div class="card-label">⚡ Newton's Law of Universal Gravitation</div>
  <div class="formula-display">

$$F = \frac{G \cdot m_1 \cdot m_2}{r^2}$$

  </div>
  <div class="formula-variables">
    <div class="var-row"><span class="var-symbol">F</span><span class="var-desc">Gravitational force between the two masses (Newtons, N)</span></div>
    <div class="var-row"><span class="var-symbol">G</span><span class="var-desc">Universal gravitational constant = 6.674 × 10⁻¹¹ N·m²/kg²</span></div>
    <div class="var-row"><span class="var-symbol">m₁, m₂</span><span class="var-desc">Masses of the two objects (kg)</span></div>
    <div class="var-row"><span class="var-symbol">r</span><span class="var-desc">Distance between the centres of the two masses (m)</span></div>
  </div>
</div>

<div class="card given-card">
  <div class="card-label">📋 Given Values</div>
  <div class="given-grid">
    <div class="given-item"><span class="given-label">m₁</span><span class="given-value">5 kg</span></div>
    <div class="given-item"><span class="given-label">m₂</span><span class="given-value">5 kg</span></div>
    <div class="given-item"><span class="given-label">r</span><span class="given-value">2 m</span></div>
    <div class="given-item"><span class="given-label">G</span><span class="given-value">6.674 × 10⁻¹¹ N·m²/kg²</span></div>
  </div>
</div>

<div class="card steps-card">
  <div class="card-label">🔢 Step-by-Step Solution</div>

  <div class="step">
    <div class="step-number">Step 1</div>
    <div class="step-title">Write the formula</div>
    <div class="step-content">

$$F = \frac{G \cdot m_1 \cdot m_2}{r^2}$$

  </div>
  </div>

  <div class="step">
    <div class="step-number">Step 2</div>
    <div class="step-title">Substitute the values</div>
    <div class="step-content">

$$F = \frac{6.674 \times 10^{-11} \times 5 \times 5}{(2)^2}$$

  </div>
  </div>

  <div class="step">
    <div class="step-number">Step 3</div>
    <div class="step-title">Calculate the numerator</div>
    <div class="step-content">

$$6.674 \times 10^{-11} \times 25 = 1.6685 \times 10^{-9}$$

  </div>
  </div>

  <div class="step">
    <div class="step-number">Step 4</div>
    <div class="step-title">Calculate the denominator</div>
    <div class="step-content">

$$r^2 = (2)^2 = 4 \text{ m}^2$$

  </div>
  </div>

  <div class="step">
    <div class="step-number">Step 5</div>
    <div class="step-title">Divide to get the final answer</div>
    <div class="step-content">

$$F = \frac{1.6685 \times 10^{-9}}{4} = 4.171 \times 10^{-10} \text{ N}$$

  </div>
  </div>

</div>

<div class="card result-card">
  <div class="card-label">✅ Final Answer</div>
  <div class="result-display">

$$\boxed{F \approx 4.17 \times 10^{-10} \text{ N}}$$

  </div>
  <div class="result-note">
    This is an extremely small force — which is why gravity between everyday objects is unnoticeable. Gravity only becomes significant when at least one object has a very large mass (like a planet).
  </div>
</div>

<div class="card insight-card">
  <div class="card-label">💡 Key Insight — Inverse Square Law</div>
  <div class="card-content">
    Notice that <strong>r² appears in the denominator</strong>. This means:
    <ul>
      <li>If you <strong>double</strong> the distance → force becomes <strong>¼</strong> as strong</li>
      <li>If you <strong>halve</strong> the distance → force becomes <strong>4×</strong> stronger</li>
    </ul>
    This is called the <strong>Inverse Square Law</strong>.
  </div>
</div>

</div>
