## 📘 Statement

> **The net work done on an object equals the change in its kinetic energy.**

$$W_{\text{net}} = \Delta KE = \frac{1}{2}mv_f^2 - \frac{1}{2}mv_i^2$$

---

## 🔑 Key Definitions

| Term | Symbol | Definition |
|------|--------|------------|
| Work | $W$ | Energy transferred by a force over displacement |
| Kinetic Energy | $KE$ | Energy due to motion: $\frac{1}{2}mv^2$ |
| Net Work | $W_{\text{net}}$ | Total work done by **all** forces |
| Mass | $m$ | Inertia of the object (kg) |
| Velocity | $v$ | Speed of the object (m/s) |

---

## 📐 Derivation

### Step 1 — Start with Newton's Second Law

$$F_{\text{net}} = ma$$

### Step 2 — Express acceleration using kinematics

From $v_f^2 = v_i^2 + 2as$, solve for $as$:

$$as = \frac{v_f^2 - v_i^2}{2}$$

### Step 3 — Define Work

Work done by net force over displacement $s$:

$$W_{\text{net}} = F_{\text{net}} \cdot s = ma \cdot s$$

### Step 4 — Substitute

$$W_{\text{net}} = m \cdot (as) = m \cdot \frac{v_f^2 - v_i^2}{2}$$

$$\boxed{W_{\text{net}} = \frac{1}{2}mv_f^2 - \frac{1}{2}mv_i^2}$$

---

## 🔬 Calculus-Based Derivation (Rigorous)

For variable forces, work is:

$$W_{\text{net}} = \int_{x_i}^{x_f} F_{\text{net}}\, dx$$

Apply Newton's second law $F = ma = m\dfrac{dv}{dt}$, and use the chain rule:

$$\frac{dv}{dt} = \frac{dv}{dx}\cdot\frac{dx}{dt} = v\frac{dv}{dx}$$

So:

$$W_{\text{net}} = \int_{x_i}^{x_f} m\,v\,\frac{dv}{dx}\,dx = \int_{v_i}^{v_f} mv\,dv$$

$$W_{\text{net}} = m \cdot \frac{v^2}{2}\Bigg|_{v_i}^{v_f}$$

$$\boxed{W_{\text{net}} = \frac{1}{2}mv_f^2 - \frac{1}{2}mv_i^2}$$

---

## ⚡ Important Notes

- Only **net work** (by all forces combined) equals $\Delta KE$
- If $W_{\text{net}} > 0$ → object **speeds up**
- If $W_{\text{net}} < 0$ → object **slows down**
- If $W_{\text{net}} = 0$ → **no change** in speed (e.g. circular motion)
- The theorem holds for **variable forces** (calculus form)
- It is a **scalar** equation — direction doesn't appear explicitly

---

## 📊 Sign Convention Summary

| Condition | $W_{\text{net}}$ | Effect |
|-----------|-----------------|--------|
| Force and displacement in same direction | $+$ | KE increases |
| Force and displacement in opposite direction | $-$ | KE decreases |
| Force perpendicular to displacement | $0$ | KE unchanged |

## 🧪 Quick Example

A 2 kg block accelerates from rest to 6 m/s. Find net work done.

$$W_{\text{net}} = \frac{1}{2}(2)(6)^2 - \frac{1}{2}(2)(0)^2 = 36 - 0 = \boxed{36 \text{ J}}$$
