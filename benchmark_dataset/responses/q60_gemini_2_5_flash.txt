### Key Concepts: Understanding Zero-G

Zero-gravity, often referred to as weightlessness, is a state where the effect of gravity is not felt. This doesn't mean gravity is absent, but rather that objects are in continuous freefall.

*   **Freefall:** All objects, including you, are accelerating at the same rate due to gravity, creating the sensation of weightlessness.
*   **Inertia:** Objects in motion tend to stay in motion, and objects at rest tend to stay at rest, unless acted upon by an external force. This becomes very apparent in zero-g.
*   **Microgravity:** The term "zero-g" is often used loosely; in orbit, there's actually still about 90% of Earth's gravity. The weightless sensation comes from constantly "falling around" the Earth.

### Characteristics of Zero-G

| Characteristic        | Description                                                                                             | Impact in Sandbox                                                                      |
| :-------------------- | :------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ | :------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Weightlessness**    | Objects and entities have no apparent weight.                                                           | Objects will float freely unless anchored or propelled.                                |
| **No Up/Down**        | Without gravity, there's no inherent "up" or "down" direction.                                          | Orientation becomes relative to your perspective or fixed objects.                      |
| **Reduced Friction**  | Surfaces no longer press together with weight, significantly reducing friction in many scenarios.         | Movement across surfaces requires less force but also offers less resistance.          |
| **Fluid Dynamics**    | Liquids form spherical blobs due to surface tension and do not settle.                                  | Spilled liquids will float as globules. Pouring acts differently.                      |
| **Propulsion Needed** | Any movement requires an initial force; objects will continue moving until another force stops them.    | Use thrusters, pushes, or tethers for movement.                                        |

### Applications (Simulated)

In a real-world zero-g environment, these principles are crucial:

*   **Space Travel:** Essential for astronauts living and working on the International Space Station.
*   **Material Science:** Growing perfect crystals or mixing immiscible liquids without gravitational settling.
*   **Biology:** Studying the effects of microgravity on living organisms.
*   **Manufacturing:** Creating foams or alloys with unique properties.

---

### Important Notes for Interaction

*   **Movement:** Use your directional controls to propel yourself. Remember inertia! A small push can send you a long way.
*   **Anchoring:** Look for handholds or tethers to stabilize yourself.
*   **Objects:** Pushing objects will cause both you and the object to move in opposite directions (Newton's Third Law).
*   **Fluids:** Be cautious with liquids; they will not stay in open containers.
*   **Orientation:** You can reorient your view freely. There's no "right side up."
