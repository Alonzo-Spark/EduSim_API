Based on the "friction_changed" event on a braking track platform, here's the interactive overlay analysis:

```markdown
### 🚗 Braking Dynamics Analysis
**Event:** Friction coefficient changed  
**Platform:** Braking track simulation  
**Initial velocity:** \( v_0 = 20 \, \text{m/s} \)  
**New friction coefficient:** \( \mu_k = 0.7 \)  

:::FormulaCard
**Deceleration Formula**  
$$ a = -\mu_k \cdot g $$  

**Stopping Distance**  
$$ d = \frac{v_0^2}{2\mu_k g} $$  

**Stopping Time**  
$$ t = \frac{v_0}{\mu_k g} $$
:::

:::ResultsCard
**Calculated Values** (\( g = 9.8 \, \text{m/s}^2 \))  
- Deceleration: \( a = -0.7 \times 9.8 = -6.86 \, \text{m/s}^2 \)  
- Stopping distance: \( d = \frac{20^2}{2 \times 0.7 \times 9.8} = 29.15 \, \text{m} \)  
- Stopping time: \( t = \frac{20}{0.7 \times 9.8} = 2.91 \, \text{s} \)  
:::

### 📊 Friction Impact Comparison
| Friction (μ) | Stopping Distance | Stopping Time |
|--------------|-------------------|--------------|
| 0.4          | 51.02 m          | 5.10 s       |
| → **0.7**    | **29.15 m**      | **2.91 s**   |
| 1.0          | 20.41 m          | 2.04 s       |

### 💡 Key Insights
1. **Inverse relationship**  
   Doubling friction coefficient reduces stopping distance by half
   
2. **Safety implications**  
   - Higher μ = Shorter stopping distance  
   - Lower μ = Risk of skidding increases exponentially

3. **Real-world factors**  
   $$ \mu_{\text{effective}} = \mu_k \cdot \text{surface} \cdot \text{tire} $$
   (Surface conditions reduce nominal μ by 30-60%)
