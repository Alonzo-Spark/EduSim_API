## Spring Connection Event: Anchor to Mass

This overlay describes the successful connection of a spring between a fixed anchor point and a movable mass in a simulation environment.

---

### Quick Definition

A **spring** is an elastic object that stores mechanical energy. When compressed or stretched from its equilibrium length, it exerts a restoring force proportional to its displacement.

---

### Event Details

A spring has been successfully connected between:

*   **Anchor Point:** A fixed point in space.
*   **Mass Object:** A physical object with defined mass.

---

### Key Concepts: Hooke's Law

The fundamental principle governing the behavior of an ideal spring is **Hooke's Law**. This law states that the force ($F$) needed to extend or compress a spring by some distance ($x$) is proportional to that distance.

---

#### Main Formula

$$
F = -kx
$$

### Characteristics of the Spring

| Characteristic      | Description                                                                                             |
| :------------------ | :------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| **Spring Constant** | Denoted by $k$, it's a measure of the spring's stiffness. A higher $k$ means a stiffer spring.           |
| **Restoring Force** | The force exerted by the spring that tries to return it to its equilibrium position.                     |
| **Equilibrium Length** | The natural length of the spring when no external forces are acting upon it.                         |
| **Damping Coefficient** | (Optional) Represents energy loss in the spring due-to friction or air resistance. Often denoted by $c$. |

---

### Simulation Impact

Connecting a spring introduces a new force into the system. The mass connected to the spring will now experience this restoring force, leading to oscillatory motion (if undamped) or a new equilibrium position.

---

### Important Notes

*   **Direction of Force:** The negative sign in Hooke's Law indicates that the spring force is always in the opposite direction to the displacement from equilibrium.
*   **Ideal Spring Assumption:** In many simulations, springs are assumed to be ideal (massless, perfectly elastic, no damping) unless specified otherwise.
*   **Conservation of Energy:** For an ideal spring-mass system, mechanical energy (kinetic + potential) is conserved.
