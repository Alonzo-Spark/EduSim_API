      Let's explore the behavior of a spring oscillator and how its motion changes when we alter the spring's stiffness. The stiffness constant, often denoted by *k*, is a crucial property that dictates how much force is required to stretch or compress a spring.

  <div class="section-card">
    <div class="section-title">Key Concepts</div>
    <div class="section-content">
      A spring oscillator is a simple harmonic oscillator where a mass is attached to a spring. The system oscillates back and forth around an equilibrium position. Several key concepts govern its behavior:
      <ul>
        <li>**Stiffness Constant (k):** Measures the spring's resistance to deformation. A higher *k* means a stiffer spring.</li>
        <li>**Mass (m):** The mass attached to the spring.</li>
        <li>**Angular Frequency (ω):**  Determines the rate of oscillation.</li>
        <li>**Period (T):** The time it takes for one complete oscillation.</li>
      </ul>
    </div>
  </div>

  <div class="formula-card">
    <div class="formula-title">Angular Frequency</div>
    <div class="formula-content">
      $$
      \omega = \sqrt{\frac{k}{m}}
      $$
    </div>
    <div class="formula-buttons">
      <button class="formula-button">Explain Formula</button>

    </div>

  <div class="section-card">
    <div class="section-title">Effect of Doubling Stiffness</div>
    <div class="section-content">
      When the stiffness constant (*k*) of a spring is doubled while keeping the mass (*m*) constant, the angular frequency (ω) changes.  Let's see how.
    </div>
  </div>

  <div class="section-card">
    <div class="section-title">Analysis</div>
    <div class="section-content">
      If we double *k* (to 2*k*), the new angular frequency (ω') becomes:
    </div>
  </div>

  <div class="formula-card">
    <div class="formula-title">New Angular Frequency</div>
    <div class="formula-content">
      $$
      \omega' = \sqrt{\frac{2k}{m}} = \sqrt{2} \cdot \sqrt{\frac{k}{m}} = \sqrt{2} \cdot \omega
      $$
    </div>
    <div class="formula-buttons">
      <button class="formula-button">Explain Formula</button>
      <button class="formula-button">Open Formula Lab</button>
    </div>
  </div>

  <div class="section-card">
    <div class="section-title">Implications</div>
    <div class="section-content">
      <ul>
        <li>**Increased Angular Frequency:** The angular frequency increases by a factor of the square root of 2 (approximately 1.414). This means the oscillator will oscillate faster.</li>
        <li>**Decreased Period:** The period (T) of oscillation is inversely proportional to the angular frequency ($T = \frac{2\pi}{\omega}$). Therefore, doubling the stiffness constant will *decrease* the period. The new period (T') will be $T' = \frac{T}{\sqrt{2}}$. The oscillator will complete one cycle in less time.</li>
        <li>**Higher Frequency:**  The frequency of oscillation (f), which is also proportional to angular frequency, will increase.</li>
      </ul>
    </div>
  </div>

  <div class="section-card">
    <div class="section-title">Visual Outcome</div>
    <div class="section-content">
      Visually, the mass will move back and forth more rapidly. The amplitude of the oscillation *might* change depending on the initial conditions and energy of the system, but the *rate* of oscillation will definitely increase.
    </div>
  </div>

  <div class="section-card">
    <div class="section-title">Summary</div>
    <div class="section-content">
      Doubling the stiffness constant of a spring oscillator leads to a higher angular frequency, a shorter period, and a faster oscillation rate. The mass will oscillate back and forth more quickly.
    </div>
  </div>
