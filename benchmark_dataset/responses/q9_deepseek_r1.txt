Here's the structured explanation of uniform circular motion and centripetal acceleration:

```jsx
<QuickDefinition title="Uniform Circular Motion">
An object moving in a circular path with constant tangential speed. While speed remains constant, velocity continuously changes direction, creating centripetal acceleration.
</QuickDefinition>

<KeyConcepts>
1. Direction-changing velocity despite constant speed
2. Requires centripetal (center-seeking) force
3. Acceleration always points toward circle center
4. Fundamental in rotational dynamics
</KeyConcepts>

<FormulaCard title="Centripetal Acceleration Formula" equation="a_c = \frac{v^2}{r} = \omega^2 r">
- **a_c**: Centripetal acceleration (m/s²)
- **v**: Tangential speed (m/s)
- **r**: Circle radius (m)
- **ω**: Angular velocity (rad/s)

<FormulaExplanation>
The acceleration required to keep an object in circular motion increases with the square of speed and decreases with larger radius. The angular velocity form shows dependence on rotation rate.
</FormulaExplanation>
</FormulaCard>

<CharacteristicCards>
  <Characteristic title="Constant Quantity">
  Tangential speed (magnitude of velocity)
  </Characteristic>

  <Characteristic title="Changing Quantity">
  Velocity direction (causing acceleration)
  </Characteristic>

  <Characteristic title="Force Type">
  Centripetal force - not a separate force but the net force component toward center
  </Characteristic>
</CharacteristicCards>

<ApplicationExamples>
1. **Planetary Orbits**: Gravity provides centripetal force
2. **Car Turning**: Friction between tires and road
3. **Centrifuges**: Medical equipment separating substances
4. **Amusement Rides**: Roller coaster loops
</ApplicationExamples>

<CommonMisconception>
"Centrifugal force pushes objects outward"  
**Reality**: What's observed is inertia - objects tend to move tangent to the circle when centripetal force is removed.
</CommonMisconception>

<SummaryBox>
- Uniform circular motion: Constant speed, changing direction
- Centripetal acceleration: a_c = v²/r = ω²r

- Applications range from atomic scales to galactic motion
</SummaryBox>
```
