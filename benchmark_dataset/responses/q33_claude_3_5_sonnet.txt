Here's a structured breakdown of the three modes of heat transfer:

---

## Heat Transfer

Heat energy moves from regions of **higher temperature** to **lower temperature** through three distinct mechanisms.

---

### ⚡ Conduction

**Definition:** Transfer of heat through direct contact between particles of matter, without bulk movement of the material.

**How it works:** Vibrating particles pass energy to neighboring particles through collisions.

| Property | Details |
|---|---|
| Medium Required | Yes — solid, liquid, or gas |
| Best in | Solids (especially metals) |
| Particle Movement | Vibrate in place — no bulk flow |
| Speed | Fast in metals, slow in insulators |

**Examples:**
- A metal spoon heating up in hot soup
- Heat spreading through a iron pan on a stove
- Your hand warming when touching a hot mug

**Key Quantity — Thermal Conductivity (k):**

$$Q = \frac{kA\Delta T \cdot t}{d}$$

Where:
- $Q$ = heat transferred (Joules)
- $k$ = thermal conductivity of material
- $A$ = cross-sectional area
- $\Delta T$ = temperature difference
- $t$ = time
- $d$ = thickness/distance

---

### 🌊 Convection

**Definition:** Transfer of heat through the **bulk movement** of a fluid (liquid or gas).

**How it works:** Warmer, less dense fluid rises; cooler, denser fluid sinks — creating convection currents.

| Property | Details |
|---|---|
| Medium Required | Yes — fluids only (liquid or gas) |
| Particle Movement | Bulk flow of matter |
| Types | Natural & Forced convection |
| Speed | Moderate |

**Examples:**
- Boiling water circulating in a pot
- Sea breezes and land breezes
- Hot air balloons rising
- Heating systems in buildings

**Key Quantity — Newton's Law of Cooling:**

$$Q = hA(T_s - T_\infty)$$

Where:
- $Q$ = heat transfer rate (Watts)
- $h$ = convective heat transfer coefficient
- $A$ = surface area
- $T_s$ = surface temperature
- $T_\infty$ = fluid temperature far from surface

---

### ☀️ Radiation

**Definition:** Transfer of heat through **electromagnetic waves**, requiring **no medium**.

**How it works:** All objects above absolute zero emit electromagnetic radiation. Energy travels as photons through space.

| Property | Details |
|---|---|
| Medium Required | **No** — works in vacuum |
| Wave Type | Infrared electromagnetic waves |
| Speed | Speed of light ($3 \times 10^8$ m/s) |
| Emitter | All objects above 0 K |

**Examples:**
- Heat from the Sun reaching Earth
- Heat felt near a campfire without touching it
- Infrared heaters
- Thermal imaging cameras

**Key Quantity — Stefan-Boltzmann Law:**

$$P = \varepsilon \sigma A T^4$$

Where:
- $P$ = power radiated (Watts)
- $\varepsilon$ = emissivity (0 to 1)
- $\sigma$ = Stefan-Boltzmann constant $= 5.67 \times 10^{-8}\ \text{W m}^{-2}\text{K}^{-4}$
- $A$ = surface area
- $T$ = absolute temperature (Kelvin)

---

### 🔁 Side-by-Side Comparison

| Feature | Conduction | Convection | Radiation |
|---|---|---|---|
| Medium needed | Yes | Yes | **No** |
| Matter moves | No | **Yes** | No |
| Works in vacuum | No | No | **Yes** |
| Speed | Slow–Fast | Moderate | Speed of light |
| Best medium | Solids | Fluids | Any / vacuum |
| Example | Hot spoon | Boiling water | Sunlight |

---

### 💡 Key Insight

> In most real-world situations, **all three mechanisms occur simultaneously.** For example, a radiator heats a room by conduction through its metal walls, convection of air currents, and radiation of infrared energy — all at once.
