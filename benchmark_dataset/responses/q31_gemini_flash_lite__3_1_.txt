          <li>\(\Delta T\) is the change in temperature (in degrees Celsius, °C).</li>

    <div class="section-content">
      Specific heat capacity provides a standardized measure of a substance's thermal behavior by considering the mass of the substance. It represents the amount of heat required to raise the temperature of *one unit of mass* of a substance by one degree Celsius (or Kelvin). This allows for direct comparison of different materials.
    </div>
  </div>

  <div class="section-card">
    <div class="section-title">Formula: Specific Heat Capacity</div>
    <div class="formula-card">
      <div class="formula-title">Specific Heat Capacity Formula</div>
      <div class="formula-display">
        $$
        c = \frac{Q}{m \Delta T}
        $$
      </div>
      <div class="formula-explanation">
        Where:
        <ul>
          <li>\(c\) is the specific heat capacity (in Joules per kilogram per degree Celsius, J/(kg·°C)).</li>
          <li>\(Q\) is the amount of heat energy transferred (in Joules, J).</li>
          <li>\(m\) is the mass of the substance (in kilograms, kg).</li>
          <li>\(\Delta T\) is the change in temperature (in degrees Celsius, °C).</li>
        </ul>
      </div>

    <div class="section-content">
      <ul>
        <li>Heat capacity depends on the amount of substance; a larger amount requires more heat for the same temperature change.</li>
        <li>Specific heat capacity is an intrinsic property of a substance, meaning it's independent of the amount.</li>
        <li>Different substances have different specific heat capacities. For example, water has a high specific heat capacity, which is why it takes a long time to heat up or cool down.</li>
      </ul>
