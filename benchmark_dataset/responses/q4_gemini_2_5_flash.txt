The request is to derive the equation of motion $s = ut + \frac{1}{2}at^2$.

Here's a structured approach to understanding this fundamental kinematic equation.

***

### Quick Definition

This equation, $s = ut + \frac{1}{2}at^2$, is one of the key **equations of motion** (also known as SUVAT equations) that describes the relationship between displacement, initial velocity, time, and acceleration for an object moving with constant acceleration in a straight line.

***

### Key Concepts

*   **Displacement ($s$)**: The change in position of an object. It's a vector quantity, but in one-dimensional motion, we often refer to its magnitude.
*   **Initial Velocity ($u$)**: The velocity of the object at the beginning of the time interval ($t=0$).
*   **Final Velocity ($v$)**: The velocity of the object at the end of the time interval ($t$).
*   **Acceleration ($a$)**: The rate of change of velocity. For this equation, acceleration is assumed to be **constant**.
*   **Time ($t$)**: The duration over which the motion is observed.

***

### Derivation

We will derive this equation using the definitions of average velocity and acceleration.

#### Step 1: Definition of Average Velocity

For an object moving with constant acceleration, the average velocity ($\bar{v}$) can be expressed as the arithmetic mean of the initial and final velocities:

$$ \bar{v} = \frac{u + v}{2} $$

We also know that displacement ($s$) is the product of average velocity and time ($t$):

$$ s = \bar{v}t $$

Substituting the expression for $\bar{v}$ into the displacement equation:

$$ s = \left(\frac{u + v}{2}\right)t \quad \cdots (1) $$

#### Step 2: Definition of Acceleration

Acceleration ($a$) is defined as the rate of change of velocity:

$$ a = \frac{v - u}{t} $$

Rearranging this equation to solve for the final velocity ($v$):

$$ v - u = at $$

$$ v = u + at \quad \cdots (2) $$

#### Step 3: Substitute Final Velocity into Displacement Equation

Now, substitute the expression for $v$ from equation (2) into equation (1):

$$ s = \left(\frac{u + (u + at)}{2}\right)t $$

Simplify the expression inside the parenthesis:

$$ s = \left(\frac{2u + at}{2}\right)t $$

$$ s = \left(\frac{2u}{2} + \frac{at}{2}\right)t $$

$$ s = \left(u + \frac{1}{2}at\right)t $$

Finally, distribute $t$ across the terms:

$$ s = ut + \frac{1}{2}at^2 $$

This completes the derivation.

***

### Main Formula

This is the derived equation:

$$ s = ut + \frac{1}{2}at^2 $$

### Variables in the Formula

*   $s$: Displacement (often measured in meters, m)
*   $u$: Initial velocity (often measured in meters per second, m/s)
*   $t$: Time (often measured in seconds, s)
*   $a$: Constant acceleration (often measured in meters per second squared, m/s²)

***

### Important Notes

*   This derivation assumes **constant acceleration**. If acceleration is not constant, calculus is required to solve the motion.
*   The motion is assumed to be in a **straight line**.
*   When dealing with vector quantities like displacement, velocity, and acceleration, it's crucial to be consistent with chosen positive and negative directions.

***

### Summary

The equation $s = ut + \frac{1}{2}at^2$ is derived by combining the definition of average velocity for constant acceleration with the definition of acceleration itself. It allows us to calculate the displacement of an object given its initial velocity, constant acceleration, and the time elapsed.
