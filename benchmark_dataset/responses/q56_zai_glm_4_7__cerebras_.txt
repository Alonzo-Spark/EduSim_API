  <h2 class="card-header">Introduction</h2>
  <div class="card-content">
    <p>When two rigid bodies collide, the outcome isn't just determined by their mass and velocity. A critical property called the <strong>Coefficient of Restitution</strong> (often denoted as <em>e</em>) dictates how "bouncy" the interaction is. By adjusting this single value, we can simulate anything from a steel ball bearing bouncing off a floor to a ball of clay sticking to the wall.</p>
  </div>

<!-- UI BLOCK: DEFINITION CARD -->
<div class="textbook-card">
  <h2 class="card-header">Quick Definition</h2>
  <div class="card-content">
    <p>The Coefficient of Restitution (<strong>e</strong>) is a dimensionless number that represents the ratio of the relative velocity of separation to the relative velocity of approach between two objects after they collide.</p>
    <p>In simpler terms, it measures the elasticity of the collision.</p>
  </div>
</div>

<!-- UI BLOCK: FORMULA CARD -->
<div class="formula-card">

  </div>

<!-- UI BLOCK: KEY CONCEPTS CARD -->
<div class="textbook-card">
  <h2 class="card-header">Key Concepts</h2>
  <div class="card-content">
    <ul>
      <li><strong>Approach Velocity:</strong> The speed at which the objects move toward each other before impact.</li>
      <li><strong>Separation Velocity:</strong> The speed at which the objects move away from each other after impact.</li>
      <li><strong>Energy Conservation:</strong> A higher restitution value implies less kinetic energy is lost during the collision.</li>
    </ul>
  </div>
</div>

<!-- UI BLOCK: IMPORTANT NOTES CARD -->
<div class="textbook-card">
  <h2 class="card-header">Important Notes</h2>
  <div class="card-content">
    <p><strong>Direction Matters:</strong> Velocities are vectors. In the 1D formula, absolute values are used to ensure the coefficient remains positive regardless of direction.</p>
    <p><strong>Material Dependency:</strong> Restitution is an intrinsic property of the materials involved (e.g., wood vs. rubber). It is not usually a constant for an object regardless of what it hits; a rubber ball has a different 'e' against steel than it does against mud.</p>

      <li>Decrease <em>e</em> towards 0.0 to make collisions feel heavy, dead, or sticky.</li>
