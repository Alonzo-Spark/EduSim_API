To calculate the gravitational force between two masses, we use **Newton's Law of Universal Gravitation**:

**Formula**  
\[
F = G \cdot \frac{m_1 \cdot m_2}{r^2}
\]

**Given**  
- \( m_1 = 5 \, \text{kg} \)
- \( m_2 = 5 \, \text{kg} \)
- \( r = 2 \, \text{m} \)
- Gravitational constant \( G = 6.674 \times 10^{-11} \, \text{N·m}^2/\text{kg}^2 \)

**Calculation**  
\[
F = 6.674 \times 10^{-11} \cdot \frac{5 \cdot 5}{2^2} = 6.674 \times 10^{-11} \cdot \frac{25}{4} = 6.674 \times 10^{-11} \cdot 6.25
\]
\[
F = 4.17125 \times 10^{-10} \, \text{N}
\]

**Final Answer**  
\[
\boxed{4.17 \times 10^{-10} \, \text{N}}
\]

**Key Notes**  
1. Gravitational force is extremely weak** for everyday masses.
2. The result has **2 significant figures** (limited by \( G \)).
3. Force direction: Always **attractive** between masses.
