Let's explore the fundamental properties of waves!

---

**Introduction**

Waves are ubiquitous in the universe, transferring energy without transferring matter. From the ripples in a pond to the light that allows us to see, understanding wave properties is crucial. We'll define three key characteristics: wavelength, frequency, and amplitude.

---

**Quick Definition**

*   **Wavelength:** The distance between two consecutive identical points in a wave.
*   **Frequency:** The number of wave cycles that pass a given point per unit of time.
*   **Amplitude:** The maximum displacement of a point on the wave from its equilibrium position.

---

**Key Concepts**

These three properties are interconnected and define the behavior of a wave. They are essential for understanding wave phenomena like interference, diffraction, and the Doppler effect.

---

**Formula**

$$
c = f \lambda
$$

**Explanation of the Formula**

The formula  $$c = f \lambda$$  relates the speed of a wave (c) to its frequency (f) and wavelength (λ). In this equation:

*   **c** represents the wave speed, which is constant for a given medium (e.g., the speed of light in a vacuum).
*   **f** represents the frequency, measured in Hertz (Hz), which is the number of cycles per second.
*   **λ** represents the wavelength, measured in meters (m), which is the distance between two consecutive crests or troughs.

The formula tells us that the speed of a wave is directly proportional to its frequency and wavelength. If the frequency increases, the wavelength must decrease to maintain a constant wave speed, and vice-versa.
