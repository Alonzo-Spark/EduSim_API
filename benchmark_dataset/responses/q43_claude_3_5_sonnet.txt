# Electric Force Between Two Charges

---

## The Core Idea

Before we derive anything, let's understand what we're trying to find.

> Two charged objects exert forces on each other — even without touching. This is the **electric force**, and it acts along the line joining the two charges.

---

## Setting Up the Problem

Place two point charges in space:

| Symbol | Meaning |
|--------|---------|
| $q_1$ | First point charge (Coulombs) |
| $q_2$ | Second point charge (Coulombs) |
| $r$ | Distance between them (metres) |
| $F$ | Electric force between them (Newtons) |
| $k$ | Coulomb's constant $= 8.99 \times 10^9 \ \text{N m}^2\text{C}^{-2}$ |

---

## Step-by-Step Derivation

### Step 1 — Identify what $F$ depends on

From experiments, Coulomb observed three things:

**① Force depends on the charges:**

$$F \propto q_1$$

$$F \propto q_2$$

Combining both:

$$F \propto q_1 q_2$$

---

**② Force depends on distance:**

As $r$ increases, $F$ decreases — and it follows an **inverse square law**:

$$F \propto \frac{1}{r^2}$$

---

### Step 2 — Combine the proportionalities

Putting both dependencies together:

$$F \propto \frac{q_1 q_2}{r^2}$$

---

### Step 3 — Introduce the proportionality constant

$$\boxed{F = k \frac{q_1 q_2}{r^2}}$$

where $k$ is **Coulomb's constant**.

---

### Step 4 — Express $k$ in terms of permittivity

The constant $k$ is not arbitrary. It is defined as:

$$k = \frac{1}{4\pi\varepsilon_0}$$

| Symbol | Meaning | Value |
|--------|---------|-------|
| $\varepsilon_0$ | Permittivity of free space | $8.85 \times 10^{-12} \ \text{C}^2\text{N}^{-1}\text{m}^{-2}$ |
| $4\pi$ | Geometric factor from spherical symmetry | — |

Substituting:

$$\boxed{F = \frac{1}{4\pi\varepsilon_0} \cdot \frac{q_1 q_2}{r^2}}$$

This is the **complete, standard form** of Coulomb's Law.

---

### Step 5 — Write the Vector Form

Force is a vector — it has both magnitude and direction.

Let $\hat{r}$ be the unit vector pointing **from $q_1$ to $q_2$**.

$$\vec{F}_{12} = \frac{1}{4\pi\varepsilon_0} \cdot \frac{q_1 q_2}{r^2} \ \hat{r}$$

| Condition | Sign of $q_1 q_2$ | Direction of Force |
|-----------|-------------------|--------------------|
| Both same sign | Positive $(+)$ | Repulsive — away from each other |
| Opposite signs | Negative $(-)$ | Attractive — toward each other |

---

## Final Result

$$\Large\boxed{F = \frac{1}{4\pi\varepsilon_0} \cdot \frac{q_1 q_2}{r^2}}$$

| Part | Role |
|------|------|
| $\frac{1}{4\pi\varepsilon_0}$ | Proportionality constant in SI units |
| $q_1 q_2$ | Product of the two charges |
| $r^2$ | Square of separation distance |

---

## Key Properties of the Electric Force

- ✅ Acts along the **line joining** the two charges
- ✅ Obeys **Newton's Third Law** — equal and opposite on both charges
- ✅ Is an **inverse square law** — like gravity
- ✅ Can be **attractive or repulsive** — unlike gravity
- ✅ Acts through **vacuum/free space** without any medium

---

## Quick Analogy

| Property | Gravitational Force | Electric Force |
|----------|--------------------|--------------:|
| Formula | $G\frac{m_1 m_2}{r^2}$ | $k\frac{q_1 q_2}{r^2}$ |
| Nature | Always attractive | Attractive or repulsive |
| Constant | $G = 6.67\times10^{-11}$ | $k = 8.99\times10^{9}$ |
| Source | Mass | Charge |
