**Introduction**

This section will explain how to calculate the equivalent resistance when resistors are connected in series.  Understanding this concept is fundamental to analyzing and designing electrical circuits.

---

**Key Concepts**

*   **Series Combination:**  Resistors are connected in series when they are placed end-to-end, forming a single path for current to flow. The same current flows through each resistor.
*   **Voltage Drop:**  Each resistor in a series circuit has a voltage drop across it, proportional to its resistance.
*   **Equivalent Resistance (Req):**  The single resistance value that can replace all the resistors in the series combination without changing the total current flowing in the circuit.

---

**Derivation**

Let's derive the formula for the equivalent resistance (Req) of resistors connected in series.

1.  **Kirchhoff's Voltage Law (KVL):**  The sum of the voltage drops across all components in a closed loop (circuit) is equal to the total voltage applied.

2.  **Consider a Series Circuit:**  Imagine three resistors (R1, R2, and R3) connected in series to a voltage source (V).

3.  **Voltage Drops:**  Let V1, V2, and V3 be the voltage drops across R1, R2, and R3, respectively. Applying KVL:

    $$V = V_1 + V_2 + V_3$$

4.  **Ohm's Law:**  The voltage drop across each resistor is given by Ohm's Law: V = IR, where I is the current and R is the resistance.  Since the current (I) is the same through all resistors in series:

    *   V1 = I * R1
    *   V2 = I * R2
    *   V3 = I * R3

5.  **Substitute into KVL:** Substitute the values from Ohm's Law into the KVL equation:

    $$V = I \cdot R_1 + I \cdot R_2 + I \cdot R_3$$

6.  **Factor out the current (I):**

    $$V = I \cdot (R_1 + R_2 + R_3)$$

7.  **Equivalent Resistance:** The equivalent resistance (Req) is the single resistance that would draw the same current (I) from the voltage source (V).  Using Ohm's Law again:

    $$V = I \cdot R_{eq}$$

8.  **Equate the two expressions for V:** Since both equations equal V:

    $$I \cdot R_{eq} = I \cdot (R_1 + R_2 + R_3)$$

9.  **Solve for Req:** Divide both sides by I (assuming I is not zero):

    $$R_{eq} = R_1 + R_2 + R_3$$

**Explanation of Formula**

The equivalent resistance (Req) of resistors connected in series is the sum of the individual resistances (R1, R2, R3, and so on).  This means that the total resistance increases as more resistors are added in series.

---

**Summary**

The equivalent resistance for resistors in series is calculated by summing the individual resistances. This leads to a higher overall resistance, which reduces the total current flowing through the circuit for a given voltage.
