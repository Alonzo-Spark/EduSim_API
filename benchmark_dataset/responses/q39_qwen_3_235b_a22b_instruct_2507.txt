┌───────────────────────────┐  
│ **Quick Definition**      │  
│                           │  
│ Wavelength, frequency, and amplitude are three fundamental properties that describe the behavior and characteristics of waves. They apply to all types of waves, including sound, light, and water waves. │  
└───────────────────────────┘  

---

### **Wavelength**

┌───────────────────────────┐  
│ **Definition**            │  
│                           │  
│ Wavelength is the distance between two consecutive identical points on a wave, such as from crest to crest or trough to trough. │  
└───────────────────────────┘  

- Symbol: $ \lambda $ (lambda)  
- Units: meters (m)  
- Represents the spatial period of the wave — how long one complete cycle is in space.

---

### **Frequency**

┌───────────────────────────┐  
│ **Definition**            │  
│                           │  
│ Frequency is the number of complete wave cycles passing a given point per second. │  
└───────────────────────────┘  

- Symbol: $ f $  
- Units: hertz (Hz) = 1 cycle per second  
- Represents the temporal period of the wave — how often the wave repeats in time.

---

### **Amplitude**

┌───────────────────────────┐  
│ **Definition**            │  
│                           │  
│ Amplitude is the maximum displacement of a wave from its equilibrium (rest) position. │  
└───────────────────────────┘  

- Related to the energy carried by the wave.
- Higher amplitude = more energy.
- For sound waves: higher amplitude means louder sound.  
- For light waves: higher amplitude means brighter light.

---

### **Formula**

$$
v = f \lambda
$$

┌───────────────────────────┐  
│ **Main Formula**          │  
│                           │  
│ $$ v = f \lambda $$       │  
│                           │  
│ [ Explain Formula ]       │  
│ [ Open Formula Lab ]      │  
└───────────────────────────┘  

Where:  
- $ v $ = wave speed (m/s)  
- $ f $ = frequency (Hz)  
- $ \lambda $ = wavelength (m)

This equation shows the relationship between wave speed, frequency, and wavelength.

### **Visual Summary**

| Property     | Symbol | Definition                                 | Unit         | Determines          |

| Wavelength   | $ \lambda $ | Distance between two identical points on a wave | meters (m)   | Wave's spatial length |
| Frequency    | $ f $      | Number of waves passing a point per second     | hertz (Hz)   | Pitch (sound), Color (light) |
| Amplitude    | $ A $      | Maximum displacement from equilibrium          | varies       | Loudness, Brightness, Energy |

---

### **Key Concepts**

✔️ Wavelength and frequency are inversely related when wave speed is constant:  
$$ \lambda \propto \frac{1}{f} $$

✔️ Amplitude is independent of wavelength and frequency — it relates only to energy.

✔️ In a given medium (like air or vacuum), wave speed is fixed, so changing frequency affects wavelength, and vice versa.

---

### **Applications**

🔊 **Sound Waves**:  
- High frequency → high pitch  
- High amplitude → loud sound  

📡 **Electromagnetic Waves**:  
- Short wavelength (e.g., gamma rays) → high energy  
- Long wavelength (e.g., radio waves) → low energy  

🌊 **Water Waves**:  
- Tall waves = high amplitude  
- Close ripples = high frequency, short wavelength

---

### **Important Notes**

⚠️ Amplitude does **not** affect wave speed in a uniform medium.  
⚠️ Frequency remains constant when a wave moves from one medium to another — wavelength adjusts instead.
