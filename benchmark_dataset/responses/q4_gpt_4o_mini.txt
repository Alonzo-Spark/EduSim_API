To derive the equation \( s = ut + \frac{1}{2} a t^2 \), we start from the basic definitions and equations of motion in physics. 

### Introduction
This equation describes the displacement \( s \) of an object under uniform acceleration \( a \) over a time period \( t \), starting from an initial velocity \( u \).

### Key Concepts
1. **Initial Velocity (\( u \))**: The velocity of the object at time \( t = 0 \).
2. **Acceleration (\( a \))**: The rate of change of velocity of the object.
3. **Time (\( t \))**: The duration for which the object has been moving.

### Derivation Steps

1. **Velocity as a Function of Time**:
   The velocity \( v \) of an object at any time \( t \) can be expressed as:
   \[
   v = u + at
   \]
   This equation states that the final velocity is equal to the initial velocity plus the product of acceleration and time.

2. **Displacement**:
   Displacement \( s \) can be calculated as the average velocity multiplied by time. The average velocity when acceleration is constant is given by:
   \[
   \text{Average Velocity} = \frac{u + v}{2}
   \]
   Substituting for \( v \):
   \[
   \text{Average Velocity} = \frac{u + (u + at)}{2} = \frac{2u + at}{2} = u + \frac{1}{2} at
   \]

3. **Calculating Displacement**:
   The displacement \( s \) over time \( t \) is then:
   \[
   s = \text{Average Velocity} \times t = \left( u + \frac{1}{2} at \right) t
   \]

   Expanding this gives:
   \[
   s = ut + \frac{1}{2} at^2
   \]

### Conclusion
Thus, we have derived the relation:
\[
s = ut + \frac{1}{2} a t^2
\]
