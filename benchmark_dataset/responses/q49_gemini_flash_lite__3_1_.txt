**Introduction**

This section will introduce the concept of voltage, current, and resistance, and how they relate to each other.

**Definition**

Voltage is the electric potential difference between two points. It is the driving force that causes electric current to flow. Current is the rate of flow of electric charge. Resistance is the opposition to the flow of electric current.

**Key Concepts**

*   **Ohm's Law:** The fundamental relationship between voltage, current, and resistance.
*   **Units:**
    *   Voltage (V) is measured in Volts (V).
    *   Current (I) is measured in Amperes (A).
    *   Resistance (R) is measured in Ohms (Ω).

**Disadvantages**

Now, let's solve the problem:

**Problem**

A current of 2 A flows through a resistor of 10 Ω. Calculate the voltage.

**Solution**

We will use Ohm's Law: V = I * R

*   I (Current) = 2 A
*   R (Resistance) = 10 Ω

**Calculation**

V = 2 A \* 10 Ω = 20 V

**Answer**

The voltage across the resistor is 20 V.
