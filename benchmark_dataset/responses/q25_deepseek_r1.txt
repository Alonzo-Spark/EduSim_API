Astronauts experience **apparent weightlessness** in space due to **continuous free-fall motion**, not because gravity disappears. Here's a structured breakdown:

---

### 🚀 Quick Definition  
**Weightlessness in space** occurs when astronauts and their spacecraft are both accelerating toward Earth at the same rate due to gravity, resulting in **no normal force** (the force you feel as "weight" on Earth).

---

### 🔍 Key Physics Explained  
1. **Gravity Still Exists**:  
   - At the ISS's orbit (~400 km up), gravity is **90%** of Earth's surface strength.  
   - Without orbital motion, the ISS would fall to Earth in minutes.

2. **Orbital Motion = Controlled Falling**:  
   - The spacecraft moves sideways so fast that Earth's curvature "drops away" beneath it as it falls.  
   - Example: Throw a ball horizontally; it arcs toward Earth. At ~28,000 km/h (orbital speed), the arc matches Earth’s curvature → perpetual free-fall.

---

### ⚖️ Physics of Apparent Weightlessness  
When **acceleration due to gravity** is the **only force** acting:  
- Astronauts and the spacecraft accelerate at **identical rates** (\(g = \frac{GM}{r^2}\)).  
- No compression occurs between them → **zero perceived weight**.

---

### 🌌 Formula: Orbital Motion & "Weightlessness"
$$
\vec{F_g} = \frac{GMm}{r^2} \quad \text{(Gravity)}  
\quad \text{and} \quad  
a = \frac{v^2}{r} \quad \text{(Centripetal acceleration)}
$$

**Balance for stable orbit**:  
$$\frac{GM}{r^2} = \frac{v^2}{r} \implies v = \sqrt{\frac{GM}{r}}$$  
At this speed, gravity provides *exactly* the centripetal force needed for circular motion → **free-fall condition**.

| **Variable** | **Meaning**                          | **Value in ISS Orbit**      |
|--------------|--------------------------------------|-----------------------------|
| \(G\)        | Gravitational constant               | \(6.67 \times 10^{-11} \text{N·m}^2/\text{kg}^2\) |
| \(M\)        | Earth's mass                         | \(5.97 \times 10^{24} \text{kg}\) |
| \(r\)        | Distance from Earth's center         | \(\approx 6,770 \text{ km}\) (Earth radius + 400 km) |
| \(v\)        | Orbital velocity                     | \(\approx 7.66 \text{ km/s}\) |

---

### 🌍 Real-World Examples  
1. **International Space Station (ISS)**:  
   - Orbits Earth every 90 min, in constant free-fall.  
   - Astronauts float because they’re falling at the **same rate** as the ISS.

2. **"Vomit Comet" Aircraft**:  
   - Parabolic flights create **20-30 seconds of weightlessness** by matching free-fall acceleration.

3. **Suborbital vs. Orbital**:  
   - Blue Origin passengers feel weightlessness only when engines cut off (free-fall), but soon re-enter atmosphere.  
   - ISS astronauts experience it **continuously** due to stable orbit.

---

### ❌ Common Misconceptions  
| **Myth**                          | **Reality**                                  |
|-----------------------------------|---------------------------------------------|
| "There's no gravity in space."    | Gravity exists everywhere; it weakens but never vanishes. |
| "Astronauts float due to vacuum." | Vacuum doesn’t cause floating; **motion** does. |
| "Only happens far from Earth."    | Occurs at any altitude if in free-fall (e.g., skydiving). |

---

### 💎 Key Insight  
**Weightlessness ≠ absence of gravity**. It’s the **equivalence** of acceleration due to gravity with the motion of the reference frame (Einstein's principle). On the ISS, **falling forever** feels like floating.

> 🔭 **Think of it like this**: Imagine an elevator in free-fall. You’d float inside it—just like astronauts in their "falling" spacecraft!
