:::section{title="Introduction"}
In the study of fluid mechanics, pressure distribution in a confined liquid follows a specific principle discovered by the French mathematician Blaise Pascal. This principle explains how hydraulic systems can multiply force to lift heavy loads.
:::

:::section{title="Definition"}
**Pascal's Law** states that:
> "The pressure exerted anywhere in a confined incompressible fluid is transmitted equally in all directions throughout the fluid such that the pressure ratio remains the same."
:::

:::section{title="Main Formula"}
$$
P = \frac{F_1}{A_1} = \frac{F_2}{A_2}
$$

**Where:**
*   $P$ = Hydrostatic Pressure
*   $F_1, F_2$ = Force applied to pistons 1 and 2
*   $A_1, A_2$ = Cross-sectional area of pistons 1 and 2

[ Explain Formula: If you apply a small force to a small area, it creates a pressure that can generate a much larger force when acting on a larger area. ]
[ Open Formula Lab ]
:::

:::section{title="Characteristics"}
*   **Uniformity:** Pressure is distributed equally in all directions.
*   **Incompressibility:** The law assumes the fluid cannot be compressed (liquids).
*   **Static Fluid:** The principle applies primarily to fluids at rest.
*   **Force Multiplication:** Allows a small input force to be converted into a large output force.
:::

:::section{title="Applications"}
| Application | Function |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Hydraulic Lift** | Used in service stations to lift heavy vehicles using compressed air/oil. |
| **Hydraulic Brakes** | Transmits foot pedal pressure equally to all wheels of a car. |
| **Hydraulic Press** | Used for crushing objects or forging metal parts with high force. |
| **Hydraulic Jacks** | Portable devices used to lift parts of a vehicle for tire changes. |
:::

:::section{title="Important Notes"}
> [!IMPORTANT]
> Pascal's Law is the fundamental principle behind **Fluidics** and **Hydraulics**. It does not "create" energy; it simply allows for the trade-off between force and distance (Work = Force × Distance).
:::

2. **Numerical:** If a force of $50\,N$ is applied to a piston of area $0.1\,m^2$, what is the force generated on a second piston with an area of $1.0\,m^2$?
3. **Application-based:** Explain the role of the master cylinder and slave cylinder in a car's braking system in the context of Pascal's Law.
