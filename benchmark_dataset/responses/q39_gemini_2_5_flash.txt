Let's explore the fundamental properties of waves!

---

### Quick Definition

A **wave** is a disturbance that propagates through space and time, transferring energy without necessarily transferring matter.

---

### Key Concepts

Waves are ubiquitous in nature, from the ripples on a pond to the light that allows us to see. Understanding their fundamental properties—wavelength, frequency, and amplitude—is crucial for comprehending how they behave and interact with their surroundings.

---

### Wavelength ($\lambda$)

<div class="card">
  <h4>Definition</h4>
  <p>The **wavelength** ($\lambda$) of a wave is the spatial period of a periodic wave – the distance over which the wave's shape repeats. It is the distance between consecutive corresponding points of the same phase, such as two adjacent crests, troughs, or zero crossings.</p>

  <h4>Units</h4>
  <p>Wavelength is typically measured in units of distance, such as meters (m), centimeters (cm), or nanometers (nm).</p>

  <h4>Visual Representation</h4>
  <p>Imagine a snapshot of a wave in time. The wavelength is the distance you'd measure from one peak to the next peak, or one trough to the next trough.</p>
</div>

---

### Frequency ($f$ or $\nu$)

<div class="card">
  <h4>Definition</h4>
  <p>The **frequency** ($f$ or $\nu$) of a wave is the number of complete cycles or oscillations that pass a given point per unit of time. It quantifies how often a repeating event occurs.</p>

  <h4>Units</h4>
  <p>Frequency is measured in Hertz (Hz), where 1 Hz is equal to one cycle per second ($s^{-1}$). Other units include kilohertz (kHz), megahertz (MHz), and gigahertz (GHz).</p>

  <h4>Visual Representation</h4>
  <p>If you were to stand still and watch a wave pass by, the frequency tells you how many wave crests (or troughs) would pass you in one second.</p>
</div>

---

### Amplitude ($A$)

<div class="card">
  <h4>Definition</h4>
  <p>The **amplitude** ($A$) of a wave is the maximum displacement or distance moved by a point on a vibrating body or wave measured from its equilibrium position. It represents the intensity or energy of the wave.</p>

  <h4>Units</h4>
  <p>The units of amplitude depend on the type of wave. For water waves, it's a distance (e.g., meters). For sound waves, it's a pressure difference (e.g., Pascals). For electromagnetic waves, it's related to the strength of the electric or magnetic field.</p>

  <h4>Visual Representation</h4>
  <p>For a wave on a string, the amplitude is how high the peak rises above the undisturbed string, or how low the trough dips below it.</p>
</div>

### Main Formula

The relationship between wavelength, frequency, and the speed of a wave is fundamental:

$$
v = \lambda f
$$

### Important Notes

*   **Inverse Relationship:** For a constant wave speed, wavelength and frequency are inversely proportional. This means if the wavelength increases, the frequency decreases, and vice-versa.
*   **Energy and Amplitude:** The energy carried by a wave is generally proportional to the square of its amplitude. A larger amplitude means more energy.
*   **Wave Speed:** The speed of a wave depends on the medium through which it travels. For example, sound travels faster in water than in air, and light travels fastest in a vacuum.
