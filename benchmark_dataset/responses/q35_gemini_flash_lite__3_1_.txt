    <div class="card-title">Key Concepts</div>
    <ul>
      <li><b>Phase Transitions:</b> Changes in the physical state of a substance, such as melting, freezing, boiling, condensation, sublimation, and deposition.</li>
      <li><b>Specific Latent Heat:</b> The amount of heat required to change the state of 1 kg of a substance at a constant temperature.  There are two main types:
      <ul>
          <li><b>Specific Latent Heat of Fusion (\(L_f\)):</b>  The heat required to change 1 kg of a substance from solid to liquid at its melting point.</li>
          <li><b>Specific Latent Heat of Vaporization (\(L_v\)):</b> The heat required to change 1 kg of a substance from liquid to gas at its boiling point.</li>
      </ul>
      </li>
    </ul>

  <div class="card">
    <div class="card-title">Change of State Explained</div>
    <p>When a substance changes state, the added or removed heat energy doesn't change the temperature of the substance. Instead, it alters the internal energy by breaking or forming bonds between the molecules or atoms. For example:</p>
    <ul>
      <li><b>Melting:</b> When a solid absorbs heat, the molecules gain enough energy to overcome the forces holding them in a fixed position.  They break free and move more randomly, transitioning from solid to liquid.</li>
      <li><b>Boiling:</b> When a liquid absorbs heat, the molecules gain enough energy to overcome the intermolecular forces holding them together. They escape into the gaseous phase.</li>
      <li><b>Freezing:</b>  The reverse of melting; heat is released as the liquid transforms into a solid.</li>
      <li><b>Condensation:</b> The reverse of boiling; heat is released as a gas transforms into a liquid.</li>
      <li><b>Sublimation:</b> A solid directly changes into a gas (e.g., dry ice).</li>
      <li><b>Deposition:</b> A gas directly changes into a solid.</li>
    </ul>
  </div>

  <div class="card">
    <div class="card-title">Characteristics</div>
    <ul>
      <li><b>Constant Temperature:</b> During a phase change, the temperature remains constant.</li>
      <li><b>Energy Absorption/Release:</b> Heat is either absorbed (endothermic process, e.g., melting, boiling) or released (exothermic process, e.g., freezing, condensation).</li>
      <li><b>Bond Breaking/Forming:</b> The energy is used to break or form bonds between molecules.</li>
    </ul>
