:::section{title="Introduction"}
Surface tension is a fundamental property of liquids that causes their surfaces to behave like a stretched elastic membrane. It is the reason why small insects can walk on water and why raindrops form spherical droplets.
:::

:::section{title="Definition"}
**Surface Tension ($\gamma$)** is defined as the force acting per unit length perpendicular to an imaginary line drawn on the surface of a liquid. It is a result of **cohesive forces** between liquid molecules.

Inside the bulk of a liquid, a molecule is pulled equally in all directions by neighboring molecules. However, at the surface, molecules experience a net inward pull because there are no liquid molecules above them to balance the force.
:::

:::formula-card{title="Mathematical Definition"}
$$
\gamma = \frac{F}{L}
$$

**Variables:**
*   $\gamma$: Surface Tension (N/m)
*   $F$: Force acting parallel to the surface (Newtons)
*   $L$: Length over which the force acts (Meters)

[ Explain Formula ]
The formula represents the energy required to increase the surface area of a liquid. In terms of energy, it can also be expressed as $J/m^2$.

:::section{title="Factors Affecting Surface Tension"}
The magnitude of surface tension depends on the nature of the liquid and external environmental conditions:

| Factor | Effect on Surface Tension | Reason |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Temperature** | Decreases as Temp increases | Increased kinetic energy weakens intermolecular cohesive forces. |
| **Impurities (Soluble)** | Increases | Highly soluble substances (like salt in water) increase cohesive forces. |
| **Impurities (Insoluble)** | Decreases | Sparingly soluble substances (like soap or detergent) disrupt the hydrogen bonding. |
| **Nature of Liquid** | Varies | Stronger intermolecular forces (e.g., Mercury) result in higher surface tension. |
| **Oxygen/Aeration** | Decreases | Presence of gases at the surface can slightly reduce the net inward pull. |
:::

:::section{title="Important Notes"}
*   **The Critical Temperature:** At the critical temperature of a liquid, the surface tension becomes zero as the distinction between the liquid and vapor phases disappears.
*   **Surfactants:** Short for "Surface Active Agents," these are substances like detergents that significantly lower the surface tension of water to help it "wet" surfaces and lift dirt.
:::
