  <div class="card">
    <div class="section-title">Introduction</div>
    <p>
      This interactive textbook explores how the scaling of gravitational acceleration affects the range of a block sliding down an inclined plane. We'll delve into the physics principles at play, examine relevant formulas, and provide interactive elements to deepen your understanding.
    </p>

  <div class="card">
    <div class="section-title">Definition</div>
    <p>
      An inclined plane is a flat surface tilted at an angle (θ) relative to the horizontal. When a block slides down this plane, its motion is influenced by gravity, the angle of the incline, and friction.  The range of the block is the horizontal distance it travels before coming to a stop.
    </p>
  </div>

  <div class="card">
    <div class="section-title">Key Concepts</div>
    <ul>
      <li><b>Gravitational Acceleration (g):</b> The acceleration caused by gravity, typically 9.8 m/s² on Earth.</li>
      <li><b>Normal Force (N):</b> The force exerted by the inclined plane on the block, perpendicular to the surface.</li>
      <li><b>Friction:</b> A force that opposes the motion of the block, dependent on the coefficient of friction (µ) and the normal force.</li>
      <li><b>Angle of Inclination (θ):</b> The angle of the inclined plane, which affects the components of gravitational force acting on the block.</li>
    </ul>
  </div>

  <div class="formula-card">
    <div class="title">Main Formula - Acceleration Down the Incline</div>
    <div class="formula">
      $$a = g(\sin(\theta) - \mu \cos(\theta))$$
    </div>

  </div>

  <div class="card">
    <div class="section-title">Formula Explanation</div>
    <p>
      The acceleration (a) of the block down the incline depends on the gravitational acceleration (g), the angle of the incline (θ), and the coefficient of friction (µ). The component of gravity acting down the incline is \(g \sin(\theta)\), and the friction force opposes the motion with a magnitude of \(\mu N\), where \(N = mg \cos(\theta)\).
    </p>
