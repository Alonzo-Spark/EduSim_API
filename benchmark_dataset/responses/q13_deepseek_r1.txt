To find the acceleration of a body when a force is applied, we use **Newton's Second Law of Motion**. Here's a step-by-step solution:

### Step 1: Newton's Second Law
The law states that the acceleration (\(a\)) of an object is directly proportional to the net force (\(F\)) acting on it and inversely proportional to its mass (\(m\)). The formula is:

**Formula**  
\[
F = m \cdot a
\]

### Step 2: Rearrange the Formula
Solve for acceleration (\(a\)):  
\[
a = \frac{F}{m}
\]

### Step 3: Substitute Values
Given:  
- Force, \(F = 50  \text{N}\) (Newtons)  
- Mass, \(m = 10  \text{kg}\) (kilograms)  

Substitute into the formula:  
\[
a = \frac{50  \text{N}}{10  \text{kg}}
\]

### Step 4: Calculate
Simplify the expression:  
\[
a = 5  \text{m/s}^2
\]

### Final Answer  
The acceleration of the body is **\(5  \text{m/s}^2\)**.

---

### Key Notes  
1. **Units**:  
   - Newtons (\(\text{N}\)) are equivalent to \(\text{kg} \cdot \text{m/s}^2\), so \(\frac{\text{N}}{\text{kg}} = \text{m/s}^2\).  
   - Always ensure units are consistent. Here, they are (force in \(\text{N}\), mass in \(\text{kg}\)).  

2. **Interpretation**:  
   - An acceleration of \(5  \text{m/s}^2\) means the body's velocity increases by \(5  \text{m/s}\) every second.  

3. **Direction**:  
   - Acceleration occurs in the direction of the applied force.
