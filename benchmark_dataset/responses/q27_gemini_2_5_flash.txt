## Pascal's Law and its Applications

Pascal's law is a fundamental principle in fluid mechanics that describes how pressure is transmitted through an enclosed incompressible fluid.

### Quick Definition

Pascal's law states that **a pressure change at any point in a confined incompressible fluid is transmitted throughout the fluid such that the same change occurs everywhere.**

This means that if you apply pressure to one part of a fluid, that pressure is felt equally throughout the entire fluid, regardless of the shape or size of the container.

### Main Formula

Pascal's law can be expressed mathematically as:

$$
P = \frac{F}{A}
$$

Where:
*   $P$ is the pressure exerted on the fluid (measured in Pascals, Pa)
*   $F$ is the force applied (measured in Newtons, N)
*   $A$ is the area over which the force is applied (measured in square meters, $m^2$)

[ Explain Formula ]
[ Open Formula Lab ]

### Key Concepts

*   **Incompressible Fluid:** A fluid whose volume does not change significantly under pressure. Liquids are generally considered incompressible.
*   **Confined Fluid:** The fluid is enclosed within a container, preventing it from escaping.
*   **Pressure Transmission:** The applied pressure is transmitted undiminished to every portion of the fluid and the walls of the containing vessel.

### Important Notes

*   Pascal's law is valid only for **incompressible fluids**. For compressible fluids (like gases), pressure changes can lead to changes in volume.
*   The law applies to **confined fluids**, meaning the fluid is enclosed within a system, preventing it from expanding freely.
*   It's the **change in pressure** that is transmitted equally, not necessarily the absolute pressure at every point (which can vary due to gravity).

### Summary

Pascal's law is a fundamental principle that explains how pressure is uniformly transmitted through an enclosed liquid. This principle is vital for the operation of hydraulic systems, which leverage this property to multiply force and perform heavy-duty tasks in various industries and everyday applications.
