### 📘 CHAPTER: ELECTROSTATICS

┌──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│ **Quick Definition**                                     │
│                                                          │
│ **Electric Field:** A physical region around a charged   │
│ particle that exerts force on other charged particles.   │
│                                                          │
│ **Electric Potential:** The amount of work needed to     │
│ move a unit positive charge from infinity to a point.    │
└──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘

### ⚡ Key Concepts

| Concept | Description | SI Unit | Type |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Electric Field ($E$)** | Force per unit charge at a specific point. | $N/C$ or $V/m$ | Vector |
| **Electric Potential ($V$)** | Potential energy per unit charge. | $Volts$ ($V$) | Scalar |

### 🔍 Characteristics

*   **Directionality:** Electric field lines point **away** from positive charges and **toward** negative charges.
*   **Superposition:** Both fields and potentials from multiple charges can be added (vectors for field, scalars for potential).
*   **Work-Energy Link:** Electric potential is the "electrical pressure" that causes charges to move, creating current.

### 💡 Important Notes
> [!NOTE]
> While **Electric Field** tells you how hard a charge will be pushed (Force), **Electric Potential** tells you how much energy is stored at that location (Energy). They are related by the gradient: $E = -\frac{dV}{dr}$.

### 📝 Suggested Questions

1.  **Conceptual:** Why is the electric field zero inside a hollow conducting sphere, but the potential is constant?
2.  **Calculation:** If the potential at a point is $10V$, how much work is required to move a $2C$ charge to that point?
3.  **Relationship:** How does the electric field change if you double the distance from a point charge?
