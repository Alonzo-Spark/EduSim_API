### Derivation of the Law of Conservation of Momentum

Consider two objects interacting — for example, colliding.

Let:  
- Object 1 have mass $ m_1 $, initial velocity $ u_1 $, final velocity $ v_1 $  
- Object 2 have mass $ m_2 $, initial velocity $ u_2 $, final velocity $ v_2 $

Assume they are isolated — no external forces act on them.

During collision, by **Newton’s Third Law**:  
The force $ F_{12} $ that object 1 exerts on object 2 is equal and opposite to the force $ F_{21} $ that object 2 exerts on object 1.

$$
F_{12} = -F_{21}
$$

<FormulaCard
  formula="F_{12} = -F_{21}"
  title="Newton's Third Law"
  description="For every action, there is an equal and opposite reaction."
  variables={{
    F12: "Force on object 2 by object 1",
    F21: "Force on object 1 by object 2"
  }}
  actions={["Explain Formula"]}
/>

Now, apply **Newton’s Second Law**:  
Force = rate of change of momentum.

For object 1:
$$
F_{21} = \frac{m_1 v_1 - m_1 u_1}{t}
$$

For object 2:
$$
F_{12} = \frac{m_2 v_2 - m_2 u_2}{t}
$$

But $ F_{12} = -F_{21} $, so:

$$
\frac{m_2 v_2 - m_2 u_2}{t} = -\frac{m_1 v_1 - m_1 u_1}{t}
$$

Multiply both sides by $ t $ (time of interaction):

$$
m_2 v_2 - m_2 u_2 = - (m_1 v_1 - m_1 u_1)
$$

Rearranging:

$$
m_1 u_1 + m_2 u_2 = m_1 v_1 + m_2 v_2
$$

### Characteristics

| Feature | Description |
|--------|-------------|
| **Applies to** | Isolated systems (no net external force) |
| **Vector Law** | Momentum conservation applies in each direction (x, y, z) |
| **Independent of Energy** | Momentum is always conserved in isolated systems, but kinetic energy may not be |
| **Universal** | Holds true in classical mechanics, relativity, and quantum physics |

---

### Applications

- **Collisions**: Car crashes, billiard balls, particle physics  
- **Recoil**: Gun kicks back when fired  
- **Rocket Propulsion**: Rockets move forward by expelling gas backward  
- **Sports**: Analyzing tackles in football, hits in baseball  

---

### Important Notes

⚠️ **Momentum is conserved only when the net external force is zero.**  
If friction, air resistance, or other forces act, momentum may not be conserved unless you include the environment (e.g., Earth).

⚠️ **Direction matters!** Use positive/negative signs for opposite directions.

⚠️ **Even when kinetic energy is lost (inelastic collision), momentum is still conserved.**

---

### Summary

- Momentum: $ \vec{p} = m\vec{v} $  
- It’s a vector and depends on mass and velocity  
- Conservation arises from Newton’s Third Law and no external forces  
- Total momentum before = Total momentum after  
- A powerful tool for analyzing motion interactions
