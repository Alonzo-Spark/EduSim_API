┌───────────────────────────┐  
│ Introduction              │  
│                           │  
│ The electric force between two point charges is described by Coulomb's Law. This law states that the force between two charges is directly proportional to the product of their magnitudes and inversely proportional to the square of the distance between them. │  
└───────────────────────────┘  

┌───────────────────────────┐  
│ Definition                │  
│                           │  
│ Coulomb's Law provides a quantitative description of the electric force between charged objects. It is essential in electrostatics and forms the foundation of classical electromagnetism.│  
└───────────────────────────┘  

┌───────────────────────────┐  
│ Key Concepts              │  
│                           │  
│ - **Charge (q)**: A property of matter that causes it to experience a force in an electromagnetic field.  
│ - **Electric Force (F)**: The push or pull experienced by a charge due to the presence of another charge.  
│ - **Distance (r)**: The separation between the two charges.  
└───────────────────────────┘  

┌───────────────────────────┐  
│ Derivation of Electric Force │  
│                           │  
│ Coulomb's Law is mathematically expressed as:  
│  
│ $$  
│ F = k \frac{|q_1 q_2|}{r^2}  
│ $$  
│  
│ where:  
│ - \( F \) is the magnitude of the electric force between the two charges.  
│ - \( k \) is Coulomb's constant (\( k \approx 8.99 \times 10^9 \, \text{N m}^2/\text{C}^2 \)).  
│ - \( q_1 \) and \( q_2 \) are the magnitudes of the two charges.  
│ - \( r \) is the distance between the centers of the two charges.  
└───────────────────────────┘

┌───────────────────────────┐  
│ Applications              │  
│                           │  
│ - Understanding electric forces in charged systems.  
│ - Calculating forces in atomic and molecular interactions.  
│ - Designing electrical devices and understanding electrostatic phenomena.  
└───────────────────────────┘  

┌───────────────────────────┐  
│ Advantages                │  
│                           │  
│ - Provides a clear mathematical framework for calculating electric forces.  
│ - Applicable to point charges and can be extended to continuous charge distributions using integration.  
└───────────────────────────┘  

┌───────────────────────────┐  
│ Disadvantages             │  
│                           │  
│ - Only applicable to point charges and does not consider other forces (like magnetic forces) in dynamic situations.  
│ - Assumes charges are at rest and does not account for relativistic effects.  
└───────────────────────────┘  

┌───────────────────────────┐  
│ Important Notes           │  
│                           │  
│ - The direction of the force is along the line connecting the two charges.  
│ - Like charges repel, while opposite charges attract.  
└───────────────────────────┘  

┌───────────────────────────┐  
│ Summary                  │  
│                           │  
│ The electric force between two charges is governed by Coulomb's Law, which quantifies the force based on the charges' magnitudes and the distance between them. This foundational principle is crucial in understanding electric interactions in physics.  
└───────────────────────────┘
