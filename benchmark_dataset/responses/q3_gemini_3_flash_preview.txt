### Distance vs. Displacement: Physics Module

:::section{title="Quick Definition"}
In physics, while both terms describe how far an object has moved, they represent fundamentally different types of quantities. **Distance** is the total ground covered, whereas **Displacement** is the net change in position.
:::

:::section{title="Key Concepts"}
| Feature | Distance | Displacement |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Definition** | Total length of the actual path traveled. | The shortest straight-line distance between start and end. |
| **Quantity Type** | Scalar (Magnitude only) | Vector (Magnitude + Direction) |
| **Path Dependency** | Depends on the path taken. | Independent of path; depends only on endpoints. |
| **Value** | Always positive or zero. | Can be positive, negative, or zero. |
| **Symbol** | $d$ | $\Delta x$ or $\vec{s}$ |
:::

:::section{title="Formula Card"}
**The Displacement Equation**
$$
\Delta x = x_f - x_i
$$

**[ Explain Formula ]**
*   $\Delta x$: Displacement (change in position).
*   $x_f$: Final position coordinate.
*   $x_i$: Initial position coordinate.

**[ Open Formula Lab ]**
*   *Scenario A:* If you walk 5m East and 5m West, $x_f = x_i$, making displacement $0$.
*   *Scenario B:* Distance is calculated by $|d_1| + |d_2| + ... + |d_n|$.
:::

:::section{title="Characteristics"}
*   **Distance** can never be less than the magnitude of displacement.
*   If an object moves in a straight line in a single direction, **Distance = Displacement**.
*   If an object returns to its starting point (like a lap around a track), the **Distance** is the track length, but **Displacement** is exactly **zero**.
:::

:::section{title="Comparative Example"}
**The Circular Track Scenario**
Imagine an athlete running one complete lap around a circular track with a radius of $50m$.

**1. Distance Calculation:**
The athlete covered the circumference of the circle.
$$
d = 2\pi r = 2 \times 3.14 \times 50 = 314m
$$

**2. Displacement Calculation:**
The athlete started and ended at the exact same point.
$$
\Delta x = 0m
$$
:::

:::section{title="Important Notes"}
> **Direction Matters:** When stating displacement, you must include direction (e.g., "5 meters **North**"). Distance does not require this.
> 
> **Odometer vs. GPS:** A car's odometer measures **distance**. A GPS displacement calculation measures the "as the crow flies" straight line between two coordinates.
:::
