┌───────────────────────────┐
│ Quick Definition          │
│                           │
│ The equivalent resistance  │
│ in a series circuit is the │
│ total resistance faced by  │
│ the current.              │
└───────────────────────────┘

┌───────────────────────────┐
│ Key Concepts              │
│                           │
│ In a series circuit, the  │
│ total resistance is the   │
│ sum of individual         │
│ resistances.              │
└───────────────────────────┘

┌───────────────────────────┐
│ Derivation                │
│                           │
│ Consider resistors \( R_1, R_2, R_3, \ldots, R_n \) connected in series. The total voltage \( V \) across the combination is the sum of the voltages across each resistor. Using Ohm's law, we can express this as:  │
│                           │
│ $$ V = I \cdot R_{eq} $$  │
│                           │
│ The voltage across each resistor can be expressed as:  │
│ $$ V_1 = I \cdot R_1 $$  │
│ $$ V_2 = I \cdot R_2 $$  │
│ $$ V_3 = I \cdot R_3 $$  │
│                           │
│ Thus, the total voltage is: │
│ $$ V = V_1 + V_2 + V_3 + \ldots + V_n $$ │
│                           │
│ Substituting the expressions for \( V_1, V_2, \) and \( V_3 \): │
│                           │
│ $$ V = I \cdot R_1 + I \cdot R_2 + I \cdot R_3 + \ldots + I \cdot R_n $$ │
│                           │
│ Factoring out the current \( I \): │
│                           │
│ $$ V = I (R_1 + R_2 + R_3 + \ldots + R_n) $$ │
│                           │
│ Therefore, the equivalent resistance \( R_{eq} \) is given by: │
│                           │
│ $$ R_{eq} = R_1 + R_2 + R_3 + \ldots + R_n $$ │
└───────────────────────────┘
