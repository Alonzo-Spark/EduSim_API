# Acceleration Due to Gravity

## 🟦 Quick Definition

> **Acceleration due to gravity** is the acceleration experienced by any object due to the gravitational force exerted by the Earth (or any massive body), directed toward the center of that body.

---

## 🟧 Starting Point — Newton's Laws

Two laws work together here:

**Newton's Law of Universal Gravitation** tells us the force between two masses:

$$F = \frac{GMm}{r^2}$$

**Newton's Second Law** connects force to acceleration:

$$F = ma$$

---

## 🟩 The Derivation

### Step 1 — Set the two forces equal

An object of mass $m$ sits on or near Earth's surface. Earth has mass $M$ and radius $R$.

The gravitational force on the object is:

$$F = \frac{GMm}{R^2}$$

By Newton's Second Law, this same force causes acceleration $g$:

$$F = mg$$

### Step 2 — Equate and cancel

$$mg = \frac{GMm}{R^2}$$

The mass of the object $m$ appears on **both sides** — it cancels completely:

$$g = \frac{GM}{R^2}$$

---

## ⭐ Main Formula Card

$$\boxed{g = \frac{GM}{R^2}}$$

| Symbol | Meaning | Value / Unit |
|--------|---------|-------------|
| $g$ | Acceleration due to gravity | $\approx 9.8 \ \text{m/s}^2$ |
| $G$ | Universal Gravitational Constant | $6.674 \times 10^{-11} \ \text{N m}^2\text{kg}^{-2}$ |
| $M$ | Mass of Earth | $5.972 \times 10^{24} \ \text{kg}$ |
| $R$ | Radius of Earth | $6.371 \times 10^{6} \ \text{m}$ |

---

## 🟪 Numerical Verification

Substituting known values:

$$g = \frac{(6.674 \times 10^{-11}) \times (5.972 \times 10^{24})}{(6.371 \times 10^{6})^2}$$

$$g = \frac{3.985 \times 10^{14}}{4.059 \times 10^{13}}$$

$$g \approx 9.8 \ \text{m/s}^2 \checkmark$$

---

## 🔑 Key Insight — Why mass of object doesn't matter

> The mass $m$ of the falling object **cancels out** in the derivation.
> This means **all objects fall with the same acceleration** regardless of their mass — which is exactly what Galileo demonstrated by dropping objects from the Leaning Tower of Pisa.

---

## 🟥 Variation with Height

If an object is at height $h$ above Earth's surface, replace $R$ with $(R + h)$:

$$g_h = \frac{GM}{(R+h)^2}$$

As $h$ increases → $g_h$ **decreases** (inverse square relationship).

---

## 📊 How g Varies

| Location | Value of $g$ |
|----------|-------------|
| Earth's surface (average) | $9.8 \ \text{m/s}^2$ |
| At the poles | $9.83 \ \text{m/s}^2$ |
| At the equator | $9.78 \ \text{m/s}^2$ |
| At height = $R$ (one Earth radius up) | $2.45 \ \text{m/s}^2$ |
| On the Moon | $1.62 \ \text{m/s}^2$ |

---

## 📝 Summary of Derivation

```
Newton's Gravitation Law   +   Newton's 2nd Law
        ↓                            ↓
   F = GMm/R²              F = mg
        ↓
   Set equal → mg = GMm/R²
        ↓
   Cancel m → g = GM/R²
```
