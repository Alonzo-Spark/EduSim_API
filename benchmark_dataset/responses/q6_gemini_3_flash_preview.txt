### Projectile Motion: A Comprehensive Guide

---

<div id="introduction-card">
  <h4>Introduction</h4>
  <p>Projectile motion is a form of motion experienced by an object or particle (a projectile) that is projected near the Earth's surface and moves along a curved path under the action of gravity only. The path followed by a projectile is called its <b>trajectory</b>, which takes the shape of a parabola.</p>
</div>

---

<div id="definition-card">
  <h4>Quick Definition</h4>
  <p><strong>Projectile Motion:</strong> The two-dimensional motion of an object thrown into the air, subject only to the acceleration of gravity ($g$) and ignoring air resistance.</p>
</div>

---

<div id="formula-card-1">
  <h4>Main Formula: The Trajectory Equation</h4>
  <div style="text-align: center; padding: 20px 0;">
    $$ y = x \tan(\theta) - \frac{g x^2}{2 u^2 \cos^2(\theta)} $$
  </div>
  <hr>
  <p><b>Explain Formula:</b> This equation describes the vertical position ($y$) as a function of horizontal distance ($x$), proving the path is a mathematical parabola.</p>

<div id="characteristics-card">
  <h4>Key Characteristics</h4>
  <ul>
    <li><b>Independence of Motion:</b> The horizontal and vertical components of motion are independent of each other.</li>
    <li><b>Constant Horizontal Velocity:</b> Since no horizontal force acts on the object (ignoring air friction), $v_x$ remains constant throughout the flight.</li>
    <li><b>Constant Vertical Acceleration:</b> The object experiences a constant downward acceleration due to gravity ($g \approx 9.8 \, m/s^2$).</li>
    <li><b>Symmetry:</b> If launched and landed at the same height, the time of ascent equals the time of descent.</li>
  </ul>
</div>

<div id="formula-card-2">
  <h4>Main Formula: Horizontal Range</h4>
  <div style="text-align: center; padding: 20px 0;">
    $$ R = \frac{u^2 \sin(2\theta)}{g} $$
  </div>
  <hr>
  <p><b>Explain Formula:</b> Calculates the total horizontal distance covered. Range is maximum when the launch angle $\theta = 45^\circ$.</p>

<div id="important-notes-card">
  <h4>Important Notes</h4>
  <p>⚠️ <b>The "Hidden" Zero:</b> At the highest point of the trajectory, the vertical velocity ($v_y$) is exactly <b>zero</b>, but the horizontal velocity ($v_x$) is still $u \cos(\theta)$.</p>
  <p>⚠️ <b>Complementary Angles:</b> Two different launch angles $\theta$ and $(90^\circ - \theta)$ will result in the <b>same</b> horizontal range, provided the initial velocity is identical.</p>
</div>
