## Potential Energy

Here's how we calculate the potential energy of the body:

### Quick Definition

Potential energy is the energy an object possesses due to its position or state. In the context of gravity, it's the energy stored in an object due to its height above a reference point.

### Main Formula

$$PE = mgh$$

Where:
*   $PE$ is the potential energy (measured in Joules, J)
*   $m$ is the mass of the object (measured in kilograms, kg)
*   $g$ is the acceleration due to gravity (approximately $9.8 \text{ m/s}^2$ on Earth)
*   $h$ is the height above the reference point (measured in meters, m)

### Given Values

| Quantity                      | Symbol | Value                |
| :---------------------------- | :----- | :------------------- |
| Mass of the body              | $m$    | $5 \text{ kg}$       |
| Height                        | $h$    | $10 \text{ m}$       |
| Acceleration due to gravity   | $g$    | $9.8 \text{ m/s}^2$  |

### Calculation

We can now substitute the given values into the potential energy formula:

$$PE = mgh$$
$$PE = (5 \text{ kg}) \times (9.8 \text{ m/s}^2) \times (10 \text{ m})$$
$$PE = 490 \text{ J}$$

### Result

The potential energy of the body is $490 \text{ Joules}$.

### Important Notes

*   The reference point for height is crucial. Potential energy is relative to this chosen point. If the body were lifted from a different starting height, its potential energy relative to the ground would change.
*   This calculation assumes we are on Earth, where the acceleration due to gravity is approximately $9.8 \text{ m/s}^2$. On other planets or in space, the value of $g$ would be different.
