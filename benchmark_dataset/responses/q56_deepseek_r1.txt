### Restitution in Rigid Body Collisions

#### 🎯 Quick Definition
**Restitution** is a measure of how much kinetic energy is conserved after a collision between two rigid bodies. It's quantified by the **coefficient of restitution (COR)**, ranging from **0** (perfectly inelastic, no bounce) to **1** (perfectly elastic, maximum bounce).

---

#### 📐 Restitution Formula
The coefficient of restitution \( e \) is defined as:  
$$  
e = -\frac{v_{\text{rel, after}}}{v_{\text{rel, before}}}  
$$  
where:
- \( v_{\text{rel, before}} \) = Relative velocity **before** collision (\( v_1 - v_2 \)),  
- \( v_{\text{rel, after}} \) = Relative velocity **after** collision (\( v_1' - v_2' \)).  

```katex
e = -\frac{v_{1}^{\prime} - v_{2}^{\prime}}{v_{1} - v_{2}}
```

<details>
<summary>🧠 **Explain Formula**</summary>
- **Negative sign**: Ensures \( e \) stays positive (velocities reverse direction after collision).  
- **Energy link**: For ideal elastic collisions (\( e = 1 \)), kinetic energy is conserved.  
- **Real-world**: Friction/heat reduce \( e \) below 1.  
</details>

---

#### ⚖️ How Restitution Changes Collision Behavior
| Coefficient (\( e \)) | Collision Type          | Bounce Behavior                  | Energy Loss       |
|-----------------------|-------------------------|----------------------------------|-------------------|
| **\( e = 0 \)**       | Perfectly Inelastic     | Bodies stick together           | Maximal loss      |
| **\( 0 < e < 1 \)**   | Inelastic               | Partial bounce (e.g., tennis ball) | Moderate loss    |
| **\( e = 1 \)**       | Perfectly Elastic       | Full bounce (e.g., superball)     | No energy loss   |

---

#### 🔬 Key Effects of Adjusting Restitution
1. **Bounce Height**  
   Higher \( e \) → Greater rebound height.  
   - *Example*: A basketball (\( e ≈ 0.8 \)) bounces higher than clay (\( e ≈ 0.1 \)).

2. **Collision Duration**  
   Lower \( e \) → Longer contact time (energy dissipates slowly).

3. **Post-Impact Velocity**  
   Velocities after collision depend on \( e \):  
   $$  
   v_1' = \frac{m_1 v_1 + m_2 v_2 + m_2 e (v_2 - v_1)}{m_1 + m_2}  
   $$  
   *(Solve similarly for \( v_2' \))*

---

#### 🛠️ Practical Applications
- **Game Physics**:  
  - Low \( e \) → Realistic car crashes (crumple zones).  
  - High \( e \) → Bouncy platforms (platformer games).  
- **Sports Equipment**:  
  Golf balls designed with \( e ≈ 0.8 \) for optimal rebound.  
- **Engineering Safety**:  
  Crash barriers use low-\( e \) materials to absorb impact.  

---

#### ⚠️ Important Notes
- **Material Matters**: Steel has higher \( e \) (0.6–0.8) than rubber (0.3–0.6) despite rubber feeling "bouncier" (due to deformation).  
- **Speed Dependence**: \( e \) often decreases at high velocities (e.g., bullet impacts).  
- **Angular Effects**: Restitution combines with friction to influence spin during oblique collisions.  

---

#### 💡 Summary
> **Restitution directly controls bounce intensity**:  
> - **Increase \( e \)** → More energetic rebounds, less energy loss.  
> - **Decrease \( e \)** → "Mushy" collisions, energy converted to heat/sound.  
>  
> Adjust \( e \) to simulate materials from putty (\( e ≈ 0 \)) to glass (\( e ≈ 0.95 \)).
