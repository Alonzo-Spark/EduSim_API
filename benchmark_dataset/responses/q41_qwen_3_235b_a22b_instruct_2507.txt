### Key Concepts

- Applies only to **point charges** at rest (electrostatics).
- The force acts along the line joining the two charges.
- It is a **central force** and follows Newton's third law (equal and opposite forces).

### Characteristics

- **Inverse-square law**: Force decreases with the square of the distance.
- **Proportional to product of charges**: Larger charges → stronger force.
- **Conservative force**: Work done is path-independent; allows definition of electric potential.

### Direction of Force

- **Like charges** (++ or --) → **Repulsive** force
- **Unlike charges** (+− or −+) → **Attractive** force

### Constant of Proportionality

$$
k = \frac{1}{4\pi \varepsilon_0}
$$

where:
- $ \varepsilon_0 $ = permittivity of free space ≈ $ 8.854 \times 10^{-12} \, \text{C}^2/\text{N·m}^2 $
- $ k \approx 8.99 \times 10^9 \, \text{N·m}^2/\text{C}^2 $

### Vector Form

$$
\vec{F}_{12} = k \frac{q_1 q_2}{r^2} \hat{r}_{12}
$$

where:
- $ \vec{F}_{12} $: force on charge 1 due to charge 2
- $ \hat{r}_{12} $: unit vector from charge 2 to charge 1

### Summary

Coulomb’s Law is the foundation of electrostatics, analogous to Newton’s law of gravitation, but for electric charges. It quantifies how charged particles interact in the absence of motion.
