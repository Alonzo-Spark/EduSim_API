### Work-Energy Theorem  
The **work-energy theorem** is a fundamental principle in classical mechanics that establishes a direct relationship between the work done by all forces acting on an object and its kinetic energy.  

---

#### Quick Definition  
**Work-Energy Theorem**:  
> *The net work \( W_{\text{net}} \) done on an object equals the change in its kinetic energy \( \Delta K \).*  

---

#### Formula  
<div class="formula-card">  
<h3>Work-Energy Theorem</h3>  
\[ W_{\text{net}} = \Delta K \]  
<p><strong>Where:</strong></p>  
<ul>  
  <li>\( W_{\text{net}} \) = Net work done on the object (Joules, J)</li>  
  <li>\( \Delta K \) = Change in kinetic energy = \( K_f - K_i \)</li>  
  <li>\( K = \frac{1}{2}mv^2 \) (Kinetic energy)</li>  
</ul>

</div>  

---

#### Derivation  
1. **Start with Newton’s 2nd Law**:  
   \[ \sum \vec{F} = m\vec{a} \]  
   *For constant force and motion in 1D (simplified):*  
   \[ F_{\text{net}} = ma \]  

2. **Relate acceleration to velocity**:  
   \[ a = \frac{dv}{dt} = \frac{dv}{dx} \cdot \frac{dx}{dt} = v \frac{dv}{dx} \]  
   *(Chain rule used: \( a = v \frac{dv}{dx} \))*.  

3. **Substitute into Newton’s 2nd Law**:  
   \[ F_{\text{net}} = m v \frac{dv}{dx} \]  

4. **Integrate both sides** over displacement (\( x_i \to x_f \)):  
   \[ \int_{x_i}^{x_f} F_{\text{net}}  dx = \int_{v_i}^{v_f} m v  dv \]  
   *Left side: Definition of work \( W_{\text{net}} \).*  
   *Right side: Integrate with respect to velocity.*  

5. **Evaluate the integral**:  
   \[ W_{\text{net}} = \left[ \frac{1}{2} m v^2 \right]_{v_i}^{v_f} = \frac{1}{2} m v_f^2 - \frac{1}{2} m v_i^2 \]  
   \[ \boxed{W_{\text{net}} = \Delta K} \]  

---

#### Key Insights  
- **Work-energy equivalence**: Forces acting over a distance (work) directly alter kinetic energy.  
- **Scalar quantities**: Work and kinetic energy are scalars (simpler than vector-based Newtonian analysis).  
- **Net work**: Only the *net* force contributes. Conservative forces (e.g., gravity) can also be analyzed via potential energy.  

---

#### Practical Applications  
1. **Calculating stopping distance**:  
   \[ W_{\text{friction}} = \Delta K \implies -\mu mg d = 0 - \frac{1}{2}mv_i^2 \implies d = \frac{v_i^2}{2\mu g} \]  
   *(Friction does negative work, reducing \( K \) to zero)*.  

2. **Projectile motion**:  
   Work done by gravity = \( \Delta K \), independent of path.  

---

#### Important Notes  
- **Applicability**: Valid for all forces (conservative/non-conservative) and all paths.  
- **Non-constant forces**: Use \( W_{\text{net}} = \int \vec{F}_{\text{net}} \cdot d\vec{x} \).  
- **System vs. object**: For multi-object systems, include internal work (rigid bodies exclude it).  

---

#### Summary  
The work-energy theorem simplifies motion analysis by linking force interactions (work) to energy transformations. It often bypasses complex kinematics, offering a powerful alternative to Newton’s laws.

#### Suggested Exercises  
1. A 5-kg object accelerates from 2 m/s to 6 m/s. Calculate \( W_{\text{net}} \).  
2. A car (1200 kg) brakes to stop from 30 m/s. If \( \mu_k = 0.6 \), find stopping distance.  
3. Derive the work-energy theorem for a variable force \( F(x) = kx \).
