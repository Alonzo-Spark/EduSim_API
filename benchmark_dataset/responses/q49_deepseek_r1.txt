To calculate the voltage across a resistor when given the current and resistance, we use **Ohm's Law**.

### Ohm's Law
Ohm's Law states that the voltage (\(V\)) across a resistor is the product of the current (\(I\)) flowing through it and its resistance (\(R\)):  
\[
V = I \cdot R
\]

### Given Values
- Current, \(I = 2  \text{A}\) (amperes)
- Resistance, \(R = 10  \Omega\) (ohms)

### Calculation
Substitute the values into the formula:  
\[
V = (2  \text{A}) \cdot (10  \Omega) = 20  \text{V}
\]

### Final Answer
The voltage across the resistor is \( \boxed{20}  \text{volts} \).

### Key Notes
- **Units**: Ensure current is in amperes (A) and resistance is in ohms (\(\Omega\)) for the result to be in volts (V).
- **Verification**: Doubling the current (to 4 A) or resistance (to 20 \(\Omega\)) would double the voltage, confirming the linear relationship in Ohm's Law.
