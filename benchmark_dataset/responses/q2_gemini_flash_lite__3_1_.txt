**Introduction**

This problem explores the concept of uniform acceleration and how it affects an object's velocity. We'll use a fundamental kinematic equation to solve it.

**Key Concepts**

*   **Acceleration (a):** The rate of change of velocity with respect to time. Measured in meters per second squared (m/s²).
*   **Initial Velocity (v₀):** The velocity of an object at the beginning of its motion.
*   **Final Velocity (v):** The velocity of an object at the end of its motion.
*   **Time (t):** The duration of the motion.

**Formula**

The key formula we'll use is:

┌───────────────────────────┐
│ Main Formula              │
│                           │
│  v = v₀ + at             │
│                           │

└───────────────────────────┘

**Explanation of Formula:**

This equation describes how an object's final velocity (v) is determined by its initial velocity (v₀), its acceleration (a), and the time (t) over which it accelerates.

*   `v`: Final velocity (what we want to find).
*   `v₀`: Initial velocity.
*   `a`: Acceleration.
*   `t`: Time.

**Solution**

Let's break down the problem step-by-step:

1.  **Identify the knowns:**

    *   Initial velocity (v₀) = 0 m/s (since the car starts from rest)
    *   Acceleration (a) = 2 m/s²
    *   Time (t) = 10 s

2.  **Apply the formula:**

    Using the formula: `v = v₀ + at`

    Substitute the known values:

    `v = 0 m/s + (2 m/s²)(10 s)`

3.  **Calculate the final velocity:**

    `v = 20 m/s`

**Answer**

The car's velocity after 10 seconds is 20 m/s.

**Summary**

We used the kinematic equation `v = v₀ + at` to calculate the final velocity of a car undergoing uniform acceleration. By understanding the relationship between initial velocity, acceleration, and time, we could solve this problem.
