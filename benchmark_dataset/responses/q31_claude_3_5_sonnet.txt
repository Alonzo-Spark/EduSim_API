<!-- CARD ROW: TWO QUICK DEFINITIONS -->
<div style="display: grid; grid-template-columns: 1fr 1fr; gap: 16px; margin-bottom: 24px;">

  <!-- Heat Capacity -->
  <div style="background: #fff8f0; border: 2px solid #ff6b35; border-radius: 14px; padding: 24px;">
    <div style="font-size: 11px; font-weight: 700; letter-spacing: 2px; text-transform: uppercase; color: #ff6b35; margin-bottom: 10px;">Definition 01</div>
    <div style="font-size: 18px; font-weight: 700; color: #1a1a2e; margin-bottom: 10px;">Heat Capacity <em>C</em></div>
    <div style="font-size: 14px; color: #444; line-height: 1.7;">
      The amount of heat energy required to raise the temperature of a <strong>given body</strong> (as a whole) by <strong>1°C (or 1 K)</strong>, without any change in its state.
    </div>
    <div style="margin-top: 14px; background: #fff0e6; border-radius: 8px; padding: 10px 14px; font-size: 13px; color: #cc4400;">
      🔑 It depends on the <strong>mass</strong> and <strong>material</strong> of the object.
    </div>
  </div>

  <!-- Specific Heat Capacity -->
  <div style="background: #f0f4ff; border: 2px solid #4361ee; border-radius: 14px; padding: 24px;">
    <div style="font-size: 11px; font-weight: 700; letter-spacing: 2px; text-transform: uppercase; color: #4361ee; margin-bottom: 10px;">Definition 02</div>
    <div style="font-size: 18px; font-weight: 700; color: #1a1a2e; margin-bottom: 10px;">Specific Heat Capacity <em>c</em></div>
    <div style="font-size: 14px; color: #444; line-height: 1.7;">
      The amount of heat energy required to raise the temperature of <strong>1 kg of a substance</strong> by <strong>1°C (or 1 K)</strong>, without any change in its state.
    </div>
    <div style="margin-top: 14px; background: #e8eeff; border-radius: 8px; padding: 10px 14px; font-size: 13px; color: #1a3a8f;">
      🔑 It is a <strong>property of the material</strong> — independent of mass.
    </div>
  </div>

</div>

  <div style="font-size: 11px; font-weight: 700; letter-spacing: 2px; text-transform: uppercase; color: #2dc653; margin-bottom: 14px;">🔗 Relationship Between C and c</div>
  <div style="background: white; border-radius: 10px; padding: 20px; text-align: center; margin-bottom: 16px; border: 1px solid #b7e4c7;">

$$C = mc$$

  </div>
  <div style="font-size: 14px; color: #444; line-height: 1.8;">
    Heat Capacity of a body = its <strong>mass × specific heat capacity</strong> of the material.<br>
    This means a larger mass of the same material has a <em>higher heat capacity</em>, but the same <em>specific heat capacity</em>.
  </div>

<!-- IMPORTANT NOTES -->
<div style="background: #fffbea; border: 2px solid #f7b731; border-radius: 14px; padding: 22px; margin-bottom: 24px;">
  <div style="font-size: 11px; font-weight: 700; letter-spacing: 2px; text-transform: uppercase; color: #c9900c; margin-bottom: 14px;">⚠️ Important Notes</div>
  <ul style="font-size: 14px; color: #444; line-height: 2; margin: 0; padding-left: 20px;">
    <li>Specific heat capacity is measured at <strong>constant pressure</strong> ($c_p$) or <strong>constant volume</strong> ($c_v$) — for solids/liquids, the difference is negligible.</li>
    <li>The formula $Q = mc\Delta T$ is valid only when there is <strong>no change of state</strong>.</li>
    <li>$\Delta T$ in Kelvin equals $\Delta T$ in Celsius — only differences matter, not absolute values.</li>
    <li>Water has the <strong>highest specific heat capacity</strong> among common liquids — a crucial fact for exams.</li>
  </ul>
