:::section{title="Introduction"}
In the study of electrostatics, the interaction between stationary electric charges is governed by a fundamental principle known as **Coulomb's Law**. Formulated by Charles-Augustin de Coulomb in 1785, it quantifies the force of attraction or repulsion between two point charges.
:::

:::section{title="Definition"}
**Coulomb's Law** states that the magnitude of the electrostatic force of attraction or repulsion between two point charges is:
1.  **Directly proportional** to the product of the magnitudes of the charges.
2.  **Inversely proportional** to the square of the distance between them.
3.  **Directed along the line** joining the centers of the two charges.
:::

:::formula{title="Main Formula: Coulomb's Law"}
$$F = k \frac{|q_1 q_2|}{r^2}$$

**Variable Breakdown:**
*   $F$: Electrostatic force (Newtons, $N$)
*   $q_1, q_2$: Magnitudes of the two point charges (Coulombs, $C$)
*   $r$: Distance between the centers of the charges (Meters, $m$)
*   $k$: Coulomb's constant ($\approx 8.987 \times 10^9 \, N \cdot m^2/C^2$)

:::

:::section{title="Key Concepts"}
*   **Point Charges:** The law strictly applies to "point charges"—charged objects where the size is negligible compared to the distance between them.
*   **Permittivity of Free Space:** The constant $k$ is often written as $\frac{1}{4\pi\epsilon_0}$, where $\epsilon_0$ is the permittivity of a vacuum.
*   **Vector Nature:** Force is a vector. Like charges repel (+ force direction), and opposite charges attract (- force direction).
:::

:::section{title="Characteristics of the Force"}
| Feature | Description |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Medium Dependence** | The force decreases when charges are placed in a dielectric medium (like water or oil). |
| **Inverse Square Law** | If the distance $r$ is doubled, the force $F$ becomes one-fourth ($1/4$) of its original value. |
| **Central Force** | The force acts along the line connecting the two charges. |
| **Newton's Third Law** | The force exerted by $q_1$ on $q_2$ is equal and opposite to the force exerted by $q_2$ on $q_1$. |
:::

:::section{title="Important Notes"}
> [!IMPORTANT]
> Coulomb's Law is only strictly valid for charges at rest (electrostatics). If charges are moving at high speeds, magnetic forces and relativistic effects must be considered.
:::

:::section{title="Suggested Questions"}
1. **Conceptual:** How does the force change if the distance between two charges is halved?
2. **Calculation:** Calculate the force between two $1C$ charges separated by $1m$ in a vacuum.
3. **Comparison:** Compare Coulomb's Law with Newton's Law of Universal Gravitation. What are the similarities and differences?
:::
