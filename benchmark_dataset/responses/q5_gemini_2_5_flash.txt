## Analyzing Train Retardation

Let's break down this problem to understand how to calculate the train's retardation.

### Quick Definition

Retardation, also known as deceleration, is the rate at which an object slows down. It's essentially negative acceleration.

### Given Values

Here's what we know from the problem statement:

*   **Initial Velocity ($u$)**: The train's speed before applying brakes.
    *   $u = 20 \text{ m/s}$
*   **Final Velocity ($v$)**: The train's speed after stopping.
    *   $v = 0 \text{ m/s}$ (since the train stops)
*   **Time Taken ($t$)**: The duration over which the train slows down.
    *   $t = 10 \text{ s}$

### Main Formula

To calculate the retardation (which is acceleration in the opposite direction of motion), we can use the first equation of motion:

$$
v = u + at
$$

Where:
*   $v$ = final velocity
*   $u$ = initial velocity
*   $a$ = acceleration
*   $t$ = time

We need to rearrange this formula to solve for $a$:

$$
a = \frac{v - u}{t}
$$

### Calculation

Now, let's substitute the given values into the formula:

1.  **Substitute values**:
    $a = \frac{0 \text{ m/s} - 20 \text{ m/s}}{10 \text{ s}}$

2.  **Perform subtraction**:
    $a = \frac{-20 \text{ m/s}}{10 \text{ s}}$

3.  **Perform division**:
    $a = -2 \text{ m/s}^2$

### Interpretation

The calculated acceleration is $-2 \text{ m/s}^2$. The negative sign indicates that the acceleration is in the opposite direction of the initial motion, meaning the train is slowing down.

### Conclusion

Therefore, the retardation of the train is $2 \text{ m/s}^2$.

### Important Notes

*   **Units**: Always pay attention to units. In this case, velocity is in meters per second (m/s) and time is in seconds (s), so acceleration is in meters per second squared (m/s$^2$).
*   **Retardation vs. Acceleration**: Retardation is simply the magnitude of negative acceleration. If the acceleration is $-X \text{ m/s}^2$, the retardation is $X \text{ m/s}^2$.
