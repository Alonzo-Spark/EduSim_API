### Surface Tension: Definition and Factors

#### 🧪 Quick Definition  
**Surface tension** is the elastic tendency of a liquid surface to minimize its area, caused by cohesive forces between molecules at the interface. It acts like an invisible "skin" that resists external forces.  
**Units**: Newtons per meter (N/m) or joules per square meter (J/m²).

---

#### 🔍 Key Characteristics  
- Arises from **imbalanced cohesive forces** at the liquid-air interface.  
- Explains phenomena like water droplets, capillary action, and insect locomotion on water.  
- Higher in polar liquids (e.g., water) than non-polar liquids (e.g., ethanol).

---

#### 📉 Factors Affecting Surface Temperature  

| Factor                  | Effect on Surface Tension | Reason                                                                 |
|-------------------------|---------------------------|------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| **Temperature**         | ↓ Decreases               | Higher kinetic energy weakens intermolecular forces.                  |
| **Surfactants** (e.g., soap) | ↓ Decreases               | Disrupt hydrogen bonding; accumulate at the interface.                |
| **Solute Type**         |                           |                                                                        |
| &nbsp;&nbsp;- Ionic (e.g., NaCl) | ↑ Slightly increases      | Strengthens water structure via ion-dipole interactions.              |
| &nbsp;&nbsp;- Organic (e.g., phenol) | ↓ Decreases               | Disrupts hydrogen bonding networks.                                   |
| **pH**                  | Varies                    | Alters molecular charge (e.g., low pH reduces tension in fatty acids). |
| **Liquid Composition**  | ↓ Non-polar < Polar       | Weaker cohesive forces in non-polar liquids (e.g., ethanol: 22 mN/m vs. water: 72 mN/m). |

---

#### ⚖️ Formula  
The fundamental equation for surface tension (\(\gamma\)):  

$$
\gamma = \frac{F}{L}
$$  

Where:  
- \(F\) = Force acting perpendicular to the surface (N)  
- \(L\) = Length over which the force acts (m)  

**Example**: A 0.05 N force on a 0.1 m wire gives \(\gamma = \frac{0.05}{0.1} = 0.5 \ \text{N/m}\).

---

#### 💡 Important Notes  
1. **Critical Temperature**: Surface tension → 0 as temperature approaches critical point.  
2. **Dynamic Measurement**: Affected by surface age due to molecular rearrangement.  
3. **Anomaly in Water**: High surface tension (72.8 mN/m at 20°C) enables capillary rise in plants.
