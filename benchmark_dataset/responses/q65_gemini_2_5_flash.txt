### **Characteristics: Stable Orbit**

A `stable orbit` implies certain conditions are met, ensuring the object will continue its trajectory without immediately escaping or crashing.

<div style="background-color: #fffacd; border-left: 4px solid #ffd700; padding: 10px; margin-bottom: 15px;">
*   **Closed Trajectory:** The object follows a repeatable path, returning to its starting point relative to the central body.
*   **Positive Energy:** The total mechanical energy (kinetic + potential) of the orbiting body is negative for elliptical/circular orbits. (A common misconception is positive; positive energy implies hyperbolic trajectory and escape).
*   **Within Hill Sphere:** The object remains gravitationally bound to the central body, staying within its sphere of influence.
*   **Perturbation Resistance:** While real orbits are always perturbed, a 'stable' orbit in simulation implies it's resistant to minor numerical errors or expected small influences.
</div>
