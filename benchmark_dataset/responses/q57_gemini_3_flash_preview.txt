### Interactive Lesson: Forced Harmonic Oscillators

:::section{title="Introduction"}
In a forced harmonic oscillator, an external periodic force $F(t) = F_0 \cos(\omega t)$ is applied to a system. Unlike free oscillation, the system eventually settles into a **Steady State** where it vibrates at the frequency of the driving force, not its own natural frequency.
:::

:::section{title="The Formula Lab"}
$$
A(\omega) = \frac{F_0/m}{\sqrt{(\omega_0^2 - \omega^2)^2 + (2\gamma\omega)^2}}
$$

**Variables:**
*   $A$: Amplitude of oscillation
*   $\omega$: Driving frequency (The "Input")
*   $\omega_0$: Natural frequency of the system
*   $\gamma$: Damping coefficient (friction/resistance)

:::

:::section{title="Visual Effects of Increasing Frequency"}
As you turn the frequency dial from zero to high frequencies, the system passes through three distinct visual phases:

| Phase | Frequency Range | Visual Behavior |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **1. Low Frequency** | $\omega \ll \omega_0$ | The mass follows the driver almost perfectly. Motion is slow and in-phase. |
| **2. Resonance** | $\omega \approx \omega_0$ | **Maximum Displacement.** The oscillations become violent and reach peak amplitude. |
| **3. High Frequency** | $\omega \gg \omega_0$ | The mass barely moves. It cannot keep up with the rapid changes of the driver. |
:::

:::section{title="Key Concept: Phase Shift"}
Increasing the frequency doesn't just change the size of the movement; it changes the **timing**.

*   **At low frequencies:** The mass moves *with* the pusher (In phase).
*   **At resonance:** The mass lags by $90^\circ$ ($\pi/2$).
*   **At high frequencies:** The mass moves *opposite* to the pusher (Out of phase).
:::

:::section{title="Summary Card"}
**The "Inertia" Effect:**
As $\omega$ increases beyond resonance, the visual amplitude drops toward zero. This is because the system's mass has **inertia**; it takes time to accelerate. If the force flips direction too fast, the mass simply stutters in place, unable to complete a full swing.
:::

:::
