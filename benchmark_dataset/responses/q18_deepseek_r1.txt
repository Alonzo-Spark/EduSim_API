To calculate the potential energy of a body lifted to a height, we use the **gravitational potential energy formula**:

### Formula Card
```plaintext
┌──────────────────────────────────┐
│ Potential Energy Formula         │
│                                  │
│           PE = mgh               │
│                                  │
│ Where:                           │
│   PE = Potential energy (Joules) │
│   m  = Mass (kg)                 │
│   g  = Acceleration due to gravity (9.8 m/s²) │
│   h  = Height (m)                │
└──────────────────────────────────┘
```

### Given Values:
- Mass (\(m\)) = 5 kg  
- Height (\(h\)) = 10 m  
- Acceleration due to gravity (\(g\)) = 9.8 m/s² (standard value on Earth)  

### Calculation:
Substitute the values into the formula:  
\[
PE = m \cdot g \cdot h = 5 \, \text{kg} \times 9.8 \, \text{m/s}^2 \times 10 \, \text{m}
\]

**Step-by-step:**  
1. Multiply mass and gravity:  
   \(5 \times 9.8 = 49\)  
2. Multiply by height:  
   \(49 \times 10 = 490\)  

### Final Answer:
\[
\boxed{490 \ \text{J}} \quad \text{(Joules)}
\]

### Key Notes:
1. **Units**: Ensure all inputs are in SI units (kg, m, m/s²) for Joules (J).  
2. **Gravity**: If \(g = 10 \, \text{m/s}^2\) is used (simplified), the result would be \(500 \, \text{J}\).  
3. **Significance**: This energy represents the work done to lift the mass against gravity.
