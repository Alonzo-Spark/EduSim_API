### 🌌 Orbital Mechanics Lab: Velocity Reduction

When a planet's tangential velocity $v_t$ is reduced below the required circular orbital speed $v_c$, the balance between **inertia** and **gravitational pull** is disrupted. The system transitions from a circular path to an elliptical decay or atmospheric reentry.

---

### 📘 Quick Definition
**Orbital Decay / Elliptical Transition** occurs when the kinetic energy of an orbiting body is no longer sufficient to maintain a constant radius. Gravity "wins" the instantaneous tug-of-war, pulling the object into a lower, faster, and more eccentric path.

---

### 🗝️ Key Concepts

*   **Circular Velocity ($v_c$):** The precise speed where centripetal acceleration exactly matches gravitational acceleration.
*   **Perigee/Periapsis:** The new "closest point" created immediately at the location where the velocity was reduced.
*   **Conservation of Angular Momentum:** As the planet falls closer to the star, it must spin faster to conserve momentum.
*   **Potential vs. Kinetic Energy:** Potential energy is traded for kinetic energy as the planet "falls" into the gravity well.

### ⚙️ Characteristics of Reduced Velocity

| Property | Change Direction | Scientific Explanation |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Orbit Shape** | Circular $\rightarrow$ Elliptical | The path becomes an ellipse with the point of deceleration as the **Aphelion**. |
| **Total Energy** | Decrease | Lower velocity means lower Kinetic Energy ($K$), reducing Total Mechanical Energy. |
| **Orbital Period** | Decrease | According to Kepler’s 3rd Law, a smaller semi-major axis leads to a shorter year. |
| **Velocity at Perihelion** | Increase | The planet accelerates as it falls inward, eventually exceeding its original speed. |

### ⚠️ Important Notes

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **The Paradox of Falling:** When you slow a planet down, it eventually ends up moving **faster** than it was before. This is because it drops into a deeper part of the gravity well where the gravitational potential energy is converted into high kinetic energy at the perihelion.

---

### 🏁 Summary
1.  **Immediate Effect:** The planet begins to "fall" toward the star.
2.  **Path Change:** The circular orbit collapses into an elliptical orbit.
3.  **New Extremes:** The point where you slowed down becomes the **highest point** (Apoapsis) of the new orbit.
4.  **Final Result:** If the velocity is reduced significantly (below the "impact" threshold), the ellipse will intersect the star's surface or atmosphere.
