**Explanation and Derivation**

The kinetic energy formula is derived from the work-energy theorem.  Work (W) done on an object is equal to the change in its kinetic energy.  Work is defined as Force (F) multiplied by distance (d):  `W = F * d`.

Force is related to mass (m) and acceleration (a) by Newton's second law: `F = ma`.

Therefore, `W = ma * d`.  Substituting this into the work-energy theorem, we get:

`W = ΔKE`
`ma * d = KE₂ - KE₁`

If the object starts from rest (KE₁ = 0), then:

`ma * d = KE₂`

Since `KE₂ = 1/2 * m * v²`, we have:

`ma * d = 1/2 * m * v²`

Dividing both sides by `m`, we obtain:

`a * d = 1/2 * v²`

We can also express acceleration as the rate of change of velocity: `a = Δv / Δt`. Substituting this into the equation:

`(Δv / Δt) * d = 1/2 * v²`

This final form is often simplified to the standard kinetic energy equation:

`KE = 1/2 * m * v²`

Where:

*   `KE` is kinetic energy
*   `m` is mass
*   `v` is velocity
