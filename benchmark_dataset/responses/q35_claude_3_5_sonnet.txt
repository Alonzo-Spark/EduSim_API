## 📘 Quick Definition

> **Latent Heat** is the heat energy absorbed or released by a substance during a change of state, *without any change in temperature*.

The word **"latent"** comes from Latin meaning **hidden** — because the energy is hidden inside the substance, not visible as a temperature rise.

---

## 🔄 What is Change of State?

Matter exists in **three states**. When energy is supplied or removed, matter transitions between them:

| Transition | Process | Energy |
|---|---|---|
| Solid → Liquid | **Melting** | Absorbed |
| Liquid → Solid | **Freezing** | Released |
| Liquid → Gas | **Vaporisation** | Absorbed |
| Gas → Liquid | **Condensation** | Released |
| Solid → Gas | **Sublimation** | Absorbed |
| Gas → Solid | **Deposition** | Released |

---

## ⚗️ Why Does Temperature Stay Constant?

During a change of state, supplied energy is used to **break intermolecular bonds** — not to increase kinetic energy of particles.

Since **temperature measures average kinetic energy**, and kinetic energy isn't changing during the state transition, **temperature stays flat**.

This is why a **heating curve** shows flat plateaus at melting and boiling points.

## 📐 Formula Card — Latent Heat

$$
Q = mL
$$

| Symbol | Meaning | SI Unit |
|---|---|---|
| $Q$ | Heat energy absorbed or released | Joules (J) |
| $m$ | Mass of the substance | Kilograms (kg) |
| $L$ | Specific Latent Heat | J kg⁻¹ |

---

## 🔬 Two Types of Latent Heat

### 1. Specific Latent Heat of Fusion ($L_f$)
Heat needed to convert **1 kg of solid to liquid** at constant temperature.

$$
L_f \text{ of water} = 3.34 \times 10^5 \ \text{J kg}^{-1}
$$

---

### 2. Specific Latent Heat of Vaporisation ($L_v$)
Heat needed to convert **1 kg of liquid to gas** at constant temperature.

$$
L_v \text{ of water} = 2.26 \times 10^6 \ \text{J kg}^{-1}
$$

> 💡 Notice $L_v \gg L_f$ — it takes **much more energy** to vaporise than to melt, because all intermolecular bonds must be fully broken during vaporisation.

## 📈 Heating Curve

```
Temp
 |                              ___________
 |                    _________/
 |          _________/
 |_________/
 |
 └──────────────────────────────────────── Heat supplied
   ^        ^         ^        ^
  Solid  Melting   Liquid  Boiling
        plateau           plateau
```

The **flat sections** are where latent heat is being absorbed — state is changing, temperature is not.

## 💡 Real-World Applications

| Application | Explanation |
|---|---|
| **Sweating** | Sweat evaporates, absorbing latent heat from skin, cooling the body |
| **Ice packs** | Ice melts absorbing heat, keeping injuries cold |
| **Steam burns** | Steam releases large $L_v$ on skin — more dangerous than boiling water |
| **Refrigerators** | Refrigerant liquid evaporates inside, absorbing heat from food |

---

## ✅ Key Takeaways

- Latent heat = energy for **bond breaking/forming**, not temperature change
- Formula: $Q = mL$
- Two types: **Fusion** (melting) and **Vaporisation** (boiling)
- $L_v > L_f$ always, because vaporisation requires complete separation of molecules
- Temperature is **constant** during any change of state
