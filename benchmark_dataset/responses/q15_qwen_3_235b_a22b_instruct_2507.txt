### How This Applies to a Firing Gun

Let’s break it down step by step:

1. **Action Force**:  
   The gunpowder explosion inside the gun creates high-pressure gases that push the **bullet forward**. This is the **action force** — the gun exerts a force on the bullet.

2. **Reaction Force**:  
   According to Newton’s third law, the **bullet exerts an equal and opposite force** on the gun — pushing it **backward**.

3. **Result**:  
   The bullet, being small, accelerates rapidly forward. The gun, being much more massive, still moves backward but with much smaller acceleration. This backward motion is what we observe as **recoil**.

### Why Doesn’t the Gun Fly Back Like the Bullet?

Even though the **forces are equal**, the **accelerations are not** — because acceleration depends on mass:

$$
a = \frac{F}{m}
$$

- The **bullet** has a small mass → large acceleration → shoots out fast.
- The **gun** has a larger mass → smaller acceleration → noticeable kick, but not extreme motion.

This is governed by **Newton’s Second Law**, working together with the Third.

⚠️ **Important Note**:  
The **action and reaction forces act on different objects**:
- Force on bullet → acts on **bullet** → makes it accelerate forward.
- Reaction force → acts on **gun** → makes it accelerate backward.

They **do not cancel each other** because they are applied to different bodies.

---

✅ **Summary**  
- Newton’s Third Law explains recoil: every action has an equal and opposite reaction.  
- The gun pushes the bullet forward; the bullet pushes the gun backward.  
- Forces are equal in magnitude, opposite in direction.  
- Due to mass differences, the bullet accelerates much more than the gun.  
- Recoil is a real-world demonstration of momentum conservation and force pairs.
